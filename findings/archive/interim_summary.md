## 1. Literature: what is established and what is not

- **Ice cores.** The ice cores show a bipolar sulfate signal of about 19 Tg S (eVolv2k), which is roughly 60% of Tambora in Greenland and 45% in Antarctica. Δ33S anomalies show the sulfur reached the stratosphere (Cole-Dai et al. 2009). Deposition timing gives **Feb 1809 ±4 months** (Cole-Dai 2010), so roughly Oct 1808 to Jun 1809, and some age scales add ±1 yr (TUNU2013 dates it 1810±1).
- **4 Dec 1808 ±7 d is not an independent date.** Guevara-Murua et al. (2014) set it by judgement. It rests on the first *reported* sighting in Bogotá (11 Dec) less an analogue spread time taken from Pinatubo, El Chichón and Krakatau. It constrains only "before about 11 Dec", and the earlier bound is open. Timmreck et al. (2021) state there is "no direct link" between the South American observations and the polar sulfate.
- **One eruption or several.** Several lines of evidence point to more than one eruption:
  - Antarctic 1809 tephra is andesitic.
  - Yukon (Eclipse Icefield) tephra is dacitic (Yalcin et al. 2006).
  - Some Dome C / South Pole shards resemble Indonesian tephra.
  - Greenland (TUNU2013) shards could not be analysed (Plunkett et al. 2023).
  - A conference abstract (Hutchison et al. 2023; not peer reviewed) reports a "time-evolving" Δ33S pattern "suggestive of multiple eruptions".

  The current balance is one tropical stratospheric eruption plus at least one extratropical Northern Hemisphere eruption close in time. Climate-forcing datasets still model a single event by convention.
- **CMIP7 SO2 inventory (Aubry et al., egusphere-2026-546).** This is still a **preprint**: discussion is closed and author replies were posted on 30 Jul 2026, but no final paper exists as of today. It carries 1809 as a single eVolv2k-derived event with the default January date and names 1809 as a priority target for tephra and isotope work. The fetched text gives "7.1 Tg SO2" for 1809. That figure conflicts with eVolv2k's 19.3 Tg S and needs checking against the dataset.
- **Prior archival search.** The only targeted search is Guevara-Murua et al.'s, covering Spanish sources in Colombia and Peru, with no published search log. I found no published systematic search of Portuguese, Brazilian, Philippine, Dutch East Indies, Pacific or naval-log material for 1808–09.

## 2. Reconstructing the two South American witnesses

**Caldas, Bogotá**
- The piece is "Noticia meteorológica" in *Semanario del Nuevo Reyno de Granada*, Año 2, **No. 5, Sunday 5 Feb 1809**. The date is inferred from the weekly sequence: No. 1 is 8 Jan and No. 6 is 12 Feb.
- Guevara-Murua et al. used the 1912 Posada reprint (pp. 347–350), not the 1809 printing. The original's page numbers are not yet verified. Scans exist at the John Carter Brown Library copy on archive.org, Banco de la República and the Biblioteca Nacional de Colombia, but none could be read from here.
- The text gives onset "desde el día 11 de diciembre del año último". It describes a silver sun mistaken for the moon, a whitened sky, a missing corona, dimmed stars, frosts, dry weather, and southerly winds alternating with calms.
- It names Pasto, Popayán, Neiva, Santa Marta and Tunja, but **gives no informants and no dates for them**.
- The Popayán rain-gauge observers (Antonio Arboleda and Santiago Pérez de Arroyo) are plausible sources, but that is inference. Their published tables cover rainfall only.
- Caldas's daily registers for this period are lost.

**The "Roulin 1832" account**
- It derives from a communication Roulin made to the Académie des sciences in **August 1831**, prompted by the "white sun" seen over Europe that month. It was reported in the *Revue encyclopédique* 1831; the English *New Monthly Magazine* 1832 version is a later echo.
- Its content follows Caldas, so it is **not independent**. Two details may be additions: an end date ("end of January 1809") and "within the memory of man". The French text has not yet been read.
- Boussingault's *Mémoires* (1900) also derives from Caldas and misprints the year as 1809.
- The Bogotá diarist J. M. Caballero has **no entries for December 1808**. He records two minor earthquakes on the nights of 7 and 15 Jan 1809.

**Unanue, Lima**
- Located in the 2nd ed. (Madrid: Sancha 1815), **Sección I, pp. 13–14, footnote 3**, in the Wellcome Collection copy (b21945469). The footnote is absent from the 1806 1st edition.
- Three corrections to Guevara-Murua et al.:
  1. The original reads "A mediados de él", following a sentence about coughs lasting "hasta el mes de Diciembre". The year 1808 is fixed only by that narrative chain, which begins with 30 Apr 1808.
  2. Chorrillos is the *direction* of the glow as seen from Lima (to the SW, "entre el cerro de los Chorrillos y el mar"), not the observer's location.
  3. The same footnote records a violent south wind on **30 Apr 1808** that scattered "un polvillo colorado hediondo", a reddish, foul-smelling dust, followed by coughs and catarrhs until December. This is a separate local event that the paper omits.
- **Alternatives:** the glow is described as a cone rising to the zenith after sunset and lasting until about 20:00. That is longer than an ordinary afterglow and also fits the zodiacal light, which becomes visible when Lima's winter overcast clears in Dec–Jan. Two points argue against zodiacal light: it was seen "every night" through moonlit weeks, and Unanue treats it as unusual. **Unanue is independent of Caldas, but his dating is coarse (mid-Dec ± about a week).**

**Timing and the ice**
- **Bipolar sulfate lag** [calc from published tables, B55]. Greenland minus Antarctic onset lag for 1809 is about −0.05 yr (range −0.4 to +0.3) across four cores, so deposition was synchronous and the source tropical. The lag cannot say north or south. About 55% of the deposited sulfate fell in the north (NH/SH ratio relative to Tambora 1.4, range 0.9-2.2). A Tambora-analogue eruption date of 1809.07 ± 0.41 is Claude's calculation, not a published date. (F29)
- **The latitude band is withdrawn** [calc, B62 then B66]. The band of about 12°S-3°N proposed on 28 Oct from the Marshall et al. 2021 deposition emulator did not survive validation: against eleven tropical eruptions of known latitude and month the emulator's predicted Greenland/Antarctic ratio has an rms error of ×3.0 and no correlation with the observed ratios. The observed ratios show a season effect (Oct-Feb eruptions about twice as Greenland-heavy as Mar-Jun ones), not a latitude effect, inside ±17°. The deposition ratio therefore says only "tropical", and the argument against Cabalían (10°N) made on 28 Oct is withdrawn. (F30)
