# Why the 1808/09 eruption is so hard to identify

Compiled 16 Oct 2026 for William. Updated 26 Oct 2026: bullets marked (26 Oct) add that day's results (register B43-B51). Updated 4 Nov 2026: bullets marked (Nov) add the results of 27 Oct-2 Nov (register B54-B74). Each reason says where it comes from. **[Lit]** = argued in published work. **[Project]** = this project's own inference from its searches; not a published claim.

## 1. The ice says "large and tropical", but not where
- **[Lit]** Sulfate falls at both poles at about the same time, and the sulfur isotopes show it reached the stratosphere. That points to a tropical eruption of roughly 13–19 Tg S, about twice Pinatubo (Dai et al. 1991; Cole-Dai et al. 2009; Toohey & Sigl 2017; Timmreck et al. 2021). Ice cores date deposition, not the eruption: Feb 1809 ± 4 months (Cole-Dai 2010), with ±1 yr in some cores (Plunkett et al. 2023 date TUNU to 1810 ± 1).
- **[Lit]** Models place the eruption at the Equator on 1 January by convention, because neither location nor season is known (Timmreck et al. 2021; Aubry et al. CMIP7 preprint 2026).
- **[Project] (Nov)** The ice cannot be pushed further. Greenland and Antarctic onsets are synchronous within about a month (Claude's calculation from four published cores), which says "tropical" but not north or south. We tried to read a latitude from the hemispheric sulfate split with a published deposition emulator; tested against eleven tropical eruptions of known position, the emulator's predictions had no skill, and the observed splits depend on the season of the eruption more than on its latitude. So the deposition ratio gives no latitude band, and we withdrew the one we had proposed.
- **[Lit] (Nov)** Published dates still disagree: Chenoweth (2001) put the eruption in March-June 1808 from marine air temperatures, and a 2026 tree-ring preprint (Wilson et al.) dates it to 1808, against the conventional early 1809.

## 2. Possibly not one eruption
- **[Lit]** The tephra in the ice does not match from site to site:
  - andesitic shards in Antarctica and dacitic shards in the Yukon (Yalcin et al. 2006);
  - "Indonesian-like" shards at Dome C and the South Pole;
  - shards in Greenland too small to analyse (Plunkett et al. 2023).
- **[Lit]** The time-evolving sulfur-isotope signal suggests "multiple eruptions" (Hutchison et al. 2023, a workshop abstract only, not peer reviewed).
- **[Lit]** Plunkett et al. 2023 conclude that a tropical plus an extratropical eruption in quick succession may have contributed. If the signal is a composite, there may be no single big volcano to find.
- **[Project]** The sulfur-isotope profile at Dome C (Gautier et al. 2019 data) runs from about 1809.7 to 1813.7, which would fit more than one injection. This is our reading of the data, not the authors' claim.
- **[Project] (26 Oct)** A per-core map of 1809/Tambora sulfate across 15 Antarctic cores is even (mean 0.48, Claude's calculation) except at Talos Dome (1.05-1.11), 245 km from Mt Melbourne and the Pleiades. That fits a small local Victoria Land eruption c. 1809-10, which would also explain the Siple Dome 1810 trachyte. So at least one of the ice signals near 1809 is local noise, which makes the tephra record harder to read (inference; input values page-image checked).

## 3. Little tephra, no field deposit
- **[Lit]** No proximal deposit has been dated to 1808–09 anywhere. The shards found are few and small (Plunkett et al. 2023).
- **[Lit]** Aubry et al. (CMIP7 preprint) name 1809 as a priority target for tephra and sulfur-isotope work.
- **[Project] (26 Oct)** The Antarctic 1809 glass is a common medium-K island-arc andesite. Scored against 8,740 GEOROC analyses and 248 whole-rock analyses (Claude's calculation), many unrelated arc glasses and rocks match it about equally well (scores 0.90-0.95: Dakataua's Makalia glass, Pleistocene SW Luzon and Vanuatu marine glasses, Dukono, Ternate, N Sulawesi, Gaua, Awu). Major elements can rule out low-K arcs (Tonga, the New Britain arc front, Witori, the northern Banda group) but cannot pick a source. Trace elements on the ice shards have not been published, and no proximal tephra dated to c. 1808 is known.
- **[Project] (Nov)** The match is not even specific inside one arc: Tanna, Ambrym and Kuwae in Vanuatu match the ice glass as well as Gaua does (Claude's calculation). We have written down in advance which trace-element ratios would separate the main candidates (Zr/Nb, La/Nb), so that the first shard analysis can be read without hindsight.
- **[Lit/Project] (Nov)** No trace-element analysis of any 1805-1812 ice glass has been published in either hemisphere (literature search, text level). New samples exist (a South Pole 1809 cryptotephra; an unread Antarctic tephra dataset), but the shards are tiny.
- **[Project] (Nov)** The obvious geological archives are missing. Of 58 sediment, coral and cave archives within 300 km of the candidates, none has a tephra dated to c. 1809, and no marine core there has an age model fine enough for the last few centuries. Only Kau Bay (Halmahera) has existing cores that could resolve 1809.
- **[Lit] (26 Oct)** The Japanese H15 core (East Antarctica) has unanalysed "fine ash-like materials (~10 µm)" in its 1809 layer (Kohno et al. 1996, page image): another possible sample that nobody has analysed.
- **[Lit/Project]** A sulfur-rich but tephra-poor eruption would leave a weak geological trace. That is a general volcanological point; no paper we read argues it specifically for 1809.

## 4. A source far from the surviving written record
- **[Lit]** Guevara-Murua et al. 2014 argue that the source is "unlikely to have been in Latin America", because a large eruption there would be expected in the colonial archives and there is no anomaly in the Quelccaya ice core.
- **[Lit]** By implication, the source was somewhere whose witnesses left no account that has reached the archives used so far: the open Pacific, island Southeast Asia or Melanesia, or a submarine eruption. People lived near most of the candidates, but their histories were mostly kept orally, and the colonial written record there is thin. The literature names no candidate.
- **[Project]** Our searches support "no conspicuous local eruption" in each of these regions, but only for the records we could reach:
  - the Caribbean, Mexico, Central America and the Andes;
  - China, Korea and Japan;
  - the Dutch East Indies papers;
  - the European, US and Iberian press.

  The searches cannot exclude Melanesia, the central Pacific, the Indian Ocean or the Southern Ocean, where few written records from 1808 survive and local knowledge was kept in oral traditions that we have searched only in part (see `strands/melanesia_oral/`).
- **[Project] (26 Oct)** We have now also read the local and colonial records for the Moluccas, Sulawesi, Mindanao, New Britain, the Banda arc and Halmahera (Dutch edicts and chronicles, Horsburgh's ship remarks, the 1810 Batavia paper, Verbeek, Bleeker, Wichmann's catalogue). None records an eruption, ash, darkness or booms between Nov 1808 and mid-1810. This is a weak negative: Amboina's 1810-17 archives are lost, the Batavia paper starts only in Jan 1810, and there were no missionaries in the zone. Banda Api, the one volcano with a resident European population, was quiet (Verbeek: 1778, then 1816). The only regional signal is a severe drought and rice failure around Batavia in 1809 (page image), which is not diagnostic.
- **[Project] (Nov)** Further negatives, all limited to the records named: we found no report of an eruption, ash, darkness or odd sky in the Batavia government registers and newspaper of 1808-11, in three Bohol parish burial books of 1806-1811 (150-180 km from Cabalían; no excess burials after Nov 1808), on the *Ramoncita*'s charts through the Surigao Strait in Jan-Feb 1809, in the Deshima register at Nagasaki for Nov 1808-Apr 1809, in four American whaling and merchant logs, in the Tonga, Tahiti and Hawaiʻi accounts, or in the Vietnamese court chronicle for 1808-10. The Leyte parish books that would have tested Cabalían directly do not survive on film for before 1861.

## 5. Wartime and colonial record gaps
- **[Project]** Our inference from what we could and could not reach:
  - **Europe:** 1808–09 is the Peninsular War, and Spanish and Portuguese printing was disrupted or censored.
  - **The Americas:** the Spanish American juntas (Quito Aug 1809) were forming.
  - **Java:** the British blockade cut it off and its Dutch records are thin.
  - **The Indian Ocean:** Mauritius and Réunion were under blockade.
  - **At sea:** Royal Navy logs survive but are not digitized.
- **[Project] (Nov)** The fleet itself was elsewhere. From the Navy List of 1 Jan 1809 and the Admiralty catalogue, no Royal Navy ship was east of the Straits of Malacca and south of China in Nov 1808-Mar 1809, and none was in the Pacific; the nearest, *Psyche* and *Doris*, reached the Philippines only in Apr-Oct 1809. The Dutch navy kept no journal for these months, and the CLIWOC log database has no record in the Celebes-Moluccas box for 1808-09. So even the undigitized logs may not include a ship in the right sea at the right time.

  We know of no paper that makes this argument for 1809; it is our explanation for why the searchable record is thin.
- **[Project]** Caldas's own daily weather registers for after July 1808 are lost (Guevara-Murua et al. 2014 note the loss). So the best-placed observer's raw data for the critical months is missing.

## 6. Only one firm, dated witness
- **[Lit]** The case rests on Caldas (Bogotá, from 11 Dec 1808) and Unanue (Lima, mid-Dec 1808 to mid-Feb 1809) (Guevara-Murua et al. 2014).
- **[Lit]** Timmreck et al. 2021 stress that there is "no direct link" between these sightings and the ice-core sulfate.
- **[Project]** The "4 Dec 1808 ± 7 days" date is a judgement from transport analogues, not a computed bound; the sightings constrain only "before about 11 Dec".
- **[Project]** Since then we have added:
  - Pérez Valencia in Popayán (Dec 1808 – Jan 1809, recalled in 1815; OCR only);
  - red twilights at Edo, 9 to about 25 Jan 1809 (image-verified);
  - a vermilion sun at Tottori, 5 Feb 1809 (OCR only).

  These support a widespread veil but say nothing about its source.

## 7. A thin veil is easy to overlook or explain away
- **[Project]** A stratospheric veil of this size produces a whitish sky, a silvery sun and vivid twilights. These are not dramatic, and most writers would put them down to weather.
  - Bogotá's dry season and Lima's summer clearing both begin in December, so the start of the sightings may partly reflect when the sky became visible.
  - Japanese winter twilights are often vivid anyway.
  - Several chronicles (the Chinese Astronomical Bureau, Korea after 1799) had nearly stopped recording sun colour.
- **[Project] (Nov)** The aftermath shows more clearly than the event. Corals at Lombok and Palau turn cool/dry in December 1808, and Java, South China and the Red River delta all had drought and famine in 1809 (page image and coded sources). A writer in 1809 would record a failed harvest, not a thin veil, and none of these records names a cause in the sky. The Everest ice core, which records Tambora, shows nothing for 1809-10.
- **[Project] (26 Oct)** Japanese-language scholarship has never linked the Edo and Tottori red skies to a volcano; the only compiler (Musha 1943) printed them as earthquake omens. The Royal Society's 1888 list of historical coloured twilights has nothing between May 1808 and late March 1809, and no lunar-eclipse colour report was found for 1808-09.
- **[Project]** Caldas's own report only reached the eruption literature in 2014 (Guevara-Murua et al.), more than 20 years after the ice-core discovery.

## 8. Overshadowed by Tambora
- **[Lit]** The 1809 event was found in the ice only in 1991 (Dai et al.). Before that, its cooling was folded into the "1810s cold decade" and attributed to Tambora (1815) or treated as background (Timmreck et al. 2021 and the cold-decade literature).
- **[Project]** The confusion runs into the sources themselves:
  - Restrepo's dim sun turned out to be autumn 1807;
  - Restrepo's "Cotopaxi 1808" may be a misdated 1803;
  - Zaō's "1809" is really 29 Dec 1809;
  - later writers (Pérez Valencia; Roulin 1831) recall 1808–09 only through comparison with 1815.

## 9. The archives are not digitized or not reachable
- **[Project]** The most likely places for a decisive witness are not searchable online:
  - Royal Navy and East India Company ship logs (most not digitized);
  - Dutch Hoge Regering records for Java 1808–11;
  - the AGI Filipinas and Guatemala series;
  - the daily Korean court diaries;
  - the Seville gazettes;
  - Caldas's correspondents' letters.
- **[Project] (26 Oct)** For eastern Indonesia, Wichmann's catalogue notes very few reports of any kind for 1806-1844 and no annual lists for 1809-1817, and Amboina's British-period archives are lost (Van der Crab 1862). The gap is in the record itself, not only in its digitization.

  Many digitized newspapers sit behind JavaScript viewers or blocks (Hemeroteca Digital Brasileira, BNE, Gallica, HathiTrust, Mediestream, Trove). See source_register.md.

## Bottom line
The published explanation is the combination of a remote tropical source with few witnesses, a weak tephra trace, and possibly several eruptions blended in the ice (sections 1–4). The project's own contribution is that the documentary record for exactly these months is unusually thin and fragmented: wartime disruption, lost registers and undigitized logs. A modest veil, rather than a dramatic event, is what the surviving witnesses describe.
