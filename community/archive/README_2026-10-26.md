# Searching for the 1808/09 "mystery" eruption

Open research notes from a documentary search for the unidentified volcanic eruption behind the 1809 sulfate layer in the Greenland and Antarctic ice cores. The search started with Spanish and Portuguese American sources and widened to East Asian chronicles, Hudson's Bay Company journals, ship logs and a reanalysis of published scientific data.

**This repository claims no discovery.** It records where we looked, what came up empty, and a few observations that might be worth checking, each with its verification level. Several will probably turn out to be weather, dust or coincidence. Nothing here has been peer reviewed.

## Verification levels used throughout
- **Page image checked**: the quotation was read on an image of the original printing or manuscript.
- **OCR only**: read from machine-transcribed text, not yet checked on an image.
- **Computed**: our own calculation from published or keyed data; reproducible with the scripts here.
- **Inferred**: an interpretation, stated as such.

"Not found in literature reviewed" means only that; it is not a claim that something is previously unknown.

## Potential evidence at a glance
See `findings/findings.csv`. If pressed for a working guess, the notes lean towards a low-latitude eruption between mid-November and early December 1808, in a region with no reachable witnesses (island Southeast Asia east of Java, Melanesia, the central Pacific or the Southern Ocean). That is an inference from the timing of the observations below, not a sighting. The items most worth a second look are the Edo red twilights from 9 Jan 1809, the packet *Townsend*'s haze in the Atlantic from 10 Dec 1808, the step in East India Company tropical air temperatures in Dec 1808 to Jan 1809, and the arrival of the 1809 sulfate at NEEM in late 1809, which argues against a mid-1808 eruption. As of 26 Oct 2026, comparing the 1809 Antarctic ice glass with published analyses from the candidate arcs rules out low-potassium arcs (Tonga, the New Britain arc front) but cannot choose among the rest; local records from the Moluccas, New Britain, the Banda arc and Halmahera are silent; and a severe 1809 drought around Batavia is a lead that has not yet been checked against El Niño reconstructions.

## How to help
The decisive records are mostly not online: monthly coral series from the north-east Indian Ocean, the original logs behind the CLIWOC haze entries (NMM NAI/3/17, NMM SPN/9, BL IOR/L/MAR/B/219D), Indian Ocean and western Pacific ship logs for March to December 1808, Japanese daily sources for winter 1808/09, the Kingston *Royal Gazette* for November 1808 to June 1809, and Caldas's correspondents' letters. Issues and pull requests with corrections, readings or new sources are welcome.

## Notes on the material
The notes were written during the search with AI assistance (Claude) and have been only partly edited; they keep their original round-by-round structure and may repeat each other. Quotations from historical sources are short and given for verification. Derived data come from CLIWOC 2.1, the oldweather/EEIC keyed logs, NEEM-2011-S1 (Sigl & McConnell 2022, PANGAEA 940553), San Fernando (Zenodo 7104289) and GHCN-Daily; cite the original datasets.
