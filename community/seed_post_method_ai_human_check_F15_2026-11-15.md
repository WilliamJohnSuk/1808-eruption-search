**Title:** A human check caught an AI error: a corona one day off (F15)

Before this repository went public, William John Suk read the page images behind headline findings himself (three so far; two more are with readers of Japanese and Dutch). In finding F15 he caught an error made by the AI.

**The claim.** In 1808, ten months before the 1808/09 veil, Francisco José de Caldas defined a "velo" (a thin, even cloud) in his Bogotá weather tables and sometimes logged one with a corona around the Sun. The AI record said this happened on **14 Feb 1808**.

**What William saw.** On the page (JCB copy on archive.org, February 1808 table, printed p. 83), the line for day 14 reads "C. cal. nieb.". The corona entry, "S ve. cla. cor. S.", is on the next line.

**Why.** The sky column is printed about half a row higher than the day numbers, so OCR and the AI reader paired each entry with the wrong day. We counted the 29 entries on the image from the top and from the bottom, and both counts put the corona on **15 Feb**. The AI had also cited the wrong issue: the table is in núm. 10 (6 Mar 1808), not núm. 7.

**And a third.** The record also said Caldas logged a veil "a handful of times a month". William then checked all seven 1808 tables himself: there are veils on 10 days out of 212, and a corona with a veil only twice (15 Feb, Sun; 12 Mar, Moon), plus one lunar corona without a veil (3 Apr).

**What changed.** The date and the citation are corrected. The finding itself stands. The full write-up is in [findings/CHECKS_README.md](https://github.com/WilliamJohnSuk/1808-eruption-search/blob/main/findings/CHECKS_README.md) under "Corrections caught by human checks".
