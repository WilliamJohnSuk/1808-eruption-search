### Guevara-Murua, Williams, Hendy, Rust & Cashman 2014, Clim. Past 10:1707–1722, doi:10.5194/cp-10-1707-2014
- Evidence: (1) Caldas, "Noticias meteorológicas", Semanario del Nuevo Reyno de Granada, Feb 1809, **transcribed from Posada (ed.) *Obras de Caldas*, Bogotá: Imprenta Nacional, 1912, pp. 347–350** (issue number not given; the 1809 printing itself does not appear to have been checked). Haze ("nube transparente que ofusca el brillo del sol"), silver sun, from 11 Dec 1808 to at least mid-Feb 1809; "en Pasto, en Popayán, en Neiva, en Santa Marta, en Tunja". (2) Unanue, *Observaciones sobre el clima de Lima*, 2nd ed., Madrid: Sancha, 1815, footnote: twilight glow in SW "between cerro de los Chorrillos and the sea", from mid-December, rising as a cone, shining until 8 pm, until mid-February. (3) Roulin, report to the Institut (1832) as printed in English in *New Monthly Magazine*, 1832, Historical Register pp. 170–171 (Appendix C, English only); authors judge it derivative of Caldas (same content and order), period "11th of December 1808 to the end of January 1809"; gives "first to the twelfth degree of south latitude" (an error: all five towns are N).
- Date: "4 December 1808 ±7 days" is a judgement, not a computed bound: eruption must precede 11 Dec; ±7 d based on Pinatubo/El Chichón (continuous 10°N–20°S layer within ~2 weeks) and Krakatau (blue sun in Bogotá ~7 d after). Caldas's own daily records after July 1808 "appear to be lost".
- Negative evidence: "To date, we have recovered no further observations of atmospheric disturbances in either Colombia or Peru in 1808–1809." No archive list or search log is published. They argue the source is "unlikely to have been in Latin America" partly because a large eruption would be expected in colonial archives, and no anomaly appears in the Quelccaya core.
- Single vs multiple: argue against two high-latitude eruptions (Yalcin 2006; Kurbatov 2006); both observation sites tropical, so one large tropical source. Cite Lamb for April 1809 London sunset haze as fitting a Dec 1808 eruption. Greenland flux 61% of Tambora, Antarctic 45%; suggest season or N-tropical source.

## Assessment for the brief
1. **Date.** Ice cores: deposition-based, Feb 1809 ±4 months ⇒ roughly Oct 1808–Jun 1809 (with ±1 yr age-scale uncertainty in some cores, e.g., TUNU 1810±1). The 4 Dec ±7 d date rests on (a) onset of observation, not of the phenomenon; Caldas's daily registers after July 1808 are lost, so "11 Dec" is a remembered or reported start; (b) an analogue transport time. It constrains only "before ~11 Dec"; the lower bound is open (an eruption weeks or months earlier, or one at higher latitude whose veil spread slowly, would also fit). The ±7 d is not an uncertainty in the statistical sense.
2. **Do the South American observations correspond to the polar signal?** Not established. Timmreck et al. say explicitly there is no direct link. Seasonality is a confounder: Bogotá's main dry season ("verano") begins in December, and Lima's winter stratus/garúa clears in Nov–Dec, so summer twilights first become observable then. An optical phenomenon first noted in mid-December in Lima is partly a visibility effect.
3. **One or several eruptions.** Tephra: andesitic (Antarctica), dacitic (Yukon), Indonesian-like (Dome C/South Pole), plus non-analysable shards (TUNU). Δ33S time-evolving pattern (abstract only). Current balance: at least one tropical stratospheric event plus probably ≥1 extratropical NH event close in time; the bipolar sulfate may itself be a composite. The CMIP7 inventory and Timmreck still model a single event (convention, not evidence).
4. **Prior archival searches.** Guevara-Murua: Colombia/Peru, Spanish, no published log. No published systematic search of Portuguese, Brazilian, Philippine, Dutch East Indies or Pacific sources specific to 1808–09 was found. Negative findings cannot exclude Latin America or anywhere else.
