# Antarctic sulfate map for 1809: per-core 1809 vs Tambora (26 Oct 2026)

Thread "Antarctic sulfate map for 1809", run without William (approved 26 Oct 19:40). Data: `data/antarctic_sulfate_1809_vs_tambora.csv`; map: `data/antarctic_sulfate_1809_vs_tambora_map.png`. Register: B46.

## Question

Is the 1809 sulfate in Antarctica a uniform, far-travelled (tropical-type) signal, or is there extra sulfate near northern Victoria Land, where Kurbatov et al. 2006 found 1810 trachytic glass at Siple Dome matched to the Pleiades or Mt Melbourne (a small local eruption in the same years)?

Method: for each core, divide its 1809 sulfate flux by its own Tambora (1815) flux. The ratio cancels most of the site effects (snowfall, wind, lab method). A uniform signal gives similar ratios everywhere; a local addition gives high ratios near the source.

## Plain-language conclusion

1. **Most of Antarctica shows a uniform signal.** The 15 independent cores away from Victoria Land have 1809/Tambora ratios of 0.26-0.76, mean 0.48, sd 0.12 (Claude's calculation). That matches the published averages (0.45, Cole-Dai 2009; 0.44, Gao composite). West Antarctica, the South Pole, Dronning Maud Land, Law Dome and the plateau all agree within the noise. Nothing here points to a source in any one sector: the main 1809 sulfate reached all of Antarctica evenly, like Tambora's.
2. **The two Talos Dome cores are the exception.** In northern Victoria Land, Talos Dome shows 1809 about as large as Tambora: ratio 1.05 (TD96) and 1.11 (TALDICE). That is about 5 standard deviations above the rest of Antarctica, or roughly 22-24 kg/km² of extra sulfate in 1809 (Claude's calculation; values page-image checked). Talos Dome is about 245 km from Mt Melbourne and the Pleiades. GV7, 450 km away on the coast, has a normal ratio of 0.57. Dome C, 1,190 km away, has the lowest ratio of all (0.26).
3. **What it suggests (inference, not a finding).** The pattern fits extra sulfate falling close to a Victoria Land volcano around 1809-10, which is the same local eruption whose glass Kurbatov found at Siple Dome. That eruption would be small and local. It adds to the Talos Dome record but does not explain the bipolar 1809 signal, which Greenland also holds (Greenland ratios 0.55-0.96, mean 0.70). The main eruption is still somewhere else. The Sigl/eVolv2k Antarctic composite does not use Talos Dome, so its 1809 estimate is not inflated by this.
4. **Weak points.** (a) The Talos Dome numbers come from one paper (Nardin et al. 2020, Table 2). **Checked against the page image on 26 Oct** (William's PDF, p. 8; images in `scans/nardin_2020_gv7/`): the GV7-ITASE cell is blank for the Tambora and 1809 rows, TD96 is 42.31 and 44.46, TALDICE is 34.44 and 38.32, and GV7 is 41.12 and 23.32. The values and the core assignment stand. The same page shows that the two Talos cores can disagree badly on single events: Reclus has TD96 119.7 vs TALDICE 22.66, and Samalas has TD96 21.72 vs TALDICE 121.51. So one high value at Talos would mean little. For 1809, both cores are high. Compared with GV7, Talos is about the same size for most events (TD96/GV7: Tambora 1.03, Krakatau 0.99, Agung 0.96, Cosiguina 0.77), but 1.91 for 1809 (Claude's calculation). (b) Both Talos values were processed by the same group and method, so they are one site, not two independent confirmations. (c) Talos Dome's 1809 and Tambora peaks are 0.37 m water apart (Stenni 2002), so a mis-split of the doublet or an extra local peak (the basanite layers Kurbatov found at 1804 and 1811) could also raise the 1809 figure. (d) Gao et al. 2007 describe 1809 deposition (in absolute flux) as low in Victoria Land, as for Tambora, Krakatau and Agung. Their Victoria Land core was probably Hercules Névé, so a high ratio there is not yet shown. (e) Siple Dome, the core with the local glass, cannot be tested: Tambora was lost in fractured ice.
5. **Supporting hint.** A KOPRI study of the Styx Glacier core (73°51'S 163°41'E, about 40 km from Mt Melbourne; Han et al., EGU2020-3262, abstract and slides only) found the Tambora fallout of the 1810s "overprinted by local volcanic aerosol input", told apart by lead isotopes and thallium. Victoria Land cores clearly record local volcanic sulfate in this decade. The abstract does not mention 1809.
