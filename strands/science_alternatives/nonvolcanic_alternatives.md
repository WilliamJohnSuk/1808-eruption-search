# Non-volcanic interpretations of the 1809 ice-core signal and the 1808–09 Colombian veil

Thread notes, 16 Oct 2026. Tools: WebSearch/WebFetch (text only, no page images) plus small calculations. Where a value comes from a fetch-tool summary rather than a page I read in full, that is said. Nothing here is claimed as new; "not found in the papers reviewed" means only that.

## Verdict in one paragraph

**Ice:** every non-volcanic source fails on at least two independent tests (mass of sulfur, bipolar timing, isotope pattern, glass shards in the layer). The only alternatives that survive are *volcanic* ones: several eruptions close together (tropical plus extratropical), and a possible ±1 yr age error in individual low-accumulation cores. **Colombia:** dry-season biomass-burning smoke is the one non-volcanic explanation that cannot be strictly ruled out from Caldas's text, but it fits badly (silver sun, green/steel-blue horizon tints, public alarm at something that recurs every year, simultaneous Lima twilight anomaly). Cirrus/altostratus, Saharan or local dust and a regional tropospheric Andean ash plume are ruled out or very unlikely. One alternative for Lima specifically is worth keeping: Unanue's cone-shaped evening glow could in part be the zodiacal light, which is steep and bright in Lima's clear summer skies; his timings argue against it, but only weakly.

## 1. The ice-core signal

### 1.1 What is measured
- **Sulfate flux, both poles, independently dated.** Sigl et al. 2013 (JGR 118:1151, doi:10.1029/2012JD018603; read via NERC repository PDF): WAIS Divide (WDC06A) deposition starts **1809.4**, ends 1812.1, 33.8 ± 1.8 kg km⁻²; NEEM S1 starts **1809.7**, ends 1811.2, 30.6 ± 2.0 kg km⁻². Fraction-of-year error 0.25 (≈3 months). Antarctic composite 25.1 ± 13.3, Greenland composite 25.9 ± 15.2 kg km⁻². Both timescales are annual-layer counts. The WDC tie points are Krakatau, Tambora and Huaynaputina, **not** 1809, so the bipolar coincidence is not imposed by synchronising on 1809 itself. In both hemispheres the event sits ~6 yr before Tambora.
- **Sulfur mass-independent fractionation (S-MIF):**
  - Antarctica, Dome C (Gautier et al. 2019, Nat. Commun. 10:466, doi:10.1038/s41467-019-08357-0; data PANGAEA doi:10.1594/PANGAEA.895169, rows read via WebFetch). Event 6.2: Δ33S **+0.19 ± 0.05‰** (age 1809.7, volcanic fraction 0.60), +0.05 ± 0.03‰ (1811.9, fV 0.75), **−0.23 ± 0.13‰** (1813.7, fV 0.35). Background variability is ~0.1‰. So there is a positive-then-negative sequence, the stratospheric pattern, though the negative value is only ~2σ. (Tambora at the same site: +0.16, +0.08‰.) One oddity: δ34S = −19.55‰ in the 1811.9 sample, against +15 for Tambora. I have not seen this discussed; it should be asked of the authors, not interpreted here.
  - South Pole: Cole-Dai et al. 2009 (GRL 36, L22703) report an S-MIF anomaly and simultaneous bipolar deposition (via ScienceDaily summary; paper not read in full).
  - Greenland: Lanciki 2010 (PhD, South Dakota State Univ., https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/etd2/1672) gives Δ33S ≈ **+0.18‰ and −0.11‰** and calls the event stratospheric (abstract only).
  - Hutchison et al. (COT-INTIMATE 2024 abstract, https://cot.iavceivolcano.org/content/uploads/sites/2/2024/09/cot_abstractvol_aug29-1.pdf; also the 2023 PAGES abstract): "Δ33S shows a more complex time-evolving pattern suggestive of multiple eruptions", and "ice-core cryptotephra extracted for 1809 ... suggest distinct geochemical tephra populations around this time". Abstract only; not peer-reviewed.
- **Tephra in the layer:** andesitic shards in Antarctica and dacitic shards at Eclipse Icefield, Yukon (Yalcin et al. 2006); Indonesian-like shards at Dome C / South Pole; brown microlitic shards with a particle spike in TUNU2013, Greenland (Plunkett et al. 2023). See notes/literature.md.

### 1.2 Alternatives tested

| Alternative | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Biomass burning** (incl. pyroCb smoke reaching the stratosphere) | Mass: biomass burning emits sulfur at the order of 1–3 Tg S/yr globally, spread through the year, mostly tropospheric with a lifetime of days. The 1809 estimates are 13–19 Tg S (eVolv2k/IVI2) for one event. The largest observed pyroCb episode (Australia 2019–20) put under 1 Tg of *smoke*, not sulfur, into the stratosphere (general literature; figure not re-checked here). Isotopes: non-volcanic S-MIF does exist. Shaheen et al. 2014 (PNAS 111:11979; known here only via the UCSD press release https://today.ucsd.edu/story/sulfur_signals_in_antarctic_snow_reveal_clues_to_climate_past_and_future) found the largest Δ33S anomaly in their South Pole snow pit in the year after the 1997–98 El Niño fires, and attributed it to fire-emitted OCS photolysed in the stratosphere. That anomaly came with **no extra sulfate** ("held no extra sulfur"). Lin et al. 2018 (PNAS, https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0646k5s6) show that tropospheric sulfate carries Δ33S from −0.66 to +0.53‰ and that combustion leaves a negative Δ36S with a steep Δ36S/Δ33S slope (≈ −4); stratospheric volcanic sulfate has a slope of ≈ −1.9. | **Ruled out as the source of the sulfate.** It fails by roughly an order of magnitude on mass and has no mechanism to give a bipolar flux comparable to half of Tambora. Caveat worth stating plainly: S-MIF *alone* is not a unique volcanic fingerprint. What makes 1809 volcanic is the MIF together with the large sulfate spike and the shards. |
| **Dust** (gypsum/sulfate-bearing mineral dust) | Would co-vary with Ca²⁺ and other crustal ions and show no MIF; cannot be bipolar and synchronous. | **Ruled out.** |
| **Marine biogenic sulfur (DMS) / sea salt** | Removed by the non-sea-salt correction; seasonal; no MIF; not bipolar-synchronous. | **Ruled out.** |
| **Anthropogenic sulfur** (coal, 1809) | British coal use ~10–15 Mt/yr ⇒ ≲0.2 Tg S/yr (rough estimate), steady, NH-only and tropospheric. | **Ruled out.** |
| **Meteoritic / cometary** | Chondrites carry a few wt% S, so ~10 Tg S needs a body of a few hundred Tg (~0.5 km stony), i.e. a globally catastrophic impact with no historical trace. Tunguska (1908) left nitrate and ammonium anomalies in Greenland, not a sulfate spike (Melott et al. 2010, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.1067). Cosmic dust deposition is negligible. Volcanic glass in the layer is incompatible as the main source. | **Ruled out.** Platinum-group elements have not, to my knowledge, been measured in the 1809 layer; that would be a cheap check, but there is no reason to expect a signal. |
| **Dating artefact / misaligned cores** (Greenland and Antarctic peaks really different events) | The layer counts are independent and not tied on 1809; the onsets agree to ~0.3 yr (1809.4 vs 1809.7, each ±0.25) and both sit ~6 yr before Tambora. S-MIF in both Greenland and Antarctica shows stratospheric sulfur at both poles. TUNU2013 dates the layer 1810 ± 1 and Dome C ages are coarse (~1.5 yr samples): individual cores can be off by about a year, but the bipolar match is robust. | **Bipolar coincidence stands.** The possibility left open is that the two hemispheres received sulfur from **different eruptions** a few months apart. That is a volcanic alternative, not a non-volcanic one. |
| **Several smaller eruptions** (tropical + extratropical, within ~1 yr) | Tephra of different composition in the two hemispheres (andesitic Antarctic vs dacitic Yukon; Indonesian-like shards); Hutchison et al.'s "complex time-evolving" Δ33S; NEEM deposition shorter (1.5 yr) than WDC (2.7 yr). | **Survives, and is currently the most serious alternative to one big tropical eruption.** It matters for this project: the climate forcing may be split, and the Colombian/Lima veil need not belong to the eruption that dominates the Antarctic sulfate. |

### 1.3 What would discriminate further (ice)
1. Published sub-annual Δ33S/Δ36S profiles for 1809 in Greenland **and** Antarctica, on one timescale. A single tropical event should show the same positive→negative sequence starting at the same time at both poles. A composite would show offset or extra MIF-free pulses. (Hutchison/Burke/Sigl group; not yet published as of 16 Oct 2026, as far as found.)
2. Major-element glass chemistry of each shard population, matched to candidate volcanoes. This is the only route to a *location*.
3. Co-registered fire tracers (NH₄⁺, black carbon, levoglucosan) across the 1809 layer in NEEM/TUNU. Not checked here. They would close off the biomass-burning route completely, although the mass argument already does.

## 2. The veil seen from Colombia (and Lima)

### 2.1 What the primary texts say (from project notes; Caldas image-verified, Unanue OCR)
- **Caldas** (Semanario 1809 Núm. 5, 5 Feb 1809): from 11 Dec 1808, a silver sun "mistaken for the moon", strongest at rising and setting, still unbearable at mid-sky. Near the horizon "rosa muy ligero, verde muy claro, azulado gris ... acero". A very light, uniform, transparent cloud over the whole sky, the blue of the sky down to the first cyanometer grades, some days white. **No coronas**, unlike the ordinary "velo". Stars of magnitude 1–3 dimmed and 4–5 vanished. Constant day and night. Dry weather, intermittent southerly winds and calms, frosts. Seen at Pasto, Popayán, Neiva, Santa Marta and Tunja. Public alarm ("indicio seguro de grandes calamidades"). Still visible when he wrote (~2 months). He explains it as "una nube extendida ... en la región superior de nuestra atmósfera", plus horizon vapours and refraction, and says "Mil veces he observado la misma disposición en el cielo".
- **Unanue** (Lima, 1815 ed. pp. 13–14 fn. 3, OCR): from mid-December, a cone-shaped evening glow in the SW (towards Chorrillos and the sea) after sunset, rising from the horizon to the zenith, bright until 8 pm, every night until mid-February.
- **Pérez Valencia** (Popayán; Acosta 1849 p. 79, OCR): the dim sun of 24 Apr 1815 recalled "diciembre de 1808 y enero de 1809".
- The Gaceta de Caracas, El Aviso and the Semanario (Dec 1808–1809) report no ashfall, smell, quake or eruption (notes/downloads_2026-10-16_findings.md). Caldas's 4 Jun 1809 note says he never saw smoke on Tolima.

### 2.3 What would discriminate (Colombia/Lima)
1. **Any report of smell, smoke or "humo" or of burnt savanna in Dec 1808**, or any reference by Caldas's correspondents to fires. Smoke would probably be named. Its absence from Caldas's note is suggestive but not proof; check whether his 1808 sky codes (Núm. 7) include smoke or haze.
2. **Colour and ring descriptions elsewhere:** a Bishop's ring (a large brownish halo round the sun) or purple light after sunset reported anywhere in the tropics in Dec 1808–Mar 1809 would point to stratospheric sulfate. Caldas notes no ring at all.
3. **Day-to-day records:** the lost daily sheets (Caldas Bogotá after Jul 1808; Popayán, Cartagena, Alegría correspondents) would show whether the veil was steady (aerosol) or varied with wind and fire (smoke).
4. **Lima:** whether Unanue saw the glow with the moon up (zodiacal light would vanish), and whether it began before mid-December. Other Lima diarists or the *Minerva Peruana* (ran to 1810) could settle this.
5. **Southern Hemisphere and Old World twilights** in the same weeks (ships' logs, Cape, India). A global stratospheric veil should show there. Smoke from the Llanos could not.

## 3. Bottom line for the project
- The ice record is volcanic. The open question is **one eruption or several**, not volcanic or not.
- The Colombian observation is best explained by a high, uniform aerosol veil. Biomass-burning smoke is the only non-volcanic rival. It survives as a contributor but fits the colours, the alarm, the winds, the uniformity and the Lima simultaneity poorly.
- The link between the Colombian veil and the ice-core sulfate remains an inference from timing alone (Timmreck et al. 2021 say the same). If 1809 was a composite, the veil may belong to only one of the eruptions.
