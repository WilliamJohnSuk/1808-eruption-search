**What was wrong.** The Lombok and Palau files store dates as two-decimal years on a start-of-month grid (1808.83 = November, 1809.08 = February). Guam (Asami 2005) uses a mid-month grid where the floor rule is right, and keeps it; the other monthly records on irregular grids also keep it. `footprint.py` and `fig.py` now pick the rule per record.

## 1. Short answer

- **The three papers named in option 4 say nothing about 1808-1812.** Charles et al. 2003 (Lombok) never mention Tambora, volcanoes or any year before 1850 except to say the Bali record shows "pronounced low-frequency variability, particularly in the 19th century". Bagnato et al. 2005 (Fiji) have no discussion of 1800-1820 at all. Linsley et al. 2006 (Fiji and Rarotonga) discuss only Tambora: after 1815 the SPCZ "shifted north and/or contracted for almost a decade", seen as a 0.4 per mil rise in Rarotonga δ18O from 1814 to 1823 that is "not observed in Fiji". None of the three computed anomalies for 1809-1812, and none cites the 1809 eruption. All three read in full [text].
- **The footprint is partly prior art.** D'Arrigo et al. 2006, a warm-pool SST reconstruction built for 1782-1860 on the same Lombok coral plus Javanese teak, already found a "pronounced decade-long cool period" from about 1808 to 1818, gave 1809-1812 anomalies of −0.28, −0.73, −0.76 and −0.79 °C (September-November, ±0.46 °C) against −0.30, −0.51 and −0.51 °C for 1815-1817, and wrote that the unknown 1809 eruption "has the greatest impact of the two on the reconstructed SSTs" [text, read in full]. Tierney et al. 2015 (Ocean2k) find the western Pacific's coolest decade of 400 years to be 1808-1817 (−0.5 °C) and the Indian Ocean's 1807-1816 (−0.8 °C), attributed to "a pair of strong volcanic eruptions during the early 1800s" (1809 and Tambora) plus the Dalton minimum [text, read in full]. Timmreck et al. 2021 (B58) compared the model to these same Tierney curves and called the western Pacific signal weak.
- **What is new here [calc].** (1) At monthly resolution the excursion at Lombok Strait (13 km from Agung) and at Palau (7°N) peaks in the same month, December 1808 (Lombok was already raised in November; corrected 27 Nov 2026, see the correction above), which the published chronologies place one month after our inferred eruption date; Guam (14°N) was already cool from June 1808. (2) The July 1809-June 1813 window at Lombok is the most extreme four-year window of its 209-year record, and ten of the twelve most extreme months of 1782-1990 fall in 1810-1812. (3) The spatial pattern: cool or dry at Lombok, Palau, Guam, Fiji, New Caledonia, Abraham Reef, Malindi and the Galápagos; nothing or the opposite at Vanuatu, Tonga, Rarotonga, Madagascar, Western Australia, the Caribbean and the Red Sea. (4) The Queensland rainfall reconstruction shows no drought in 1809-1812, so the Coral Sea signal is temperature, not runoff. (5) The eastern Pacific cooled in 1810-1811 (La Niña-like) while the Maritime Continent stayed cool and dry, which is not an ENSO pattern; the signal is volcanic or at least not ENSO [inference].
- **Vanuatu cannot test Gaua.** The only coral that spans 1809 near Gaua is Quinn et al. 1996 at Espiritu Santo, 98 km from Gaua (not 300 km as the strategy note said; distance recomputed from the archived coordinates [calc]). It is an annual record averaged from two to four samples a year, and it shows no cool or dry excursion in 1809-1812 under any baseline (δ18O −5.02, −5.00, −5.16, −5.06 per mil against a 1807-1899 mean of −4.94, so warmer or wetter by 0.3 to 1.1 sd). A local ash effect lasting weeks would not survive annual averaging, and a regional climate signal is absent at Vanuatu, Tonga and Rarotonga alike. The record neither supports nor excludes Gaua.
- **For the search** the corals do not point at a volcano. They describe what the eruption did: a cool, dry excursion over the Maritime Continent, the Coral Sea and the East African coast that outlasted Tambora's at the same sites, which is the natural-archive twin of the 1809 Java rice failure and the 1809 Guangdong-Guangxi drought (B51, B57, B60). That is the side result (M13), and it is written below so that it can be lifted into the synthesis or the blog.

## 4. Data and method [calc]

- **Source.** PAGES2k v2 (Emile-Geay et al. 2017) as packaged in github.com/fzhu2e/cfr-data (`pages2kv2.json`), the only route open from here (NOAA Paleo is blocked from Claude's cloud container for downloads, though the fetch tool can read single text files on ncei.noaa.gov and the LTRR mirror). Every coral record with data in 1806-1816 was taken: 34 records at 25 sites. Palmyra, Mafia (Tanzania) and Bunaken have no data in the window; Hendy 2002 and Calvo 2007 (GBR) are five-year bins; Wei 2009 is five-year blocks before 1940.
- **Anomalies.** Sub-annual records: monthly climatology 1790-1830 by calendar month, anomaly divided by the standard deviation of all monthly anomalies of 1790-1830, then July-June means (the year named is the July year), kept when at least six months exist. Annual records: anomaly from the 1790-1830 mean divided by its standard deviation. Records that begin after 1790 (Vanuatu 1807, Wei 1807) use their first 50 years; Malindi (from 1801) has enough of the window. Raw proxy sign throughout: higher δ18O or Sr/Ca = cooler and/or saltier (drier); for Hendy's SST the sign is reversed in the "cool-dry index". Calcification records carry no sign.
- **Windows.** 1806-08 (before), 1809-12 (the excursion; for monthly records July 1809 to June 1813), 1815-18 (Tambora). The percentile column gives the share of all four-year windows in the whole record that are at least as cool/dry as 1809-12, so 0.5 means the most extreme window of the record.
- **Differences from the 28 Oct strategy note.** That note standardised each calendar month by its own standard deviation; this one uses a single standard deviation per record, which is more stable for months with few extreme values. Lombok December 1808 is +2.1 sd here (19th highest of 492 months in 1790-1830 after the 27 Nov binning correction; 18th before it) against +2.5 (14th) there; ten (nine before the correction) rather than seven of the twelve most extreme months fall in 1810-12. The yearly values differ by less than 0.1 sd. A half-year rounding error in a first run of this note's script (annual records dated to mid-year) was found and fixed before any number below was written.
- **External series.** Tierney 2015 (four regions), Lough 2007 and 2015, Wei 2009 and the Vanuatu raw values were transcribed from the NCEI and LTRR text files through the fetch tool; D'Arrigo 2006 values were transcribed from the paper. All are in `coral_footprint_external_series_2026-10-28.csv` with their URLs.

## 6. ENSO cross-check [inference from the numbers above and B58]

- In **1810-1811** the eastern Pacific was cool (Galápagos +1.65 and +2.32 sd; Tierney −0.65 and −0.95 °C; tree rings La Niña). A La Niña wets and warms the Maritime Continent. Lombok, Palau and Guam were instead cool and dry. The Maritime Continent excursion of 1810-1812 is therefore not an ENSO signal; a volcanic weakening of the Australian-Indonesian monsoon, or a positive Indian Ocean Dipole-like state, is what remains, and the first is what large tropical eruptions do in the following year or two [memory, the Tambora and Pinatubo literature].
- In **December 1808-mid 1809** the western corals jump together (Lombok +2.1, Palau +2.2 in December 1808; Lombok +0.9 already in November) while the Galápagos coral is cool in 1808 (+0.94) and Tierney's eastern Pacific is −0.33 °C in April 1808-March 1809: no eastern-Pacific warming. The "El Niño-like DJF 1808/09" of the coral ENSO indices (Freund 2019; B58) rests on these same western records, which an aerosol veil pushes the same way. This is option 19 of the strategy note in one paragraph: the coral classifiers mistook the eruption for an El Niño, and the tree rings, which say La Niña, are not contradicted by the eastern Pacific.
- The signal is also not a warm-pool salinity artefact: Chenoweth's marine air temperatures (D'Arrigo 2006) and the EEIC step of December 1808-January 1809 (B38) show the cooling in the air.

## 7. What this says about the eruption [inference]

1. **Not a locator.** The pattern is a climate response: strongest where the Australian-Indonesian monsoon and the East African short rains are felt (Lombok, Malindi) and in the Coral Sea, weak or absent in the central South Pacific, Western Australia and the Atlantic. Any tropical source produces it. The map does not distinguish Dakataua, Gaua, Cabalían or the Banda arc.
2. **Gaua is untestable by coral.** The Espiritu Santo record is annual, 98 km away, and flat. No monthly coral spanning 1809 exists in Vanuatu in PAGES2k (Gorman 2012 at Sabine Bank begins 1842). A re-sampled Quinn core at monthly resolution for 1807-1812 would be the test, and that is new laboratory work (suggestion, not a finding).
3. **Agung.** The Lombok coral grew without interruption 13 km from Agung through 1808-1813 and survived Agung 1963, so continuity does not exclude an Agung source; equally, the December 1808 jump appears at Palau 2,100 km away in the same month, so it is regional and does not single out Agung. Nothing here changes Agung's "possible" rank in B53.
4. **Timing.** Two monthly records peak in December 1808 by their published chronologies (Lombok already raised in November; corrected 27 Nov 2026), one month after the Bogotá veil (11 December 1808) and consistent with the November-December 1808 date inference. Guam starts in June 1808. With chronology errors of "several months" this neither confirms nor refutes an earlier (Chenoweth) date; it does show that by December 1808 the western Pacific was already responding, which an eruption in March-July 1808 would also allow.
5. **Size and character (M13).** At Lombok the excursion is longer and larger than the site's Tambora response; at Fiji, Abraham Reef, Malindi and the Galápagos it is of the same order as Tambora's or larger; D'Arrigo 2006 already drew the same conclusion from the warm-pool reconstruction. The response was as much hydrological (dry) as thermal, which bears on Timmreck 2021's puzzle of a "weak" 1809 temperature response in the Northern Hemisphere tree rings: in the tropics the response was not weak, it was wet-dry.
6. **The Java and South China droughts.** The Lombok coral is dry from February 1810, not in the 1809 dry season (July-November 1809 normal). The documented Java shortfall of 1809 (decree of 14 July 1809, "unusual drought" order of 5 October 1809, B51 and B57) therefore precedes the coral's drought by half a year. Either the Batavia drought of 1809 was local or labour-driven (Daendels), or the Lombok record's 1809 dry season is mis-dated by a few months, or the coral responded to the 1809/10 wet season failing rather than to the 1809 dry season. The coral cannot adjudicate the 1809 decree; it does document a regional drought of 1810-1812 that the Dutch archives have not yet been searched for (inv. 23 and 2.01.27.03 inv. 96 are the nearest items, B57).

## 8. Caveats

- Coral chronologies slip by months and occasionally a year (Charles 2003; Linsley 2006 cross-check only from 1856). Monthly coincidences between sites are suggestive, not proof.
- δ18O mixes temperature and salinity; at Lombok and Fiji the published calibrations say salinity (rainfall) dominates the interannual part. "Cool/dry" is the honest label.
- One standardisation window (1790-1830) was used for all; it is short, and it includes the excursion itself, which if anything understates the anomaly.
- The Vanuatu record's early part has two to four samples a year, and its 1996 authors doubled the resolution because the 1993 version was doubted; the 1807-1812 values stand on few measurements.
- Wei 2009's 1810-14 block is a single bulk sample and should not be quoted on its own.
- PAGES2k v2 is a temperature-screened subset; corals that failed its screening (e.g. the Quinn 1998 New Caledonia δ18O, Tudhope's Madang) are not here, and the NOAA originals of the records here were not re-read except Lombok's header and five rows.
- The regional composites average records with very different sensitivities; they are a reading aid, not a reconstruction.
