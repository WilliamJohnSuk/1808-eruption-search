## 1. Answer in short

- **No published trace-element (LA-ICP-MS) analysis exists for any glass shard of 1805–1812 age in any ice core, in either hemisphere.** Every 1809-era analysis found is major-element (SEM-EDS, or EPMA at Siple Dome). The comparison with candidate trace signatures (GEOROC) therefore cannot be run. The desk-study limit stands.
- **New since 22 Oct:** (a) a second South Pole 1809 cryptotephra sample, from the new SPICEcore (SPC14), analysed 2020–22 by Helmick, Kurbatov and Yates (University of Maine); (b) a USAP-DC spreadsheet (Wheatley, 601038) that holds 1809 tephra data from four Antarctic cores; (c) a firm negative for 1809 glass in two AWI cores in Dronning Maud Land (Abbott et al. 2024).
- The two new data files (b, and the SPC14 data behind a) are the best chance of individual-shard numbers. Both need a person's browser.

## 4. What this means (Claude's inference)

1. The South Pole has now been sampled twice for 1809 (PS1, 1990; SPC14, 2022). The 2022 work found a broader spread (dacite–rhyolite "subtropical" and trachyte "local" particles as well as the andesite). That agrees with the project's reading of more than one 1809 source, but the abstract does not separate the 1809 particles from the Tambora ones.
2. Particles under 2 µm are too small for LA-ICP-MS with current spot sizes. Trace elements on 1809 ice glass will need larger shards. The only 1809-era layers with larger shards on record are Siple Dome (local trachyte) and TUNU (8–15 µm, unanalysed).
3. The Dronning Maud Land negative (T3) is a point for the project's map: those plateau sites at 30–32°E received 1809 sulfate but no glass large enough to find. This fits a distant (tropical) source, but is not proof of one.
