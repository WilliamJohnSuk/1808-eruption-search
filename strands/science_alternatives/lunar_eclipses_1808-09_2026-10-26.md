## 3. What this means

- Nothing found either way, so the eclipses do not yet test the mid-Nov to early Dec 1808 estimate.
- The 3 Nov 1808 total eclipse was invisible in Europe, so European silence about it means nothing. The only possible witnesses were in the Americas between about 01:30 and 04:00 local time, in Hawaii, and at moonrise in Japan, Australia and the western Pacific. Ferrer (Havana) and Caldas (Bogotá), the two careful American observers who timed the May 1808 eclipse, left no published observation of 3 Nov 1808 that we have found.
- The 30 Apr 1809 partial eclipse was well placed for every European observatory. Its observations should exist in Bode's Jahrbuch 1812/1813, Zach's MC 1809, the Connaissance des Temps 1811/1812, the Vienna and Milan ephemerides, and the Madrid/San Fernando records. These were not searchable from here. If those reports give only timings, as Ferrer's and the San Fernando ones do, that itself suggests the eclipse looked unremarkable.
