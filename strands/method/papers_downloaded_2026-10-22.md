## 1. Stothers 2005, PASP 117: 1445-1450, "Stratospheric Transparency Derived from Total Lunar Eclipse Colors, 1801-1881" (file Stothers_2005_PASP_117_1445.pdf)
- Method: he searched almost 300 journals (no newspapers, diaries or logs) and found colour reports for 31 of the 54 total lunar eclipses that von Oppolzer lists for 1801-1881. "Until 1860, only about half of all total lunar eclipses were reported."
- **The catalogue (Table 1) has no entry between 1805 Jul 11 and 1812 Feb 27.** So there is no published colour report in his corpus for any eclipse of 1808-1811. Our ranked target 4 (3 Nov 1808, 30 Apr 1809) is still open: if those eclipses were total, their colour has to come from newspapers, diaries, logs or observatory registers, the sources Stothers did not search.
- 1812 Feb 27 (Lofft, Monthly Magazine 33:210) and 1812 Aug 22 (Moorcroft, from Tibet, Quarterly Review 17:429) were both L = 4, "visible". Stothers: "If real, the eruption's atmospheric effects must have dissipated by 1812 February 27 and August 22 ... however, highly colored twilights did appear in the second half of 1811 (Russell 1888)."
- He cites Clausen et al. 1997, for whom the only significant Greenland acid signal besides 1816 is in 1810, and Antarctic acid signals in 1809, 1816, 1831 and 1836 (Delmas et al. 1992; Cole-Dai et al. 2000), "confirming that the first three were due to tropical eruptions".
- **Lead:** Russell 1888 (Krakatoa Committee report, ed. Symons, p. 384) lists coloured twilights in the second half of 1811. It is on archive.org, so it can be checked for any 1808-1810 entries.

## 2. Cole-Dai, Ferris, Lanciki, Savarino, Baroni & Thiemens 2009, GRL 36: L22703, doi:10.1029/2009GL040882
- Δ33S (Table 1): Summit, Greenland, early sample +0.18 ‰ and late sample −0.12 ‰ (±0.08), the same positive-to-negative pattern as Tambora, so the 1809 sulfur was stratospheric. In Antarctica, only single bulk samples were measured: Dome C +0.10 ± 0.17 and South Pole 0.003 ± 0.12, which show no anomaly. The authors say this "does not directly lead to the conclusion that the 1809 stratospheric eruption occurred in the tropics". The tropical conclusion rests on the timing and flux below.
- Timing (Table 2, sub-annual layer counting, ±4 months). Midpoint of the 1809 deposition: **South Pole SP01 Jun 1810 (21 months long); WAIS Divide WDC Jul 1810 (22 months); Summit SU07 Sep 1810 (17 months).** Midpoint to Tambora midpoint: on average 77 months in Antarctica and 72 in Greenland. The authors judge the deposition "essentially at the same time" in both hemispheres. The 1809/Tambora flux ratio is 0.45 in Antarctica and 0.61 in Greenland.
- **Inference (mine, not theirs):** apply each core's own Tambora lag (eruption Apr 1815 to deposition midpoint: South Pole about 21 months, WDC 18, Summit 17) to its 1809 midpoint. That gives an eruption in about Sep 1808 (South Pole), Jan 1809 (WAIS Divide) and Apr 1809 (Summit). The Antarctic cores point earlier than Greenland, which fits our "southern source, mid-Nov to early Dec 1808" reading but is inside the ±4 month error. Note: this WAIS Divide core is WDC06A, the same core as our ranked target 1. Its sub-annual 1809 timing is already published here, so the target becomes a check of their timing and of the onset (not the midpoint) against NEEM.
- The paper does not give the onset month in each core (Fig. 1 only). The auxiliary material (Text S1, Table S1) has the dating details. It is on the AGU page as supporting information and was not downloaded.

## 3. Kurbatov et al. 2006, JGR 111: D12307, doi:10.1029/2005JD006072 (Siple Dome, West Antarctica)
- Table 1: the 1809 sulfate signal (residual 3.1) has tephra matched to "Buckle Island, Pleiades, Antarctica".
- Table 4, sample SDMA-166, 33.75 m, age 1810 CE, n = 8 shards: **trachytic glass, SiO2 63.4, Al2O3 16.77, FeO 6.3, CaO 1.21, Na2O 5.99, K2O 4.91 wt%.** Text §27: "geochemically similar to known eruption products from the Pleiades volcanic center but the Mount Melbourne volcanic center ... is also potential candidate. Timing of this local, Antarctic eruption coincides with the 1809 C.E unknown eruption." Also a basanitic layer at 33.61 m (1811 CE) and one at 34.60 m (1804 CE), with poor source matches (Balleny Islands or Royal Society Range suggested).
- They favour Moore et al. (1991): two simultaneous eruptions, one in each hemisphere, because the Illimani (Bolivia) core shows no signal 6 years before Tambora (De Angelis et al. 2003). Tambora was missed in their core (fractured ice).
- **Comparison (inference):** Yalcin 2006 Yukon 1809 glass is also trachytic (SiO2 ~64.9, Na2O ~6.6) but has K2O ~2.7 against 4.9 here, so the two are probably not the same magma. Palais 1990 / Yalcin 2006 call the South Pole and Dome C 1809 shards andesitic to trachyandesitic, which differs again. So three different 1809 glass populations are on record (Yukon, Siple Dome, South Pole/Dome C), and none is from a tropical volcano.
- Related, checked 22 Oct: De Angelis et al. 2003, ACP 3: 1725 (free). The Illimani conductivity (ECM) record "does not show any marked signal 6 years before Tambora, i.e. around 73 m depth". The chemistry was analysed only for 71.0-72.3 m (1815-17), so this is a conductivity-only negative.
- Related, checked 22 Oct (Dunbar's New Mexico Tech ice-core research page, text): in WAIS Divide WDC06A, five sulfate intervals including "an unknown eruption (1809)" were filtered and microprobed; "no population of tephra shards with chemical compositions corresponding to the known eruptions were found". They suggest the silicate from these eruptions does not reach Antarctica.

## 4. Wohlt 1978, PhD thesis, University of Minnesota, "Ecology, agriculture and social organization: the dynamics of group composition in the highlands of Papua New Guinea" (file "dissertation .pdf"; scanned, OCR)
- His field plan includes a "Time of darkness" item: find who knows the story and ask set questions. **The thesis does not report the results.** "darkness" occurs only in the field plan, and the thesis never mentions ash, tephra, volcano or yuu kuia. The legend 059 material is therefore only in Blong 1982 and the ANU tape.
- Yumbisa (upper Wage) settlement: the founder Kapii is 4-6 generations before married adults of 1975. Wohlt's reconstruction starts about 1800-1820 (graph axes), but "The situation before 1840 is thus uncertain"; slope-to-valley transition around 1830; valley clearance from 1860. Knowledgeable men recall 8-9 ascending generations; "the world will end after eleven generations".
- Bad frosts about 1900 and about 1940 (people dispersed to relatives at lower altitude). No earlier disaster is described.
- Meaning (inference): settlement of the valley in about 1800-1840 would make an ashfall *remembered by Yumbisa people* post-1800 if it happened there. But the thesis gives no date link between the darkness legend and the settlement history.
