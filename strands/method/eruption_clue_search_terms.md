# Search terms for eruption clues, by language of the text searched

Written 16 Oct 2026 by the "non-volcanic interpretations" thread at William's request, for every thread rescanning downloads, online papers, books and archives. Version 2: one block per language, covering all nine clue types plus the veil itself, in period spelling with common OCR variants. Not yet tested against any corpus, so expect noise and check every hit on the page image.

**How to use:** pick the block for the language of the text, not the language of the searcher. Search the stem where the engine allows wildcards (e.g. `volcan*`, `cinz*`). A hit only counts once the page image has been checked and the date of the *event* (not the paper) is established.

## Windows
- **Americas, Europe, Atlantic:** Oct 1808 – Dec 1809.
- **Asia, Indian Ocean, Pacific (and news about them printed anywhere):** Oct 1808 – **Jun/Jul 1810**. News from the East took 4–8 months to reach Europe or the Americas.
- Best sections: shipping news / arrivals, captains' letters, extracts from foreign papers, "varieties"/"miscellany" columns, weather tables and their remarks.

## The clue types (numbering used in every block)
0 the veil itself (dim/silver/red sun, haze, dry fog) · 1 booms or "gunfire" with no battle · 2 ashfall, dust, darkness by day · 3 floating pumice · 4 volcano, eruption, fire/glow, new island · 5 earthquake, sea retreat, tsunami, dead fish, discoloured sea · 6 mud floods, grey rivers · 7 sulfur smell, acid fog, scorched crops · 8 optical effects (red twilights, blue/green sun, ring round the sun, dark lunar eclipse) · 9 knock-on effects (dearth, frost, epidemic, public prayers)

## OCR variants to allow for (Latin script)
- Long s read as f: "afhes", "fulphur", "ftone", "fea", "fhock", "fmoke"; in Spanish/Portuguese "efpefo", "obfcuro"; in German Fraktur ſ/f and s/f confusion, and ä/ö/ü printed as aͤ/oͤ/uͤ or a/o/u.
- u/v, i/j and y/i swaps; missing or odd accents; c/e, rn/m, li/h confusion.
- Spanish 1808: x for j (*baxo, dixo*), ç (*ceniça* rare), *qual, quando*, *obscuro*. Portuguese 1808: *ph* for f (*phenomeno*), *y* for i, *-ão/-am*, *he* for é. Dutch 1808: *ae/aa*, *y/ij*, *ch/g*, *sch* endings (*asch, Javaansch*). French 1808: *-oit/-oient* imperfects (*paroissoit*), *tems*. German 1808: *th* (*Thal, Theil*), *-iren* (*explodiren*). Italian: *j* for i (*gennajo*).

## Notes

- Classical Chinese, Korean and Japanese records mostly use the fixed phrases in their blocks. The "East Asian and Arabic sources" thread's search log (notes/asian_arabic_round4.md) shows which of these were already searched.

## Additions 21 Oct 2026 (from notes/eruption_markers_knowledge_base.md)

Added by the "Eruption markers knowledge base" thread. Each term comes from period wording found in accounts of Huaynaputina 1600, Mindanao 1641, Fuji 1707, Laki 1783, Tambora 1815, Dutch East Indies reports 1822-1908, Cosegüina 1835 and Krakatau 1883 (sources and verification levels in the knowledge base), unless marked *(inferred)*. Same clue numbering as above.

**The main lesson:** distant explosions were almost always reported as gunfire: a battle, a ship in distress or at gun practice, pirates, troops turned out, or blamed on the nearest local volcano. In 1808-10 wartime papers, search naval news, pirate alarms and "supposed engagement" items with clue-1 terms. Omen and sermon vocabulary (day of judgement, divine anger) can find far-field red suns.
