# Language corrections log: colonial framing of literacy and inhabitants

Started 10 Nov 2026 at William John Suk's request, after he flagged the phrase "Literate Spanish parishes nearby in 1808" in a draft Discussions post. Keep adding rows: date, file, old wording, new wording, whose words they were.

**Observed bias (William's request to record it).** Claude, the AI model that drafted most of this repository, repeatedly defaulted to a colonial view of literacy: it described places as having "no literate witnesses" or "effectively nobody writing", called inhabitants "natives", and treated a Spanish parish as the mark of a literate place. Those regions were inhabited, and their people kept history, mostly orally. What is missing is a *surviving written record in the archives we can reach*, which is a statement about the archives, not about the people. Readers of any AI-drafted note here should watch for the same framing. This is the project's own observation from its own drafts, not a published finding.

**Rule from now on.** Our own prose says "written records survive / do not survive", "parish registers were kept", "local residents", "inhabitants", "oral traditions". Quotations and titles from period sources stay verbatim (for example Mariner's *An Account of the Natives of the Tonga Islands*, 1817), because changing them would falsify the source; where useful, a note can say that the word is the source's.

## Strongest instances and why they arose (12 Nov 2026, at William's request)

William asked which original wording made the strongest claim erasing Indigenous people, and why, "not to shame these mistakes, but [to explain] them so as to improve analytical acuity". Searched: every .md note in 1808-eruption/ and the repo copy, the project chat, and 13 threads (the first research thread, Taking stock, Rank, Dakataua profile, Moluccas/New Britain records, New Britain/Banda/Halmahera, Oral histories, Think creatively, Wide search, Pre-launch synthesis, plus this one). About 35 other threads were not read.

1. **Strongest: "It's a caldera with nobody there to see it in 1808."** Claude's chat reply to William, thread "Rank the candidate volcanoes", 26 Oct 2026 23:04 UTC (same message: "the only one from an unwatched volcano"). It denies the presence of people, not of records. Twenty-five minutes earlier (thread "Profile Dakataua", 22:39 UTC) Claude had reported that Makalia's last eruption is dated "only from what villagers said in 1963", quoting Johnson 2013: "Information collected in 1963 from villagers at the northern end of Willaumez Peninsula". The note itself only said "no European observer"; the chat summary hardened it.
2. **"Remote or unpopulated source" and "where in 1808 there was effectively nobody writing"** (why_unidentified.md section 4, 16 Oct; corrected 10 Nov). The most-read synthesis file, so the widest reach.
3. **The watched / partly watched / blank zoning** (22 Oct; still live in data and script). Not a sentence but an instrument: it labelled regions by whether we had read their records, then ranked volcanoes higher for being "blank". The words "unwatched" and "nobody there" in item 1 come straight from it.
4. **"Makassarese trepang fleets ... left no written record (inference)"** (26 Oct; corrected 12 Nov). Shows the default was not only "oral societies leave no record" but "non-European voyages leave no record", even for societies with their own script; the project found three Bugis court diaries on 4 Nov.

**Mechanisms (Claude's own analysis of its drafts; inference, not a published finding):**
- *Inherited vocabulary.* Volcanology catalogues define "historical" as "written": GVP's dating category is "Historical Observations", and Specht & Torrence 2007 write "no recent records of eruptions". Claude carried "no record" over into "no observer".
- *Compression amplifies.* Hedged notes ("no European observer") became short chat summaries ("nobody there to see it"). The shorter the sentence, the stronger the erasure.
- *Researcher's reach mistaken for the region's.* "Watched" meant "we have read its records". Absence in our reading list became absence in the place.
- *A stock narrative.* "A remote volcano with no one to witness it" is a familiar story shape; the phrase was produced against facts in Claude's own context (the 1963 villagers), which points to pattern completion rather than inference from evidence.
- *Against the brief.* William's opening instructions (16 Oct) asked for "Indigenous testimony preserved in local-language or colonial records" and warned: "Do not infer that an eruption could not have occurred in a region merely because previous searches found no report." The same logic applies to people.
