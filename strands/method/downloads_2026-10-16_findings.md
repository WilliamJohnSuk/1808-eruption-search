- **New: the NOTA (p. 40), omitted from the transcription previously used.** Key sentence (image, 300 dpi):
  > "La Aurora Boreal, ese Oceano de fuego nadando sobre nuestras cabezas, barras, plumas zicxacs, fuentes exercitos, y todas las fantasmas que puede suministrar la imaginacion mas vivaz ¿no tienen mas de grande y de raro que una nube trasparente que le quita sus resplandores al Sol? … ¿no son mas terribles, mas extraordinarios, mas seductores que el fenomeno que ha dos meses observamos?"

## 9. Semanario 1808 Núms. 1–29 (archive.org JCB copy, semanariodelnuev01unse.pdf): pre-eruption baseline

Read at headline level throughout, closely for Núm. 7 and the monthly tables (subagent; key footnote re-checked by me on the image).
- **Caldas's daily Bogotá observatory tables run Jan–Jul 1808 only** (Tablas 1–7; PDF pp. 71, 105, 131, 165, 199, 245, 281): barometer, inside/outside thermometer (Réaumur), rain, lunar points, sky state. **No table for Aug–Dec 1808** appears anywhere in the volume, so there is no printed daily record for the onset on 11 Dec 1808.
- **Núm. 7 (14 Feb 1808), pp. 51–56: definition of "velo"** (PDF p. 78, footnote 3, verified on image): "Velo llama Lamark y los Meteorologistas á la nube igualmente extendida, como un velo, y que dexa ver al Sol, Luna, &c. como detras de una gaza." ("Lamarck and the meteorologists call a *veil* the evenly spread cloud, like a veil, through which Sun, Moon etc. are seen as if behind gauze.") Sky codes include "ve. esp." (thick veil), "ve. cla." (thin veil), "Cor. S./Cor. L." (solar/lunar corona).
  - **Why it matters:** "velo" was a routine cloud category in Caldas's log. In Jan–Jul 1808 it appears a handful of times a month, sometimes with a corona (Feb ~14: "S ve. cla. cor. S."). In the Feb 1809 note Caldas explicitly contrasts the Dec 1808 veil with this ordinary kind: "Han faltado las coronas … que se observan con tanta freqüencia … quando exîsten aquellas núbes que los meteorologistas conocen con el nombre de Velo", and stresses that it was constant day and night for weeks with dry weather. So, by the observer's own standards, the Dec 1808 veil was **not** his familiar cirrostratus velo: persistent, uniform, corona-less, with whitened sky and dimmed stars. This supports the stratospheric-aerosol reading (inference).
- Núm. 7 footnote (1), p. 53: Popayán rain records by Santiago Pérez de Valencia and Antonio Arboleda **from Oct 1807**; Mariano Larraondo about to start at Alegría near Quilichao. These series are the manuscript leads in §3 and §10.
- **9 May 1808 total lunar eclipse** seen from Bogotá (Núm. 21, p. 199): Moon "en partes naranjado, en otras roxiso" — a normal, fairly bright eclipse. Pre-eruption benchmark.
- **Late April 1808:** Bogotá overcast, rain and thunder 28–30 Apr; nothing on dust or a red sky (weak negative for the Lima 30 Apr event).
- Otherwise nothing in Jan–Jul 1808 on haze, odd sun, ash, quakes or eruptions. The volume also contains Núms. 30–53 (to 1 Jan 1809), read separately from the Banrep scans (§8).
