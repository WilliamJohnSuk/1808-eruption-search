## 1. Recent science, 2022-2026, not previously in the register

### 1.1 Meuer, Timmreck, Fang & Kadow 2024: machine learning puts the 1809 fingerprint in the northern extratropics [text]

*Communications Earth & Environment* 5:455, doi:10.1038/s43247-024-01617-y (open PDF read).

**Method.** A convolutional network was trained on climate-model simulations of eruptions of different size and latitude. It was then applied to 20CR-v3 reanalysis surface temperatures.

**Results for 1809.**
- "The 1809 eruption was identified as NHE with a score of 83%."
- Table 4 / Fig. 4b scores: NHE 83%, tropical 10%, SH extratropical 0%, unclassified 7%.
- A northern-hemisphere-only tree-ring model gives NHE "with a skill of 99%".
- The authors' reading: the eruption "most likely has its aerosol loading concentrated in the northern extra-tropical region", "or a tropical one but with an asymmetric forcing similar to the historic 1982 El Chichón eruption".

**Caveats (theirs).**
- The 1809 surface temperatures come from 20CR-v3's experimental period (1806-1835).
- The reanalysis has no volcanic forcing.
- The NH-only tree-ring model has lower skill (72%).

**Why it matters [inference].**
- The ice says bipolar and tropical-looking (deposition c. 55% north, B55, B66).
- The surface fingerprint says the cooling was concentrated in the north.
- Three readings fit both:
  - (a) a tropical eruption with a northern-skewed aerosol cloud, the El Chichón case;
  - (b) a second, northern extratropical eruption in 1809-10, the Yalcin et al. 2006 Eclipse Icefield tephra and the Hutchison abstract in 1.3;
  - (c) a classifier artefact of sparse 1809 data.
- A north-skewed cloud from a southern-tropical eruption would be unusual. So (a) and (b) slightly favour northern-tropical sources such as Cabalián or northern Sulawesi, or a second eruption. This is not evidence for any candidate.

### 1.2 Wilson et al. 2026: an ice-timing date for the eruption [text; calc]

Wilson, Mundo, Marshall, Reid, Sigl, Schmidt, Timmreck, Fang, Abernethy, Daux & Villalba. "Volcanically forced cooling over the last 600 years in Northern Patagonia", EGUsphere preprint, doi:10.5194/egusphere-2026-4785 (PDF read).

**Table A2: onset of the sulfur rise for the 1809 event (decimal years).**

| Core | Onset |
|---|---|
| D4i | 1809.5 |
| Summit composite | 1809.9 |
| NEEM-2011-S1 | 1809.7 |
| **Greenland mean** | **1809.7** |
| WDC | 1809.4 |
| Law Dome | 1809.7 |
| DIV2010 | 1809.5 |
| **Antarctica mean** | **1809.5** |

**Lag between eruption and onset of deposition**, from five dated eruptions (Krakatau, Cosigüina, Tambora, Parker, Huaynaputina):
- Greenland: 0.58 ± 0.20 yr (Parker excluded).
- Antarctica: 0.52 ± 0.17 yr.

**Their results.**
- "Projected mean eruption date": 1809.1 (Greenland) and 1809.0 (Antarctica).
- Recommended eruption age: "1808 [+1]".
- They cite the dust-veil evidence: "An eruption late in 1808 has previously been proposed based on observations of an atmospheric dust veil in South America."
- Table 2 row: "Unknown, lat 0.0, actual year 1808, model year 1809, NH flux 32.39, SH flux 25.40, VSSI 19.26 Tg S". Flux units are not stated; kg/km² is inferred.

**Claude's arithmetic [calc].**
- Greenland: 1809.12 = 14 Feb 1809 ± 73 days.
- Antarctica: 1808.98 = 25 Dec 1808 ± 62 days.
- Inverse-variance mean: **1809.04 = 15 Jan 1809 ± 47 days** (29 Nov 1808 to 3 Mar 1809).
- An eruption on 25 Nov 1808 is 0.9 SD earlier than this mean.
- This ignores the dating error of the onset itself, so the real spread is wider.

**Reading [inference].** The ice timing is independent of the Bogotá-based dates, consistent with them, and leans slightly later. It does not support an eruption much before mid-November 1808.

### 9.9 Dome C: a small positive Δ33S in the sample before the 1809 peak [data; calc]
- Re-read the Gautier et al. 2019 Dome C rows for 1790-1820 (PANGAEA 895169, text file) to test related mystery 6.1.
- The authors count six samples as their event 6.1-6.2 (1807.8-1818.1). The earliest is labelled "Ev 6.2-8", at 13.44 m and age 1807.8.
  - Its sulfate is 65.5 µg/kg with volcanic fraction fV = 0.00, so no excess over background by the authors' measure.
  - Its Δ33S is **+0.09 ± 0.02‰**.
  - The 1809.7 sample has 163.6 µg/kg, fV 0.60, Δ33S +0.19 ± 0.05‰.
- **Calculation:** +0.09 ± 0.02 is about 4.5 standard errors from zero [calc]. A non-zero Δ33S is the usual marker of sulfate oxidised above the ozone layer, which a tropospheric source does not give.
- **Reading:** with DF01's "unknown c. 1807" signal (Takahashi et al. 2022, also seen in other Antarctic cores), there are now two Antarctic hints of stratospheric sulfur a sample or so before the main 1809 peak. [inference] Three readings fit:
  - (a) Antarctic depth-age scales put the 1809 onset too early, and the eruption sits near the start of the Wilson et al. 2026 window (section 1.2).
  - (b) A separate, smaller stratospheric eruption occurred c. 1806-08.
  - (c) The 1807.8 sample's isotopes are mixed by diffusion or sampling across the event boundary. Samples are about 1.2 years long here.
- Reading (c) is the cautious default, because fV = 0. This is one sample from one core and Claude's reading of it, not the authors'.
- A test is in T48: compare the DF01, WAIS Divide and Dome C depth-age ties for 1807-1810.

## 11. Case file for related mystery 6.2: was 1809 two eruptions? [data; calc; inference]

This section gathers what the project already holds and what this search added. It is a framing note. It does not establish anything.

**For two (or more) eruptions:**
1. **Glass differs between north and south** [data: data/glass_ice_1809.csv; calc]. The Eclipse Icefield (Yukon) 1809 glass averages SiO2 64.9, FeOt 3.8, Na2O 6.6 (Yalcin et al. 2006). The South Pole and Dome C 1809 glass averages SiO2 59.4-60.3, FeOt 7.9-9.0, Na2O 4.1-4.3 (Palais data). The differences are about 2-5 times the within-population spread, for example FeOt at Eclipse vs Dome C by -5.2 spread units. South Pole and Dome C agree with each other (all within 0.5). Siple Dome's 1810 shards (trachyte, SiO2 63.4, K2O 4.9; Kurbatov et al. 2006) fit neither well. Yalcin et al. already argued from their glass for "a second volcanic eruption around 1809 in the Northern Hemisphere".
2. **Sulfur isotopes:** the signal is "weaker and temporally complex" and the cryptotephra falls into "distinct geochemical populations" (Hutchison et al. 2025, section 1.3).
3. **Climate fingerprint:** a neural-net classifier calls the 1809 pattern northern extratropical, with 83% probability (Meuer et al. 2024, section 1.1). Its own caveat applies: an asymmetric tropical forcing can look the same.
4. **Regional cold** is centred on the Gulf of Alaska and Wrangell-St Elias in 1810 (Leland et al. 2023, section 9.2). That is the same mountain range as the Eclipse glass.
5. **Precedent:** the 1450s pair was resolved as a northern extratropical eruption plus Kuwae (section 9.3).

**For a single tropical eruption (or against reading too much into the above):**
1. **A tropical veil was seen at 4°N in Bogotá on 11 Dec 1808** (Guevara-Murua et al. 2014). Its timing fits a low-latitude eruption in Nov-early Dec 1808.
2. **Deposition is bipolar**, with a positive Δ33S at Dome C at 1809.7 (Gautier et al. 2019): stratospheric sulfur reached Antarctica. A northern extratropical eruption alone cannot do that. So a tropical member is needed in any case.
3. **Onsets agree at both poles** [calc from Wilson et al. 2026]. Subtracting the mean lags gives an implied eruption date of 1809.12 from Greenland and 1808.98 from Antarctica. The difference of about 51 days has an uncertainty of about 96 days (1 sd), so the two poles do not need different eruptions.
4. **Small numbers:** the Eclipse glass is 10 analyses from one core, and Siple Dome is 8 shards. The South Pole and Dome C analyses are 1990s SEM-EDS on few particles.

**What it means for the main search [inference]:**
- The target of the documentary search is unchanged: a tropical eruption in Nov-early Dec 1808 that produced the Bogotá veil and the Antarctic glass. The iron-rich andesite points to the candidate list as it stands.
- A northern companion in 1809-10 is plausible and would explain the Yukon glass and the Gulf of Alaska cold. It would have its own documentary test in Kamchatka, the Kurils and the Aleutians, covered in north_pacific_round2.md.
- If the companion is real, the hemispheric sulfate ratio used for the withdrawn latitude band (B66) would be contaminated. That is a second reason the withdrawal was right.

**Cheapest next steps:**
- Wait for the Hutchison group's paper; their glass data are the decisive evidence (U90-U92).
- Read the northern day-books that already exist. Three leads from asian_arabic_round4.md were never put on the hand-items list, and are now listed:
  - the Sendai-domain guard diary on Kunashiri, 1808, possibly running into 1809 (U214);
  - Mamiya Rinzō's Sakhalin and Amur journals, winter 1808-09 (U215);
  - the Ryukyu court diary of Ie Ueekata, which probably covers Jan 1809 (U216).
- In the meantime, run the Eclipse glass against GEOROC North Pacific arcs (6.8).

- **U155 resolved by inference: the "5 Dec." date belongs to 1810, not 1808** [OCR word positions from the archive.org in-item search; the reading is inference]. On NTNI vols 80-82 p. 327, the archive.org search returns word boxes (page 1786 x 2868 px) that show the Java list is running text across full-width lines, not columns:
  - Line at y 2156-2211: "1806. Loemadjang. Uitbarsting van de Lamongan." (x 305-1451), then "1808" at x 1494-1620, the end of the line.
  - Line at y 2221-2281: "1810" at x 465-591 (after "5 Dec." at the line start), then Rembang at x 1285-1529 and Solo at x 1567-1680.
  - Line at y 2298-2352: Banjoewangi at x 306-642, then "22/11 1815" and Soerabaja at x 944-1193.

  So the entry reads "1808, 5 Dec. 1810, 24/3 1814, 10/3 1815 Rembang, Solo, Banjoewangi": a run of shocks felt at those places. The page writes day before year everywhere else ("22/11 1815", "23/1 '17", "15/12 1830"), so "5 Dec." most likely goes with 1810. That matches Wichmann's catalogue (B49), which has an undated 1808 Bali/Banyuwangi quake and a Batavia shock on 5 Dec 1810. **So the source gives no dated Java event on 5 Dec 1808.** The page image would confirm this but is no longer important. U155 yield is lowered to L; its status is unchanged.
- **Where the year "1808" for Agung comes from** [OCR snippet; the chain is inference]. The index of Lyell's *Principles of Geology*, 2nd edition, vol. 1 (1832; Lyell Virtual Geography University copy on archive.org) has "Carang Assam volcano in eruption about 1808, 465". This is the earliest explicit "1808" found so far. Raffles (1815, printed in the 1835 *Memoir*) says only "about seven years ago", and Lyell's text repeats that wording. Later catalogues may have taken their date from this arithmetic (1815 minus 7) rather than from a dated record. Only *Babad Gumi* (U163, U232) or the Bali reports to Daendels (U222) could give an independent date.

Checks run after the screen [text]:
- Makian: Wikipedia, citing GVP, dates the eruption to 22 Sep 1760, VEI 4. It gives "about three thousand" deaths, with no source for that figure. A Sep 1760 tropical eruption fits sulfate arriving in 1761, Pang's dark eclipse of May 1761, and an ice-year label of 1762. That makes 6.3 the most tractable side mystery: a named candidate, a date and VOC records.
- The Wikipedia list "Unexplained volcanic eruption" (2026 snapshot) covers only 1809 and 1831 for 1700-1900, and 1831 is now solved. The events of 1762, 1770, 1786, 1796-97, 1821 and 1846 are not presented publicly as mysteries. They would make good community-layer issues [suggestion].

- **6.2 (a second, northern 1809 eruption), GVP check** [calc]: north of 30°N, GVP 4.6.7 starts only four eruptions in 1808-1810: Etna 1809 (VEI 2), Bandai 1808 (VEI 2), Zao 1809 (VEI 2) and São Jorge, Azores 1808 (VEI 1). So if a northern extratropical eruption contributed to the 1809 signal, as Meuer 2024 and the Eclipse Icefield trachyte suggest, it is unrecorded too [inference]. That would make the North Pacific a second blind spot, and the Russian-American Company and Kamchatka records the written test (north_pacific_round2.md).
- 1821 widened [calc]: with the VEI cut-off lowered, GVP has only small (VEI 1-2) southern eruptions for 1819-1822: Piton de la Fournaise 1820-21, San José and Llaima 1822, Villarrica 1822, Antuco 1820, Fueguino 1820, and Saunders and Zavodovski (South Sandwich) 1819. None is a likely stratospheric source. An unrecorded South Sandwich or Southern Ocean eruption, the southern analogue of the Melanesian gap, is the natural hypothesis [inference].
