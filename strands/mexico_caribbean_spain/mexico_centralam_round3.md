  - Translation: "According to tradition, from 25 March 1806, when there was a strong vertical-shock earthquake known as 'the Earthquake of Annunciation Day', and for two years from then, the Volcano remained in continual agitation. Lavas rolled without interruption to the foot of the mountain, and the city of Colima was lit every night by the pale and terrible glow of the Volcano's fire."
  - Dating: effusive activity from 25 Mar 1806 to about Mar 1808 ("por término de dos años"). This rests on local tradition, not on a dated contemporary document.
- **p. 530 (another article in the same volume, quoting the same tradition)** adds a critical comment: "Esta descripción es exagera- da y más poética que verídica, pero dá alguna idea de la actividad del volcán en aquella época." Translation: "This description is exaggerated and more poetic than truthful, but it gives some idea of the volcano's activity at that time."
  - The text just before it, still on p. 530: "…sa y muy obscura, la que después aclaró y luego quedó el volcán sofocado como lo había estado muchos días antes del terremoto." This appears to refer to the 1806 earthquake, which was followed by a dark cloud that later cleared.
- **The next dated event in the catalogue is 1818.** The "1818" hits are pp. 530, 547, 549 and 554. "1809" has no Colima hit. "1808" appears only in the p. 530 summary ("1806 1808", OCR-garbled).
- Page 549 continues: "En la noche del 15 de febrero de 1818, el Volcán hizo una fuerte erupción cubriendo á todos los pueblos con una gran lluvia de arena y ceniza, las que alcanzaron á caer en la capital de Guadalajara, Guanajuato, San Luis y Zacatecas…"
- Page 547, "Artículo tomado de un Calendario", describes the 1818 eruption: a dull noise like 16-pounder artillery heard from Zapotlán, a dense cloud that covered the moon, flames, and a powder ("polvillo") that reached "San Luis Potosí y Querétaro en mucha abundancia, y aún á México en poca cantidad".
- **Interpretation**
  - Arreola records Colima in Mar 1806 – ca. Mar 1808 as a lava-effusive phase lit at night, with **no ashfall, detonation or regional sky effect reported**. Nothing is recorded for Nov 1808 – Jun 1809.
  - By contrast, the 1818 VEI-4 event left ashfall, artillery-like noise and darkness in the record that reached Mexico City. A comparable regional event at Colima in 1808–09 would probably have been recorded the same way.
  - So **Colima is unlikely as the source of the Dec 1808 veil**, and it is in any case incompatible with a bipolar signal.
  - Verification level: OCR text via the IA in-book search API, not a page image. The p. 530 summary table was not read in full.
