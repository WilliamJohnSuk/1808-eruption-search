### Philippines — Taal 1808
- Taal had a significant phreatomagmatic eruption in 1808. Wikipedia (Taal Volcano, eruption table): "February 16, 1808 to April 16, 1808 ... Commenced and ended ± 15 days of dates given. Ash to depth 84 cm". Narrative: "Not until March 1808 did another big eruption occur" citing Knittel 1999 summary of Saderra Masó (1911).
- PHIVOLCS WOVOdat eruption history (https://wovodat.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/volcan/erupt-history?page=5): 1808, Main Crater, "Phreatomagmatic, moderate"; "84 cm Tephra fall deposits at TVI"; quotes Saderra Maso 1911 p.11; source Delos Reyes et al. 2018.
- Verification: secondary only (OCR text via WebFetch). Timing (Feb–Apr 1808) is ~8–10 months BEFORE the Dec 1808 veil; scale (moderate, local tephra) is far too small for the bipolar sulfate signal. Does not fit.

### Nicaragua — Cosigüina "1809" (GVP uncertain eruption, 28 Mar 1809, VEI 2)
- Earliest source found for an 1809 eruption: Alexander Caldcleugh, "Some Account of the Volcanic Eruption of Cosegüina in the Bay of Fonseca...", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. London 126 (1836), JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/108021 ; OCR text via https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-108021/ (reached; OCR via WebFetch, no page numbers):
  - "two eruptions are on record, viz. those of the years 1709, and 1809." ... and on the 1835 onset: "On the 19th of January, after twenty-six years of repose, a slight noise attended with smoke ..."
  - Caldcleugh's information came from official reports sent from towns to the Central American government and from "intelligent friends"; he does not say where the 1709/1809 dates come from. Writing date 1835–36; publication 1836. Secondary/hearsay for 1809 (26 years after the fact).
- The specific day "28 March 1809" in GVP: primary source NOT traced. Not in Caldcleugh (no day given). Searches for "28 de marzo de 1809" + volcán/temblor gave nothing relevant. Candidate intermediate sources not reached: Montessus de Ballore (1884, 1888), Sapper (1913/1925), Feldman, Mountains of Fire (1993). GVP lists it as "Uncertain".
- Geology argues against anything large: Scott et al. 2006 (GSA Spec. Pap. 412:167–187) find the penultimate eruption before 1835 was "a few centuries" earlier (perhaps 15th c.); Hradecký & Rapprich 2008 (Rev. Geol. Am. Central 38:65–79) tephra-stratigraphy has sequences assigned to ~1500, 1609?, 1709 — no 1809 deposit. Self, Rampino & Carr 1989 do not mention 1809.
- Assessment: even if real, a March 1809 Cosigüina event is (a) 3–4 months after the 11 Dec 1808 Bogotá veil onset, (b) VEI ≤2 and leaving no deposit — cannot be the 1808/09 sulfate source. Could at most be a small local event; worth a check in Gazeta de Guatemala Apr–May 1809 issues (vol. XI–XII) if they can be reached.

### Mexico — Colima 1806–1809
- GVP (2018 snapshot): Colima confirmed eruption 1806–1809, VEI 2. VolcanoDiscovery list: "1795, 1804, 1806-1809, 1818, 1819". Crummy et al. 2019 (J. Appl. Volcanol. 8:3) list 1806 as one of nine sub-Plinian/Plinian events (citing Medina-Martínez 1983; De la Cruz-Reyna 1993).
- Figueroa García, "Cartografía geomorfológica del volcán de Colima" (tesis, UNAM FFyL, 2016), p. 5 (https://ru.dgb.unam.mx/bitstream/20.500.14330/TES01000743374/3/0743374.pdf, OCR via WebFetch): "Antes de 1818, se reconoce a la emisión de flujos de lava como la principal actividad que desarrolló el volcán (1807 y 1808)" ... intense fumarolic activity with sporadic explosions, "la más importante ocurrió en 1809 (Arreola, 1915)".
- Arreola, J.M. (1915), catalogue of old Colima eruptions in Memorias de la Sociedad Científica "Antonio Alzate" — not reached; that is the route to the primary sources (probably parish/Zapotlán/Colima chronicles).
- Assessment: effusive lava + minor explosions (VEI 2) at 19.5°N; Northern-Hemisphere location and small size make it incompatible with a bipolar large sulfate signal and with Caldas's veil. Possible contributor of local haze only in W Mexico; no evidence of that found.

### Spain — San Fernando (Cádiz) Royal Naval Observatory: "calimoso" sky, Jan–Mar 1809 (STRONGEST SPANISH LEAD, but weak)
- Observer: Real Observatorio de la Armada (San Fernando, Cádiz), routine log; sky state from 1805 summarised in one word per day.
- Repository: Real Instituto y Observatorio de la Armada (ROA), San Fernando; logbook catalogue no. AH1080 (per authors). Digitised dataset: Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.7104289 / record 7104288 (file SF1799-1813_1.txt, 998 kB) — I could NOT download it (Zenodo file/API fetch failed/robots).
- Secondary: Bravo-Paredes, Gallego, Trigo & Vaquero, "Earliest meteorological readings in San Fernando (Cádiz, Spain)", Clim. Past Discuss. preprint cp-2022-75 (2022), pp. 16–18 (https://cp.copernicus.org/preprints/cp-2022-75/cp-2022-75.pdf; OCR via WebFetch):
  - "The term “calimoso” (haze sky in English) is found in some days of January and February of the year 1809" ... "Specifically, the days are: 29/01-02/02, 07-08/02 and 27-28/02. The term “calimoso” also appears on March 21st."
  - Event dates: 29 Jan–2 Feb, 7–8 Feb, 27–28 Feb, 21 Mar 1809. No December 1808 calimoso days are mentioned.
- In peer review the volcanic interpretation was called "really speculative" (Referee 2) and challenged by M. Chenoweth (CC1). The authors agreed to remove it ("the best option is remove it", AC2). The final paper (Clim. Past 19:1397–1410, 2023) does not discuss calimoso or the eruption. It attributes the warm early-1809 winter to a SW-European regional pattern.
- Alternatives: "calima" or "calimoso" is a routine Cádiz word for haze from Saharan dust, levante or humidity haze. Without a multi-year baseline of calimoso frequency this proves nothing. Next step: download the Zenodo file and count calimoso days by month for 1805–1813, including Dec 1808.
- Independence: fully independent of Caldas and Unanue. Verification: published secondary text (OCR) only; I did not see the logbook.

### Mexico — weather 1808–1810 (frosts/drought)
- The 1809–1810 agricultural crisis has these dates: rains were late in May 1809, with "varias heladas y granizadas" in the second half of 1809 (Aug–Sept). Central and northern New Spain were worst hit (Guanajuato, México, Michoacán, SLP, Querétaro). Sources: Alberola Romá & Arrioja, "Extremismo climático durante la segunda mitad del siglo XVIII" (divulgameteo PDF), and Molina del Villar, Relaciones 121 (2010) pp. 110–116 (scielo.org.mx). Both rest on Florescano & San Vicente, Fuentes para la historia de la crisis agrícola (1809–1811) (UNAM 1985) and AGN Intendentes vols. 63, 73. Tenango del Valle: "nevadas y 'aguas extemporáneas' de marzo y abril [1809]" ruined maize (AGN Intendentes 73 exp. 9 via Florescano).
- Assessment: the timing (spring–autumn 1809) fits a post-eruption cool/dry year, but ENSO-type drought is a full alternative. The crisis contains no direct optical/haze observations. It is independent of Caldas and Unanue.
