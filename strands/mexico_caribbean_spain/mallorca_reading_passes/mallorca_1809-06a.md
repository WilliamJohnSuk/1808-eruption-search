## Other notes
- **Tables reused from the previous day.** The printer seems to have repeated the previous day's type on several days. The numbers (and often the sunrise text too) are identical in these pairs: 05-29 and 05-30 (only the 5 pm wind differs); 06-01 and 06-02 (fully identical); 06-04 and 06-05 (identical apart from SO./SSO.); 06-10 and 06-11 (fully identical). On 06-12 the numbers are the same again but the winds differ. Treat the second issue of each pair as doubtful.
- The 7 am barometer on 05-29 and 05-30 reads "27 p. 1½ l.", against 28p2l at noon. This is probably a misprint for 28 p.
- The 06-05 noon wind is printed "S S O." or similar (SSO.). The 06-06 noon entry looks like "O O." The 06-14 evening entry "FSO." is probably a misprint for ESO. or SSO.
- The thermometer rises sharply on 05-30, to 17-18½ R (about 21-23 C), with NE/E winds and pressure up to 28p3l. After that it settles at 15-17 R.
- The highest pressure in the range is 28p5l, at 7 am on 06-13 (from the 06-14 issue).
- The sunrise and sunset text is normal throughout.
- 1809-06-10, p. 651, Fonz 19 de Mayo: "Si el tiempo me lo permitiera daria á V. S. un detalle mas individual". Here "tiempo" means time available, not weather.
- 1809-06-13, p. 662, Lisboa 11 de Mayo: an English admiral will send news "si el tiempo lo permitiere" ("weather permitting"). This is a routine phrase.
- 1809-05-31, p. 612, and 1809-06-01: there is an epidemic in Victor's French army facing Cuesta, "extendiendo hasta los caballos" (spreading even to the horses). The cause is not given.
