### 2.2 Alexander Beatson, *Tracts relative to the island of St. Helena* (London 1816): a new lead with weather data
- IA `tractsrelativeto00beat`: https://archive.org/details/tractsrelativeto00beat. Other copies: `tractsrelativet00beatgoog`, `tractsrelativet01beatgoog`.
- Beatson was Governor from 4 Jul 1808 and kept dated farm-experiment journals and a rain register at Plantation House.
- p. 60 and pp. 204–205: a comparison of six years (1806–1811) of "days of heavy rains, and fine showers". OCR on p. 60 gives "1808 - - - 58" and "1809 - - 71", and "In 1806 heavy rains and fine showers, 92 days". p. 204 carries monthly headings "Heavy Rains." / "Fine Showers." for each year.
- pp. 200–202: "Fall of heavy Rains / Effects of heavy Rains". The extracts include "1809, March 4th. — Heavy rains that fell in the short space of one hour ..." (p. 202) and "the rains set in on the 1st February" (p. 201, year not seen in snippet).
- Dated agricultural entries for Jan–May 1809 are on pp. 162–190 and p. 260: barley sown 13 Sep 1808 and first cutting 19 Jan 1809 (p. 190); barley weighed 14 Mar 1809 (p. 178); potatoes planted 12 Feb 1809 (p. 260).
- No hits for "haze" or "sun" in the St Helena context. "atmosphere" hits are agronomic. p. 97 ("atmosphere most uncommonly clear") describes the Cape winter generally.
- **Value:** a tropical South Atlantic (16°S) monthly rain-day register for 1808–09, and the only instrumental-type record found for St Helena in the window. It does not record optics.
- Original papers: St Helena consultations and the Governor's letters are in BL IOR/G/32 (St Helena Factory Records) and in the St Helena Government Archives, Jamestown. Not searched at item level.

## 3. Mauritius (Île de France) and Réunion

### 3.1 NEW high-priority lead: Matthew Flinders, *Private Journal*, 17 Dec 1803 to 8 Jul 1814
- **Observer:** Flinders, a navigator and magnetic and barometer researcher. He was a prisoner on parole at "Wilhems Plains", in the uplands of Mauritius (~20.3°S 57.5°E, ~400 m), for the whole of Dec 1808 to mid-1809. He kept a daily journal there.
- **Repository:** State Library of New South Wales, Mitchell Library, **Safe 1/58**. Catalogued as "Digitised" (digital order no. Album ID 823218), with a full English transcription.
  - Catalogue: https://devfeature-collection.sl.nsw.gov.au/record/9NaAQqgY (opened from here). The public mirror https://collection.sl.nsw.gov.au/record/9NaAQqgY failed to fetch.
  - Transcript: http://acms.sl.nsw.gov.au/_transcript/2013/D17745/a007.html. Opened from here, but only the first ~122,000 characters (17 Dec 1803 to Nov 1804) were returned. Sibling files (a008, a010) are robots-blocked. **Not read for Dec 1808 to Jun 1809.**
- **Printed editions:**
  - *Matthew Flinders private journal, 1803–1814, the Mitchell Library manuscript* (Genesis, 1986; facsimile, 366 pp.), RMG library: https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/library/rmgl-123630
  - Anthony J. Brown and Gillian Dooley (eds.), *Matthew Flinders: Private Journal from 17 December 1803 at Isle de France to 10 July 1814 at London* (Friends of the State Library of South Australia, 2005). Record: https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/matthew-flinders-private-journal-from-17-december-1803-at-isle-de/
- **What to check (human):** the daily entries for 1 Dec 1808 to 30 Jun 1809. Look for haze, a pale or red sun, coloured twilights, unusual sunsets, or "smoky" air. Flinders also observed the satellites of Jupiter and made magnetic observations, so watch for remarks on poor seeing or transparency. Note that Piton de la Fournaise (Réunion, ~200 km WSW) erupted 17 Jul to 8 Aug 1809, so any haze after mid-July 1809 has a local source.
- **Independence:** fully independent of Caldas and Unanue.
- **Alternatives:** cane-field and forest burning, local Réunion eruptions, cyclone-season cloud.
- **Already checked here (negative):** Flinders's letters on the RMG "Flinders Papers" site (https://flinders.rmg.co.uk/AllDocumentsd2c7.html?browseBy=Date):
