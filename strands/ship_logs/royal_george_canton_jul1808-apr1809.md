**What is normal here:** in Nov–Feb, Canton and the Pearl River have the north-east monsoon. Expect fair, dry weather, broken by north-easterly surges that bring fresh or strong gales, cloud and some rain (as on 5 Dec, 15 Dec, 24–25 Dec, 4–5 Jan, 3–4 Feb and 25–28 Feb). Haze is common in winter. Warm, sultry spells between surges are normal, especially as February ends. Everything recorded in this batch fits that pattern. The one "hazy" entry (15 Dec) is weak evidence: it is a single word, tied to a gale, from someone who almost never remarks on the sky.

**How useful this source is:** low. The log is a port book. Weather is one formulaic clause a day, often "as yesterday". There are no instrument readings and nothing about the sky. Not seeing optical phenomena here is not evidence that none occurred.

The hand is a clear copperplate and the scans are good. All 15 pages read without great difficulty. Points of doubt are marked [?]. None of these doubts touches a weather remark that matters. I checked the "hazy" line of 15 Dec in a crop.

# Royal George (IOR/L/MAR/B/17L), images 0206–0220: transcription

**Date coverage is not what the brief expected.** These 15 images do **not** cover winter 1808–09 at Whampoa. They run continuously from **Thursday 2 March 1809** (last harbour days in the Canton River) through **Wednesday 5 April 1809** (sailing from Penang). The Dec 1808 to Feb 1809 Whampoa entries must be on images before 0206.

Layout: even-numbered images are left-hand pages headed "Ship Royal George from China". Odd-numbered images are right-hand pages headed "towards England" and carry circled folio numbers (101–107). The sea log runs from noon to noon in ship's days. Each day has an hourly column (courses, K/F, winds and short remarks) and a right-hand remarks column. In this period the K/F column is the speed in knots and fathoms (tenths), not depth. There is no barometer or thermometer entry on any page (the "Bar." and "Ther." boxes are blank).

Abbreviations: Comm. = the Commodore's ship (position bearing). Sd/Gd = soundings, ground. Tkd = tacked. Vble = variable. Fms = fathoms.

**Weather words that do occur:** cloudy, rain, much rain, squally, lightning ("Rain & Lightning" 13 Mar; "Lightning @ SW" 16 Mar; "squally, rain thunder & lightning" 30 Mar), clear weather (10 Mar), fair, fine, pleasant, hot sultry.

**Red skies, Jan to Feb 1809:** not testable from these images, which start on 2 March 1809. There are no sky-colour remarks in March either.

**What is normal for the season:** at Canton in early March the NE monsoon is weakening, with overcast skies and drizzle common, as in "Moderate and cloudy, with rain" on 3 and 4 Mar. The South China Sea run on 8 to 12 Mar had a "fresh monsoon", clear or cloudy skies and a NE swell. The equatorial Malacca Strait in late March had squalls, rain, thunder and lightning, sultry heat, calms and land and sea breezes. Everything recorded fits this pattern.
