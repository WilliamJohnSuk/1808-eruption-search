**Every other indicator: none found.** In 0155–0174 there is no ashfall, dust or darkness by day; no report of guns or explosions heard (the only guns are the ship's own salutes and gun exercise); no pumice; no glow at night or new island; no unusual sea withdrawal or wave; no discoloured sea; no sulphur smell; no comment on the colour of the sun, moon or sky, halos or twilights; and no earthquake.

Other weather notes are routine:
- "Hot sultry Weather" and "excessive Hot Weather" (19 May, 28 May, 29 June, 1 July, 3–6 July, 11–12 July, 23 July, 25 July, 28 July, 1 Aug).
- "very heavy Dew" (28 May, 4–6 July, 16 July, 24 July).
- Strong NW gales with much sea (3–4 June, 17 June, 22 June, 18 July "violent Squalls in the Night").
- "unsettled Weather" (31 July).

Noon thermometer readings run 85–97½°F.

**Seasonal context.** The haze comes with NW winds, often strong. It is worst from late May to early June and again in late July. Dust haze in the Gulf at that time of year is normal. This is the summer shamal season, when strong NW winds over Iraq, Kuwait and Iran lift dust that often hides coasts from a few leagues out. Heavy night dew and "sultry" weather are also typical of Gulf summers. Nothing in these pages calls for a volcanic explanation. The haze records are consistent with ordinary shamal dust. The pages also come before the eruption window that is usually proposed (late 1808, with the sulphate signal in ice cores dated to around Nov–Dec 1808). That makes them a useful pre-eruption baseline: in this region and season, "hazy" in this log is expected background.

**Hard-to-read pages.** The sea pages (0155–0164 and 0172–0174) are clear, and only some bearings and proper names are uncertain. The in-port pages (0165–0171) are in a fainter, smaller hand that runs across the ruled columns, and I read them from zoomed crops. The weather phrases there are fairly secure. Some names and numbers are not, for example "Toussaint", "Ezling" and the regiment number. On 0168, 0169 and 0171 I am unsure whether the wind word is "Easterly" or "Southerly".
