Weather items worth keeping. None is volcanic; the readings are Claude's:
- Cyrus 20 Mar-4 Apr 1809, 2.5-5.5S, 84-89W: two weeks of heavy rain and dark weather, "very much like the bay of Panama". This is a warm-water (El Niño-like) pattern.
- Cyrus 18-23 Aug 1809, Galápagos: "on account of the extreme heat and drought that has attended these Islands for two seasons past the whole vegetation appears to be dried up, and the surface of the ground parched so as to resemble a blacksmith's cinder". The tortoises gone from 1804 ground. "Cinder" describes parched lava ground, not ash.
- Cyrus 11-15 Mar 1810, St Helena: surf of "uncommon violence" washed away the pier paths and stores. These are the known St Helena rollers (swell lasting days), not a tsunami.
- Sukey 18 Apr 1810, Charles I. (Floreana), 1 league off: "saw a fire on the Mountain". Floreana has no historical eruption, and people lived ashore. Most likely a vegetation or camp fire in the drought. No smoke or fire was noted at Albemarle or Narborough by either Cyrus (May-Aug 1809) or Sukey (Apr-Jun 1810).
- Fame 11-17 Mar 1810, 18-7N, 21-22W: "the water still very much discoloured as it has been for a number of days", "smoaky hazy weather as usual". This is harmattan-season Saharan dust, the same zone as Cyrus's "smoaky" weather in Oct 1808.
- Fame 23-24 May 1810: passed Île St Paul (S Indian Ocean volcano) with no remark of smoke.

**What this strand shows.** American and London whalers left a dense daily record across the S Atlantic, SE Pacific and SE Africa from Sep 1808 to mid-1810, and they saw nothing unusual in the sky or sea. That is a weak negative for any large eruption plume, ash or pumice reaching those waters. It says nothing about the western Pacific or Indonesia, where no American log in this set was present.

## 5. Other nations' whaling records, Sep 1808 to mid-1810 (27 Oct, asked by William; catalogue and web search only)
- **British South Sea whalers (London, Enderby and others).** The biggest fleet after the American one. The BSWF voyage database (A. G. E. Jones, D. Chatwin; https://whalinghistory.org/bv/voyages; 2,500+ voyages 1775-1859) gives voyage dates, grounds and sources. Its viewer is interactive and was not queried here. Of the British ships in this window, the March 2022 list of surviving British logs and journals (https://www.britishwhaling.org/uploads/1/0/9/5/109542063/logbooks_journals___first_hand_accounts_british_ss_whalers_march_2022.pdf, read as text) names only four for 1808-1811:
  - Cyrus 1808-10: already read, §3.
  - Argo 1808-11, Charles Gardner, log on microfilm PMB 237, with extracts in Stackpole, *Whales and Destiny* pp. 293-297. Argo was at the Galápagos with Cyrus in May 1809, so it is the same SE Pacific waters.
  - Perseverance 1809-11, James Choyce, *The Log of a Jack Tar* (1891), a printed memoir. The ship was off Peru in Nov 1809 (spoken by Cyrus).
  - Santa Anna 1810-11, George Miller, *A Trip to Sea from 1810 to 1815* (London 1854). The ship was wrecked off New Guinea in 1811. The list says it is on Google Books, which is blocked here; not found on archive.org. It is the only British first-hand account that reaches the New Guinea zone, but only from 1811, which is late.
  - No archive.org copy of Choyce was found in a quick search.
- **Colonial Sydney whalers and sealers.**
  - Small vessels sealed in Bass Strait, New Zealand and the subantarctic in 1808-10. For example, Hasselborough's Perseverance found Campbell Island (Jan 1810) and Macquarie Island (Jul 1810); that is from general knowledge, not checked here. These are Southern Ocean waters, one of the unwatched zones.
  - Almost no logs survive. Their reports went into the Sydney Gazette shipping news, which still needs a Trove API key.
  - The whalinghistory.org Australian Colonial Whaling database (https://whalinghistory.org/tv/reports/) mainly covers later decades. Not queried.
- **Dutch, French and other European whaling.** Near nil in 1808-10 because of the war and the British blockade. There are no known southern voyages, and the northern (Greenland/Davis Strait) Dutch and German fleets had stopped. British Greenland whalers (Hull, Whitby) did sail. They worked the Arctic in Apr-Aug only, which is far from a tropical source, though their logs could record the 1809 summer conditions. Not searched.
- **Japanese whaling.** These were coastal net-whaling groups (kujiragumi) at Taiji (Kii), Tosa, Ikitsuki/Hirado and Gotō (Hizen), and Nagato. The surviving records are catch ledgers, accounts and group histories in Japanese, mostly in print or local archives. They are not ship logs, but they are dated day by day in the winter season (about Dec-Mar). A Kii, Tosa or Kyushu ledger for winter 1808-09 could note odd skies, a tsunami or ash, and Taiji is on the Kii coast where Tsuji 2025 places the small 4 Dec 1808 tsunami. Not found online in this search. This is a lead for a Japanese-reading specialist (suggestion).
- **What needs human hands:** nothing new. The Trove key (already listed) would open the Sydney sealing reports. Library or ILL options, all optional: Stackpole *Whales and Destiny* pp. 293-297 (Argo); Choyce *Log of a Jack Tar*; Miller *A Trip to Sea* (in Google Books, which he can open in a browser).
