# Weather and sky records from the slave trade and colonial operations, Sep 1808 to mid-1810 (22 Nov 2026)

Thread: "Search slave-trade and company records for 1808-09 weather". William asked (22 Nov 2026, 22:45 UTC) whether anything survives from slave-trading ships, from colonial operations like the Hudson's Bay Company, from the Spanish in South America, and from the Portuguese in southern Africa, and whether any of them wrote down weather observations.

**Short answer.** We found no report of haze, a dim or coloured sun, ashfall or unusual darkness in the records read here (the printed Church Missionary Society journals from the Rio Pongas, Feb 1809; catalogue descriptions of about 60 Admiralty, prize-court and Africa Company files). The useful result is a map of where the daily records are. The most promising single item is the **captain's log of HMS *Blossom*, 7 Oct 1808 to 21 May 1809**, which escorted an Africa convoy to Cape Coast and Príncipe in the Gulf of Guinea through the months the veil should have crossed the tropics (U237). A second structural result: after the Portuguese court reached Rio de Janeiro in March 1808, the governors of Angola and Mozambique wrote to Rio, not Lisbon, so their 1808-10 letters are mostly in Rio de Janeiro archives, not in the Arquivo Histórico Ultramarino (section 4, Claude's inference supported by a count).

## 1. What a slave-ship record could and could not tell us

- After 1 May 1807 (British) and 1 Jan 1808 (United States) the Atlantic trade from Britain and the USA was illegal, so in the window it was carried on mainly by Portuguese-Brazilian ships (Angola, Benguela and Mozambique to Rio, Bahia and Pernambuco) and Spanish-Cuban ships, with some ships under false flags [background]. Logs from these ships rarely survive, because they were working documents of private owners [background].
- Logs do survive when a ship was **captured**: ship's papers, sometimes including logbooks, went to the prize court. Captures in our window went to the new Vice-Admiralty Court at Sierra Leone (from June 1808) or to Caribbean courts [catalogue].
- The routes matter for the veil. Mozambique-Brazil ships rounded the Cape at about 34-35°S; Angola-Brazil ships crossed the South Atlantic at 5-15°S; ships from the Upper Guinea coast (Rio Pongas, Rio Nunez) to Cuba crossed the tropical North Atlantic [background].

### 2.2 Prize-court records of captured slave ships

- **HCA 49/97**, "Vessels, cargoes and slaves proceeded against in the Court of the Vice-Admiralty at Sierra Leone between June 1808 and March 1817", and **HCA 49/97A**, the court's entry book of captures since 1808, with the numbers of enslaved people taken off each ship [catalogue]. These give the names and dates of every ship brought into Freetown, which is the first step to finding any surviving ship's papers. U239.
- Item-level HCA 42 and HCA 45 appeal descriptions searched for 1808-1811 ("slaves", "Angola", "Mozambique", "Benguela", "Bahia", "Havana"): 25 slave-ship cases found, but **every capture in them was made before the window** (Dec 1805 to Feb 1808), except two Sierra Leone captures of Aug 1810 (*Pez Volador*, Spanish, 82 enslaved people, HCA 42/485/961; *Santiago*, Spanish, 57 enslaved people, HCA 42/500/1117), just after it [catalogue]. So the High Court of Appeals papers are not a route to logs for Dec 1808-mid 1810.
- Prize Papers Portal (portal.prizepapers.de): the digitised ship papers so far are mostly from the 1740s and 1770s; for 1808-10 only the printed case books HCA 45/54-57 are online [checked in the portal 22 Nov 2026]. Not useful yet.

### 2.3 Company of Merchants trading to Africa (Cape Coast Castle and the Gold Coast forts)

- TNA T 70/1588-1591 are catalogued item by item for 1808-10: letters, acts of council, rank lists and reports on the "state and condition" of Cape Coast Castle (Torrane, 23 Dec 1809, T 70/1589/49) and Anomabu fort (13 Dec 1809, T 70/1589/48; 30 Jun 1810, T 70/1590/31) [catalogue]. Searches of these descriptions for rain, season, sickness and weather found nothing; the descriptions are short, so this is not a negative for the documents themselves. T 70/809-814 (1808-10) are ledgers, not journals [catalogue]. U240.
- T 70/1589/34 (14-20 Oct 1809): papers on an "insurrection and conspiracy" at Bunce Island and the trial that followed [catalogue]. Not about weather; noted because the separate thread on social context may want it.

### 2.4 Sierra Leone colony and the Rio Pongas missionaries

- **Church Missionary Society, *Proceedings* vol. 3 (1810-1812), Appendix I, "Extracts from the Letters and Journals of the Missionaries on the Western Coast of Africa, from July, 1808"** (archive.org per_proceedings-church-missionary-soc-for-africa-a_1810-1812_3, pp. 87-100) [OCR, read in full for Jul 1808-Mar 1810]. The German missionaries Butscher, Renner, Nylander and Wenzel lived at Bashia on the Rio Pongas (about 10°N, in Susu country, now Guinea) and at Freetown. Dated extracts: Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct 1808; Feb 1809; May 1809; Dec 1809; Mar 1810.
  - Aug 1808 (rainy season): roads flooded, "it had rained very much for several days before" (Butscher, p. 89) [OCR]. Normal for the season [inference].
  - **Feb 1809**, the month after the ice-core onset, Butscher camped on the north bank of the Rio Pongas in a shelter built "from the burning heat of the day, and the cold dews of the night" (p. 94); Susu farmers nearby were preparing their rice farms for sowing and were "well furnished with rice" (p. 95) [OCR]. This is ordinary dry-season weather and a good harvest [inference]. **We found no report of haze, a dim sun or any sky remark in these extracts.**
  - Context worth keeping: Mongé Hate, a Susu chief, gave his son's age as fourteen rice farms ("lugards"), one made each year, and Butscher adds that "Some count their ages by the rainy seasons" (p. 91) [OCR]. Local timekeeping was by farming years and rains, so a year of failed rains or an odd season could survive in family and oral history even where no written diary was kept [inference].
  - The printed text is an edited selection. The original journals are in the CMS archive (U241).
- *Proceedings* vol. 2 (1806-1809) searched for weather terms [OCR]: only general remarks about the rainy seasons of 1804-05 and the missionaries' fevers; nothing in the window.
- Governor Thomas Perronet Thompson (Freetown, Jul 1808-Feb 1810): letters at Leeds (MS277/1/1, 4 Mar 1809, to the Sierra Leone Company; MS277/1/2, 14 Aug 1809, to Castlereagh; MS277/3/42, letter from a slave trader at the Rio Pongas, 7 Feb 1810) and his family letters at Hull History Centre (U DTH) [catalogue]. Thompson wrote long private letters home [background]. U242.
- E. H. Columbine (Senegal 1809, Governor 1810-11): "journals, corresp and papers" 1802-11, University of Illinois Chicago (Sierra Leone collection), and copies of letter books and journals 1810-11, British Library RP 324 [catalogue]. U243.
- Already in the register: *Sierra Leone Gazette* 1808 and *The African Herald* Jan-Dec 1809 (B14, not read).

## 4. Portuguese Angola and Brazil: why the AHU is thin for 1808-10

- Claude counted dated entries by year in the AHU Bahia catalogue (Projeto Resgate, *Documentos manuscritos avulsos da Capitania da Bahia*, vol. II, archive.org bahia-avulsos-ii) [count]: 1805: 270, 1806: 385, **1807: 301, 1808: 59, 1809: 75, 1810: 94**, 1815: 108, 1822: 353. The fall in 1808 fits the move of the court and the colonial ministry to Rio in March 1808 [inference]. Of the 94 entries for 1808-10 that the search could separate cleanly, three matched weather words (chuva, seca, fome, trovoada, nevoeiro, fumo, cinza, escuridão, frio and others), and all three were false hits (a ship named *Trovoada*, two passports) [OCR].
- Reading [inference]: for 1808-21 the letters of the governors of Angola (António de Saldanha da Gama, 1807-10) and Mozambique went to the ministry in Rio. They should be sought in the **Arquivo Nacional, Rio de Janeiro** (SIAN catalogue; needs a free user account) and the IHGB, not in the AHU caixas listed in U150. Published work on the Angolan trade cites ANRJ, Junta do Comércio, box 448 for 1809 ship passports (Domingues da Silva 2017, archive.org snippet) [OCR]. U246.
- The Rio *Gazeta* for 1809 (U24) remains the best printed Brazilian source; it prints ship arrivals from Angola, Benguela and Mozambique with passage times [background].
