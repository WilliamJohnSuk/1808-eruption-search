## 1. The structural point

In 1808-1810 the Moluccan posts wrote to Batavia, not to The Hague. The British blockade cut most traffic between Java and Europe, and what Daendels did send home is summary-level. So the Moluccan originals are at ANRI Jakarta (local archives of Banda and Ternate, plus the Batavia government series), and the NA holds only what Batavia forwarded. Amboina's own 1806-1817 archive does not appear in the ANRI Ambon list (it starts 1817, with a few 1732 and 1784-91 items), which fits Van der Crab 1862 p. 146 on the lost British-period records (see B48). The British took Amboina (Feb 1810), Banda (Aug 1810) and Ternate (Aug 1810), which is why some bundles end in 1810.

It was evidently made for the Ministry in Europe. The printed issues follow: Nos 22-39 (0067-0113) and No. 50, 14 Dec 1810 (0114-0116). Nos 40-49 are not in the volume. William's inventory text lists No. 40, but the run seen here stops at No. 39 plus No. 50 (0109-0110 may hold No. 40; heading strip not conclusive).

**Read in full (printed pages):** No. 27 (6 Jul 1810: officers' pay rises "in aanmerking genomen hebbende de tegenwoordige duurte, zoo der levensmiddelen als stoffagien", i.e. the current dearness of provisions; Zeetijdingen 29 Jun-3 Jul, including a brig arriving from Banda), No. 37 (14 Sep), No. 38 (21 Sep), No. 39 (28 Sep: Daendels's report of 2 Oct 1810 on the English capture of Banda, 9 Aug 1810) and No. 50 (14 Dec; it contains a natural-history item on electric eels from a Gazette of 2 May 1810, not local).
**Read through the French digest only:** Nos 28-36.

Result: the digest entries and the pages read cover appointments, pay, paper money, tax farms, the Fiscal and Filz trials, the Bantam war, prisoner exchanges and European news. **No earthquake, eruption, ashfall, darkness, unusual sky or harvest failure is reported, Jul-Dec 1810** (Claude's reading). The only relevant economic line is the July 1810 "duurte der levensmiddelen", which is a general price rise under blockade and paper money. It does not name a cause, so read it as context only.

This closes the NA scanned material listed in B57, except inv. 19 (Daendels's missives), inv. 15, inv. 23 and 2.01.27.03 inv. 96/98, which have not been fetched.

## 14. NA 2.01.27.05 inv. 19, Daendels's missives 1808-11 (729 scans; read from 27 Oct 04:30; page images, Claude's reading)

**Volume index (scans 0002-0003):** for 1809 it lists the missives of 19 Mar 1809 (fol. 60), 23 Aug 1809 (fol. 73), GG and Raden 30 Aug 1809 (fol. 105a), 7 Sep 1809 (fol. 106), 16 Oct 1809 (fol. 110, 122) and 7 Nov 1809 (fol. 133). The 7 Nov enclosures are at fol. 145-263: instructions for Amboina, Banda, Pulau Ay, Ternate and the Prefect of Manado, the Polanen papers, and Javanese letters from the Emperor (Pakubuwana) and Sultan Hamengkubuwana. The 1810 missives are 4 Mar (fol. 267), 19 and 21 Apr, 25 Apr, 26 May, 2 Jul, and 26 Oct (fol. 336, "Relaas omtrent de Overgave van de Eilanden van Banda"). Scan ≈ folio + 138 for fol. 74-105. The volume also holds duplicates, such as 0245-0266.

**23 Aug 1809 missive, fol. 100 verso to 102 (scans 0238-0241).** Read in full on these pages:
- Coffee: the harvest near Samarang doubled on last year's, credited "eenlijk door de meerdere zorg" (solely to greater care) in harvesting and weeding.
- Rice, fol. 101: **"Het rijst gewas daarentegen is in sommige districten van Java minder gunstig geslaagd, zoodat zelfs op dit ogenblik geen overvloed aan dien kost [?] heerscht."** That is, the rice crop did less well in some districts of Java, so that even at this moment there is no abundance of that food. The word read as "kost" is uncertain; the rest is clear. No cause is given, and drought is not mentioned.
- Banda, fol. 101-101v: spices "vry voordeelig uitgevallen" in quantity. The nutmeg was again subject to the same "infectie", blamed on handling by former Governor Cranssen; only a small part was usable. A thorough enquiry was ordered, and Cranssen was replaced by Wieling, former gezaghebber of Ternate.
- Fol. 102: General Buyskes's expeditions to fetch produce from the east returned safely. The brig de Javaan took an English sloop near the Lombok strait. **"Alle de Bezittingen van Zyne Majesteit in Indien zyn ... van vyandelyken aanval en onrusting bevrijd gebleven ... De inwendige rust is insgelyks op dit Eiland zoo wel als op alle de overige Bezittingen van Zyne Majesteit ongestoord gebleven"** (all possessions free of enemy attack; internal peace undisturbed on Java and all other possessions). The Cheribon unrest was quelled.
- Fol. 102v-103: Penang intrigues and European news.

**Reading:** this is Daendels's general state-of-the-colony report covering March to August 1809. It mentions harvests, Banda and the outposts, but **no earthquake, eruption, ashfall, darkness or unusual weather** on the pages read (fol. 100v-103). Fol. 73-100 (Java administration, finance, the army) were not read page by page. Their subjects are known from the inv. 22 register (§8-10), which shows nothing natural.

### 14a. inv. 19, Daendels to the Minister of Marine and Colonies, Buitenzorg 4 Mar 1810, original, fol. 267 (scans 0546-0551, read in full; page images, Claude's reading)

- **Rice:** after the capture of the English frigate La Piémontaise, "het geschikt [?] van den invoer van rijst herwaarts" kept rice coming in. Batavia "tot de aanstaande oostmousson of nog wel langer, buiten alle verlegenheid van dit graan gesteld is" (supplied with this grain, beyond any difficulty, until the coming east monsoon or longer). This concerns rice supply under the blockade, not the harvest.
- **Weather:** the English blockading ships left the coast, probably because of the many illnesses their crews suffered "in dit voor hun ongewoon climaat en het natte weer, eigen aan de tegenwoordige mousson" (in this climate unfamiliar to them and the wet weather proper to the present monsoon). The wet season is described as normal.
- The rest of the letter covers copper from Japan (no ship sent in 1808; the 1809 ship was taken), lack of specie (agio 100%), Ceylon prisoners, the fortification of Merak bay, organisation, Buitenzorg and printing types.
- **No natural event is reported.** The phrase "goeden oogst van de Ryst" summarised in inv. 22 (scan 23) belongs to Daendels's letter of the same date **to the King, in French**. That letter is not in inv. 19; it is probably inv. 23 (Daendels to the King 1809-10), not fetched.
- The private letter of the same date (No. 2 "particulier", scans 0524-0531) concerns shipping produce to Bordeaux/Nantes/Lorient and requests for arms and officers. It notes "De Kolonie is geheel ontbloot van Europische artikelen"; there is nothing on harvest or nature.
- The 30 Nov 1809 "particulier" (scans 0518-0522) and a Javanese letter of the Emperor (0503-0504) were seen only by heading.

Cited scans added: 0546, 0547.

### 14b. inv. 19, missives of 16 Oct 1809 (fol. 110-117) and 7 Dec 1809 (fol. 133-141) (page images, Claude's reading)

- **16 Oct 1809, fol. 110-117 (scans 0278-0284), read in full.** It covers the exchange of prisoners with Elliot after La Piémontaise; "ruim 22 maanden" without replies from home; paper money; the Japan ship taken near Macao; reorganisation of the High Court of Justice; the blockade; coffee prices and American buyers; and requests for officers and doctors, because "het Klimaat hier toch steeds te vreezen is". **No rice remark and no natural event.**
- **Fol. 133-141 (scans 0319-0327), read in full.** The volume index (§14) was read as "7 Nov 1809". The shipping register at fol. 142 (scan 0329) lists it as "7 December 1809" ("Origineele Missive ... van den 7 decemb. 1809, fol. 133"), sent on the Nimrod via New York, so 7 Dec is taken as the date. The letter covers:
  - the climate and Daendels's health, and the provisional successor: J.A. van Braam, with C.W. Thalman as administrator general;
  - the death of the Prefect of Amboina, C.L. Wieling;
  - a commission, including the Amboina prefect L. Kluckvlugt, to reorganise the eastern possessions (Amboina, Banda, Ternate);
  - Moluccan credit paper and copper coin;
  - the Van Polanen affair; Brigadier de Sandol Roy;
  - letters of the Emperor and the Sultan of Mataram;
  - the King's birthday, 2 Sep, celebrated across Java;
  - the Samarang revenue farms, which raised 17,000 rixdollars more than the year before.
- **The letter has no harvest, weather or natural event.** Wieling's death and the Moluccan reorganisation are administrative only.

**inv. 19 summary:** none of the letters read in full reports an earthquake, eruption, ashfall, darkness or unusual sky: 23 Aug 1809, 16 Oct 1809, 7 Dec 1809 and 4 Mar 1810. 19 Mar 1809 (fol. 60) and the 1810 letters after March were not read.
