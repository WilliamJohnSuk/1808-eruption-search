## 1. STRONGEST FINDINGS

### 1A. Agung (Bali) "about seven years" before April 1815: an eruption with Tambora-like effects "in a lesser degree" (c. 1808)
- **Observer/author:** T. S. Raffles (Lieutenant-Governor of Java), compiling Residents' and officers' reports in his narrative of the Tambora eruption.
- **Place:** Bali. "Carang Assum" = Karangasem, i.e. Gunung Agung.
- **Event date:** "about seven years ago" relative to April 1815, so **c. 1808 (±1 yr)**. No month is given.
- **Writing date:** 1815, soon after the eruption. **Publication:** the original printing was probably in the Batavian Society transactions (Verhandelingen vol. 8, 1816) and the Asiatic Journal; this is unverified. Reprinted in Lady Sophia Raffles, *Memoir of the Life and Public Services of Sir T. S. Raffles* (London 1830), printed p. 244 (IA leaf 274).
- **Verbatim (OCR, IA `memoiroflifepubl00raff`, leaf 274):** "All reports concur in stating, that so violent and extensive an eruption has not happened within the memory of the oldest inhabitants, nor within tradition. They speak of similar effects in a lesser degree, when an eruption took place from the volcano of Carang Assum, in Ba[li], about seven yea[r]s ago; and it was at first supposed that this mountain was the seat of eruption in the present instance."
- **Translation:** English original.
- **What "similar effects" means:** in the Tambora narrative these effects are detonations heard far away (taken for cannon fire), ash falls and darkness reaching Java and Celebes. So c. 1808 an Agung eruption was **regionally noticeable**: probably heard and/or ash-fall-producing across the Java Sea region. This is still an inference.
- **Scan:** https://archive.org/details/memoiroflifepubl00raff (reachable; text via OCR search; page image not fetched).
- **Independence:** this is independent of Caldas and Unanue.
- **Downstream:** Junghuhn, *Java, seine Gestalt…* Bd. 2 (Leipzig 1857), p. 909 (IA `javaseinegestalt02jung`), turns this into: "1808. In diesem Jahre warf er eine ungeheure Menge Asche nebst Bimstein aus. (Siehe Life and Service of Sir Stamford Raffles p. 241.)" Printed p. 241 is the first page of the Raffles Tambora narrative. His chronology at p. 1014 repeats: "1808. Der G.-Agung auf Bali warf ungeheure Mengen Bimstein aus." **Junghuhn's "immense ash and pumice" and the exact year 1808 are his own inferences and amplification of Raffles's vaguer wording.** Raffles says "about seven years ago" and "in a lesser degree". The GVP entry "Agung 1808, VEI 2, historical observations" (no month) very likely derives from this chain through Neumann van Padang (1951). The VEI 2 is a catalogue default, not a measured value.
- **Constrains:** this is the only Indonesian report found of a regionally noticed eruption close in time to the ice-core event. Bali is at 8.3°S, which fits the Guevara-Murua tropical, southern-hemisphere preference. But the date could be 1807–1809, the scale is unknown, and Agung is not recorded as producing a stratospheric veil.
- **Next steps:** find the Dutch/English 1808 contemporary record. Leads: (a) the full Raffles narrative in *Verhandelingen van het Bataviaasch Genootschap* 8 (1816) and the *Java Government Gazette* 1815; (b) R. Friederich's Bali reports (*Verh. Bat. Gen.* 22–23, 1849–50); (c) Daendels-era Residents' reports from Besuki/Banyuwangi (Oosthoek) for 1808 in the Arsip Nasional RI (Jakarta) and the Nationaal Archief (Den Haag), e.g. archives of the Gouverneur-Generaal; (d) Delpher for Dutch reprints in 1809–1810. These leads were not reached from here.
- **Alternatives:** Raffles's informants may have been comparing Tambora with the 1808 Agung event, with Lamongan (8 Dec 1808 in GVP, East Java, opposite Bali across the strait), or with a 1807–1809 blur of several events.

### 1B. Luke Howard, London: coloured haze and twilights in spring 1809, the probable basis of Lamb's "early April 1809 sunset colouration"
- **Observer:** Luke Howard, Plaistow (Essex, near London).
- **Source:** *The Climate of London*, 1st ed., vol. 1 (London 1818), IA `climatelondon00howagoog`. The notes to the tables were first published in the *Athenaeum* until midsummer 1809. Howard writes at leaf 142: "Having ceased to publish my Observations when the ATHENÆUM was discontinued at Midsummer, 1809".
- **Writing date:** contemporaneous diary notes. **Publication:** 1809 (*Athenaeum*), republished 1818.
- **OCR fragments, IA in-book search:**
  - Leaf ~118, notes for late March to April 1809 (OCR day markers "March 28?" and "April 18?" are garbled): an "extensive … rose-coloured haze" at sunset, "spreading on the face of an opaque twilight", with Cirrostratus below dispersing. The same leaf has: "Hail has appeared occasionally in the showers".
  - Leaf 120, probably April–May 1809: "A faint red blush on the evening twilight, followed by hoar frost." Also: "A strong but not clear twilight" after calm, dewy nights.
  - Leaf 117, spring 1809: a meteor "much more conspicuous in the twilight than the planet Venus"; "The bat and black beetle come abroad."
  - Leaf 127, Sixth Month [June] 1809: "19, 20. A blush on the evening twilight."
  - Leaf 133, Ninth Month [Sept] 1809: "18. A blush on the evening twilight: windy." Also: "24. The moon rose clear but with a red [recess?] round it", and "28. … much redness as if from elevated Cirri at sunset."
  - Leaves ~105–112 (Dec 1808 to Feb 1809): searches for haze, twilight, sunset, red and sun found **no** optical notes. Leaf 108 starts "Twelfth Mo. 17. This night is described … as very stormy".
- **Verification:** OCR only. Exact day-dates in March–April 1809 are not secured. The next step is to read leaves 115–121 as page images at https://archive.org/details/climatelondon00howagoog, or in the 1833 2nd ed., vol. 2 (IA `b29350529_0002`). The OCR text of that edition fetched only up to Jan 1808.
- **Independence:** independent of Caldas and Unanue. Guevara-Murua et al. 2014 cite Lamb (1970) for "changes in sunset colouration seen over London in early April 1809". This matches Howard's leaf ~118 and is probably the same source.
- **Alternatives:** Howard notes coloured twilights in many years. He himself ascribes such colours to "elevated Cirri", and similar notes appear in 1806–1807 (see 2nd ed. vol. 2: "The whole hemisphere was very red for some time after sunset, which we ascribed to the reflection of light from elevated Cirri"). This is **weak, non-diagnostic evidence**. There is no sign of a persistent dry fog or dimmed sun in London in Dec 1808 to Feb 1809.

### 1C. Ruang (Sangihe), 1808, destructive eruption on Tagulandang
- **Source:** Junghuhn 1857, Bd. 2, p. 937 (OCR): "letzte Ausbruch dieses Vulkan's fand in 1808 Statt und verwüstete alle Häuser"; the island's former settlement "die alte Negory Tagalunda … auf der Insel"; islanders regard "den Ausbruch von 1808 als eine Strafe für diejenigen" who had planted there. Cross-reference "(Siehe Nr. 98.)".
- No month is given. The source is probably local oral information collected later (Dutch officials or missionaries).
- **Constrains:** a locally destructive eruption at 2.3°N. GVP lists it as VEI 2. There is no evidence of a large scale.

### 1D. Lamongan (East Java), 8 Dec 1808 (GVP): source **not traced**
- GVP: start 1808 Dec 8, VEI 2, "Explosion", with no source attributed per eruption. GVP references: Neumann van Padang 1951 (IAVCEI Catalogue, Indonesia).
- Junghuhn 1857 Bd. 2: a search for "Lamongan" gave 40 hits and none in 1799–1820, apart from "seit 1806 auch nicht geruht hatte" at p. 670. The chronology for 1808 at p. 1014 has **no Lamongan entry**. Neumann van Padang 1983 (*Scripta Geologica* 71, p. 20) says "Junghuhn mentioned eleven outbursts between 1799 and 1847", but no dates were visible.
- Verbeek & Fennema, *Description géologique de Java et Madoura* (1896), t. 1 and 2 (IA `descriptiongol11verb`, `descriptiongol12verb`): searching "1808" found only rock-sample numbers; Lamongan hits are geological only. The OCR may be incomplete.
- **Conclusion:** the exact day (8 Dec 1808) must come from a later Dutch compilation, probably from archival Residency reports (Besuki/Probolinggo) in the Daendels period. Candidates: Hageman, or the *Natuurkundig Tijdschrift voor Nederlandsch-Indië* lists of Java eruptions, Neumann van Padang 1951 p. ~150 (not online here), or Carn 2000 (JVGR 95:81–108, "The Lamongan volcanic field…"; ScienceDirect robots-blocked).
- **Interest:** 8 Dec 1808 is 4 days after the Guevara-Murua date of ~4 Dec ±7 d. It sits inside the window, but a VEI 2 explosion at a small cone-and-maar volcano cannot produce a bipolar sulfate signal. It is a coincidence unless the date actually belongs to a larger regional event: compare 1A, Raffles's "Carang Assum … about seven years ago", which is only ~100 km from Lamongan across the Bali Strait.
- **Next steps:** obtain Neumann van Padang 1951 (Catalogue of Active Volcanoes of the World, Part I Indonesia, Lamongan entry) and Carn 2000, Table of historic activity, to get the primary source citation.

### 1E. Chenoweth (2001): what the "Malaysian" data were (partly resolved)
- The abstract (EarthRef ERR/7234) says the eruption is dated to about March–June 1808 "based on a sudden temperature drop in Malaysian data". Guevara-Murua et al. 2014 paraphrase this as "an abrupt negative anomaly in noon temperatures of two Malaysian records". Timmreck et al. 2021 say "a sudden cooling in Malaysian temperature data".
- So the data are **two series of noon temperatures from Malaysian locations**, most likely ships' noon readings at anchor or shore registers at Penang (Prince of Wales Island) and/or Malacca. "Noon temperatures" is ship-logbook language. **The identity of the two records was NOT confirmed.** The Wiley full text (doi:10.1029/2000GL012648) could not be fetched.
- The Royal Society's Penang register (MA/350) covers 1839–45 only, so it is not the source.
- Chenoweth's 1808 onset is earlier than the Dec 1808 sky observations and the ice-core estimate (Feb 1809 ±4 months). A local temperature drop at two stations is weak timing evidence: possible causes are changes of instrument or exposure, monsoon variability, and ENSO.
