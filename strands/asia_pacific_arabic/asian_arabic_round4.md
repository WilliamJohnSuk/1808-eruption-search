### 2. The one finding that bears on dating: red twilights in Japan, winter 1808/09

**A. Edo, 文化5年11月24日 to 12月上旬 = 9 Jan to about 16–25 Jan 1809.**
- Musha prints the 續日本王代一覧後記 entry: 「二十四日ヨリ日出東天甚ダ赤シ、落日モ亦西南天甚ダ赤シ、十二月上旬ニ至ル迠毎日カタノ如シ」. In English: "From the 24th the eastern sky at sunrise was extremely red, and at sunset the southwestern sky too; it was so every day until early in the 12th month."
- Musha's printed conversion "1809.1.19" is wrong. 11/24 is 9 Jan 1809, and 19 Jan is 12/4.
- The month (11th) is confirmed on the manuscript image; see below.

**B. Tottori, 文化5年12月21日 = 5 Feb 1809.**
- The 因府年表 entry reads 「朝夕日輪の色赤き事如朱」. In English: "morning and evening the sun's disc was red as vermilion".
- It is a single day and independent of A.

**Image check, 16 Oct 2026 (image-verified).** Kagoshima University Library, Tamazato Bunko TZ0441, 續日本王代一覧後記 vol. 1 (item 0104410001), IIIF image 30 (left page, third column from the left). Read from William's download, ~/Downloads/1808-9 Volcano Project/kagoshima/vol1/p030.jpg:
- 「同廿四日ヨリ日出東天甚タ赤シ落日モ赤西南天甚赤シ」, followed by a two-line interlinear note 「十二月上旬ニ至ル迠毎日カクノ如シ」.
- The month is the **11th**. Image 29 has the heading 「十一月朔日」, and the entries that follow (同六日, 同八日, 同九日, 同十日, 同廿二日) carry no new month heading before 同廿四日. 同廿八日 comes next.
- So the start is **9 Jan 1809**, the 10th-month reading (11 Dec 1808) is ruled out, and the end, "early 12th month", is 16–25 Jan 1809.
- The OCR read 「落日モ亦」; the manuscript has 「落日モ赤」.
- The chronicle is mostly political; this is a rare sky entry. Image 29 has the only other sky items in Bunka 5: the total lunar eclipse of 9/15 = 3 Nov 1808, 「雨天ニテ見ヘズ然ドモ其時刻大暗冥」 ("not seen because of rain, but at that hour it was very dark"), and the partial solar eclipse of 10/1 = 18 Nov 1808, also clouded out. The eclipse darkness is weather, not a Danjon observation.

**Baseline.** The ERI database lists no comparable red-sky entry anywhere in Japan in the winters of 1806/07, 1807/08, 1809/10 or 1810/11. Its only other red-sky entry for 1806–1811 is Ōdate in April 1806. The database collects only earthquake-related material, so this baseline is weak.

**What they constrain.** If these are aerosol effects, a stratospheric veil reached about 35–36°N over Japan by early January 1809 and was still there in early February. That fits the Lima/Bogotá timing and an eruption before about December 1808. They do not locate the eruption, and they are not proof: vivid winter twilights over the dry Kantō plain are common. Their weight depends on the chronicler treating the Edo episode as remarkable, and on the page-image check.

### 3. Negatives (with their sensitivity)
**China.** Searches covered the full text of the Jiaqing Veritable Records (仁宗實錄) for JQ12–15, all of the Qingshigao imperial annals and disaster chapters, 18 Taiwan gazetteers, 371 geography files in the Daizhige corpus, the Ryukyu chronicles 球陽 and 中山世譜, and about 15 biji. None records haze, a dim or red sun, ash, pumice or detonations in 1808–09. Weather and cold items, none of them diagnostic:
- North China snow drought in winter 1808/09.
- Snow at Jiangshan (Zhejiang) about 3 May 1809.
- Snow in N Guangdong, Jan–Feb 1809.
- A severe Jiangsu winter in 1809/10.
- A stock omen of a "black vapour across the sky" at Dongguang, 14 Feb 1809.

On 6 May 1809 the emperor complained that the Astronomical Bureau's reports were written in advance, so the court's silence on the sky is weak evidence. The court record is well covered; mainland local gazetteers mostly are not.

**Korea.** The sky items reached for 1806–1811 come only through the KASI astronomical index. It has no entry of any kind between 18 Nov 1808 and 11 Aug 1809. The baseline shows Sunjo-era sources had almost stopped recording halos, white rainbows and sun colour (none at all from 1799 to 1820), so this silence says nothing. The daily court diaries (Seungjeongwon ilgi, Ilseongnok) could not be opened from here.

**Arabic, Persian and Ottoman.** Al-Jabarti's Cairo chronicle, read in full for AH 1223–1225, records hail, a black storm cloud and a fish fall, but no haze, dim sun or twilight glows. The 1809 Nile flood was early and excessive, which is weak evidence against strong monsoon suppression. Al-Jabarti is equally silent about the sky after Tambora (1815–17), so his silence has low sensitivity. Dunbuli (Persian) records severe early cold in the Caucasus in Nov–Dec 1808, which is context only.

**Other events in the window that are not eruption signals:**

- An abnormal tide at Naha on 18 Aug 1808.
- Zaō's explosion was 文化6年11月23日 = 29 Dec 1809, too late to be the source.

# II. Japan, Ryukyu, Deshima


Strand: Japanese-language records, Ryukyu, Dutch Deshima factory. Compiled 2026-10-16.
Window searched: Sept 1808 – mid 1809 (core Nov 1808 – Feb 1809); baseline 1806–1811.

### 0. Calendar

- I used the `sxtwl` package (Chinese lunisolar calendar) and checked it against fixed points in the Japanese Kansei calendar. In every month from Bunka 5 month 7 through Bunka 6 month 12, the Japanese month began on the same day as the Chinese one. The only difference is the leap month: Japan intercalated **閏六月** in Bunka 5 (Musha's note: 「文化五年ハ六月閏ナリ」), while China intercalated after month 5. The calendars therefore label June–Aug 1808 differently, but the days still match. Fixed points used: the ERI database's own conversions (文化五年十一月六日 = 1808-12-22, recorded as 冬至 in 〔御広間雑記〕 and 〔東所家乘〕; 文化五年七月廿二日 = 1808-09-12; 文化六年一月二日 = 1809-02-15), the Phaeton incident 文化5年8月15日 = 4 Oct 1808, and births on 文化6年3月14日 = 1809-04-28 and 11月11日 = 1809-12-17.
- First day of each Bunka 5 month: 8月 = 20 Sep 1808, 9月 = 20 Oct, 10月 = 18 Nov, 11月 = 17 Dec, 12月 = 16 Jan 1809. **Bunka 6 began 14 Feb 1809 (confirmed).** Bunka 6 months: 1月 = 14 Feb, 2月 = 16 Mar, 3月 = 15 Apr, 4月 = 14 May, 5月 = 13 Jun, 6月 = 13 Jul 1809.

### 1. Has anyone already studied Japanese records for the 1809 eruption?

**No dedicated study found.**
- Timmreck et al. 2021 (*Clim. Past* 17:1455, "The unidentified eruption of 1809: a climatic cold case") say there is "no historical source reporting a strong volcanic eruption in 1809". They do not use any Japanese, Korean or Chinese documents.
- Japanese diary-climate work skips 1808–09:
  - Yoshimura 2007 (*Tenki* 54:83) describes the historical weather database (HWDB).
  - Hirano et al. 2012 (*Geogr. Rev. Japan* 85:275) reconstruct winters from the 1830s on.
  - Kondo 1994 (*Tenki* 41:465) works from 1834 on.
  - Wang/Toride/Ichino/Yoshimura (EGU23-12593) cover the 1810s and Tambora.
  - JpGU 2021 O07-P01 (Nakamura Heizaemon diary) starts in 1812.
- The only systematic Japanese compilation that touches 1808–09 sky anomalies is **Musha Kinkichi's 増訂大日本地震史料 vol. 3 (1943), p. 174**. Musha printed two red-sun / red-twilight entries for winter 1808/09 as possible earthquake "omens", with no volcanic reading (see Findings A and B). As far as I can tell, nobody has connected them to the 1809 sulfate signal.
- Hayashi 1998 (*Kazan* 43:207) shows that the "1810 Kampū (Oga) eruption" record was fabricated in the Akita domain's Edo office. It is not a real eruption.
- Tsuji 2025 (*Tsunami Engineering Research Report* 42) treats the small tsunami of 4 Dec 1808 on the Kii and Tosa coasts as a local Nankai "tsunami earthquake", not a far-field or volcanic event (see Section 3).

### 2. Positive findings

#### Finding A. Edo: very red sky at sunrise and sunset every day from about 9 Jan 1809 until mid/late Jan 1809

- **Source:** 〔續日本王代一覧後記〕 (Zoku Nihon Ōdai Ichiran kōki). This is a manuscript annal for Bunka 1 to Bunsei 9 (1804–1826), compiled by **片山円然 (Katayama Enzen)**, an active scholar of the Kasei period (1804–1830); Kyoto University holds his portrait, dated 1812.
  - Where it was kept: its other entries consistently report "江戸地震 / 東都 / 江都" with the hour, so it is an **Edo-centred** chronicle. The entry gives the sunrise sky as east and the sunset sky as **southwest**, which matches the winter sunset azimuth at Edo (about 240°).
  - Manuscript: Kagoshima University Library, Tamazato Bunko, call no. TZ0441, 3 vols. IIIF manifest: https://dc.lib.kagoshima-u.ac.jp/iiif/0/metadata/0104410003/manifest.json
- **Printed in:** 増訂大日本地震史料 第3巻 (文部省震災予防評議会, 1943), p. 174.
- **Database copy:** ERI 地震史料集テキストデータベース, ID **J0300406**, https://materials.utkozisin.org/articles/detail?id=J0300406
- **Text.** OCR transcription marked [未校訂] (unproofread) by the database, NOT checked against the image:
  - 綱文 (Musha's headnote): 「文化五年十一月二十四日（西暦一八〇九、一、一九、）ヨリ十二月上旬ニ至ルマデ日出及ビ日沒時ノ天色甚ダ赤シ、」
  - 本文 (entry text): 「二十四日ヨリ日出東天甚ダ赤シ、落日モ亦西南天甚ダ赤シ、十二月上旬ニ至ル迠毎日カタノ如シ、」
- **Translation:** "From the 24th, at sunrise the eastern sky was extremely red, and at sunset the southwestern sky was also extremely red. It was like this every day until the early part of the 12th month."
- **Dates:**
  - Start: 文化五年十一月二十四日 = **9 January 1809 (Gregorian)**.
  - End: 十二月上旬 (12/1–12/10) = **16–25 January 1809**.
  - Duration: roughly 1–2.5 weeks of daily red twilight.
- **Date problem:**
  - Musha's headnote gives 「一八〇九、一、一九」, but 11/24 is 9 Jan 1809. 19 Jan is 12/4, and the database files the entry under 12/4 because of that number.
  - Most likely the OCR read "一、九" as "一、一九", or Musha made a misprint.
  - The month of "二十四日" depends on the chronicle's preceding entry, which I could not see. If the 24th belonged to the 10th month, it would be **11 Dec 1808**, the same day the Bogotá veil began. That reading would give about six weeks of red twilight. Musha read it as the 11th month.
  - **This must be checked on the p. 174 page image (ERI DB) or the Kagoshima manuscript.**
- **Independence:** Independent of the South American observations. Not dependent on Finding B.
- **Alternative explanations:**
  - Ordinary winter twilight colour in the dry, clear Kantō winter. Against this, the chronicler thought it remarkable enough to record, and it is the only sky-colour entry from this chronicle printed for 1804–1828.
  - Smoke from a large fire. I found no great Edo fire in Jan 1809.
  - Aurora: excluded, because the colour was tied to sunrise and sunset.
  - Asian dust: unlikely in midwinter.
- **What it constrains:** If the 11/24 reading is right, an optically thick stratospheric aerosol layer was over central Japan (about 35.7°N) by 9 Jan 1809. That fits the Lima/Bogotá timing (Dec 1808–Feb 1809) and an eruption in or before early Dec 1808. If the 10/24 reading is right, onset was 11 Dec 1808, simultaneous with Bogotá.

#### Finding B. Tottori (Inaba): sun red as vermilion morning and evening, 5 Feb 1809

- **Source:** 〔因府年表〕 (Inpu nenpyō), the Tottori domain annals compiled by 岡島正義 (Okajima Masayoshi, 19th c.), based on domain records. It is a later compilation, not a diary.
- **Printed in:** 増訂大日本地震史料 第3巻, p. 174.
- **Database copy:** ID **J0300407**, https://materials.utkozisin.org/articles/detail?id=J0300407
- **Text.** OCR [未校訂]:
  - 綱文: 「文化五年十二月二十一日（西暦一八〇九、二、五、）因幡國鳥取ニテ太陽ノ色赤ク見エタリ、」
  - 本文: 「廿一日 朝夕日輪の色赤き事如朱、」
- **Translation:** "21st [of the 12th month]: morning and evening, the colour of the sun's disc was red like vermilion."
- **Date:** 文化五年十二月二十一日 = **5 February 1809**. Musha's conversion is correct here.
- **Place:** Tottori, about 35.5°N, 134.2°E.
- **Independence:** A different region and a different compiler from Finding A, about 600 km apart, on a different date.
- **Alternative explanations:**
  - Local smoke or haze.
  - Normal reddening of the sun low on the horizon. "如朱" (like vermilion) is the stock phrase of Japanese red-sun records. The same annal uses it again for 1841 (天保12年6月17日), so the annal records such events only occasionally.
- **What it constrains:** At best it suggests aerosol was still present over western Japan in early February 1809, consistent with deposition around Feb 1809. On its own it is weak evidence, a single day.

#### Context finding C. No ash-fall, pumice, detonation or eruption reports in Japan, Sept 1808 – mid 1809

- **Scope:** I scanned the full ERI database listing for 1806-01 to 1811-12: about 560 records over 6 result pages. I looked at headnotes and snippets for sun/sky, ash, sand, rumbling, tide, eruption, pumice and "hair rain" (毛降).
- **Non-earthquake items in or near the window:**
  1. **4 Dec 1808 (文化五年十月十七日): a small tsunami on the Kii and Tosa coasts.** It followed earthquakes on the night of 3 Dec felt in Tottori, Osaka, Tosa and Kii. Three sources record it:
     - 〔隠見雑日記〕, Akaoka (Tosa), 新収日本地震史料 vol. 4 p. 290, ID J1001233: 「十七日 朝曇当日手結浦ノ湊へ鈴波入り引汐之時湊ノ舟 其汐ニゆらるる事物すざましき事」
     - 〔反古の綴〕, Kinomoto (Kumano), same volume p. 290, ID J1001236: 「文化五年十月十七日、晴天、海静ニシテ八ツ時潮干、卯（？）時俄ニ汐水溢来、湊より大河ノ如ク溯リ…」
     - 〔田辺万代記〕 vol. 11, Tanabe, 日本の歴史地震史料 拾遺二 p. 154, ID J2800472: 「…七ツ過急ニ汐満海近ハ汐入引てハ指引てハさし五ツ比迄四度也、汐時ニて無之ニ不審成事と…大方外国ニ津波行余浪ニても候ハん」 ("…probably the leftover waves of a tsunami that went to some foreign country").
     - The Tanabe writer guessed a foreign tsunami. Tsuji (2025) argues instead for a local Nankai tsunami earthquake. I list it only as a possible but unlikely far-field signal. OCR text.
  2. **8 Apr 1809 (文化六年二月二十四日): a large landslide with rumbling (鳴動) in Azumi district, Shinano.** It is recorded in 〔一話一言〕〔野史〕〔慶弘紀聞〕〔續王代一覧後記〕. This was a non-volcanic mass movement.
  3. **3 Oct 1809 (文化六年八月二十四日): 富士山崩**, i.e. a collapse on Mt Fuji (〔日本災異志〕, citing 續皇年代略記). It falls outside the window and is not an eruption.
  4. **Zaō 1809:**
     - 〔栗藩史稿〕: 「十一月二十三日、刈田郡藏王嶽燒焰暴發、硫黄流溢レ、阿武隈川ノ魚盡ク死ス、」, 増訂大日本地震史料 vol. 3 p. 175, ID J0300419.
     - Date: 文化六年十一月二十三日 = **29 Dec 1809 (Gregorian)**. JMA's "1809年12月29日" is therefore already a Gregorian date. This event comes about 10 months after the ice-core deposition and **cannot be the source**. Extends the earlier pass.
  5. **Baseline sky entries in the same database:**
     - Apr 1806, 〔大館𦾔記〕: 「文化三寅年四月中頃、天赤く、月共に朱の如し」.
     - 1807: comet (〔御日記草書〕, Takeo, 文化四年八月晦日 = 1 Oct 1807). Sulfur fire at Iwaki-san (〔津輕藩史〕, 文化四年二月二十三日 = 31 Mar 1807).
     - 1810–11: Oga earthquake (with the fabricated eruption story), "white hair fell" in Kami-Ina (文化七年七月, 1810), Miyake-jima eruption (文化八年一月三日 = 27 Jan 1811).
     - Nothing comparable appears in winter 1806/07, 1807/08, 1809/10 or 1810/11. The only database red-sky entries for 1806–1811 are Findings A and B and the 1806 Ōdate entry.
- **Caveat:** This database collects material related to earthquakes and eruptions. A sky remark is included only if a compiler copied it. Absence here is weak evidence.

#### Context finding D. Weather and crop context

- **Kyoto cherry full-flowering:** 1809 = day 107 (17 Apr). 1808 = day 98, 1810 = day 100. The 1800–1820 mean is about 106, so there is no anomaly (Aono series, via rethinking/cherry_blossoms.csv). The reconstructed March temperature for 1809 is 4.94 °C, slightly lower than 1808 (5.14 °C) and among the lowest of the 1800s–1810s; this is a smoothed series.
- **Kawamata, Fukushima** (〔累世年鑑〕, 新収日本地震史料 続補遺 p. 464 ff., OCR), spring and summer 1809:
  - 4月7–8日 (20–21 May 1809): 「甚冷気両日霧雨」 (very cold, drizzle on both days).
  - 4月26日 (8 Jun 1809): 「昼頃ゟ甚冷気」 (very cold from about noon).
  - 6月4日 (16 Jul 1809): 「朝少冷し…袷着」 (somewhat cold in the morning; wore a lined garment).
  - These are only hints of a cool early summer in southern Tōhoku in 1809. I found no published 凶作 (crop failure) record for 1809.
- **Ryukyu:** the 球陽 disaster table in Takara et al. 2009 (Univ. Ryukyus, 沖縄の災害情報に関する歴史文献を主体とした総合的研究, part 2) has **no entries between 尚灝2年 (1805) and 尚灝15年 (1815)**. That means no recorded typhoon, tsunami, ash or famine for 1808–09. This negative comes from a disaster tabulation, not a check for sky remarks.

### 1. Has anyone examined Korean records for the 1809 eruption or for 1808–09 optical anomalies?

**No, as far as I could find.** No published work examines Korean sky or optical records (日暈, 白虹, 日色, 赤氣/白氣, 霾, 雨土) for 1808–09 in connection with the unidentified eruption.

- **Kim Sung-woo (Daegu Haany Univ.):**
  - Climatic Change 176 (2023), doi:10.1007/s10584-023-03480-w, "Successive volcanic eruptions (1809–1815) and two severe famines of Korea…".
  - Clim. Past 21:1521 (2025) / cp-2024-7.
  - Both papers use Sillok, Seungjeongwon ilgi (SJW), crop reports, Wada's Seoul rainfall, and the diaries of No Sang-ch'u, Kwŏn Cho and Yi Pyŏng-t'ak.
  - Both contain no sky, halo, sun-colour or haze passages. I checked with WebFetch keyword queries; "halo", "haze", "fog" and "sun" (other than "Sunjo") are absent.
  - The only sky item is the court's belief that "the appearance of a comet in 1808" caused the 1809 drought, cited to SI 9/10/14 = 21 Nov 1809. Kim also gives a frost on 22 Sept (1809, 20 days early), with no primary citation.
- **Timmreck et al. 2021, CP 17:1455 (the "cold case" paper):** no East Asian documentary records. It says there is "no historical source reporting a strong volcanic eruption in 1809".
- **Guevara-Murua et al. 2014, CP 10:1707, and its review (Cole-Dai, C589):** only Bogotá, Lima and Europe. Asia is not mentioned.
- **Wikipedia, "1808 mystery eruption":** no Asian records.
- **Hwang & Park, EGUsphere-2026-4101:** tests volcanic forcing on the Seoul Chugugi rainfall series (1778–2024). The abstract does not discuss 1808/09 specifically or any optical phenomena.
- **Hyun, Mihn, Lee, Kim & Bahk 2024, Weather (doi:10.1002/wea.4532):** halo terminology in Korean documents, but it uses 17th-century material (Donggung ilgi, Pungun-gi of 1748), not 1808–09.
- **Silverman & Hayakawa 2021, SWSC 11:17 (Dalton Minimum aurorae):** no Korean data.
- **Wang et al. (ESSOAr 10503630), Korean aurora catalogue 1012–1811:** would contain any 赤氣/白氣 entries for 1806–11. Only the abstract was readable (lead below).

Conclusion: the Korean optical record for winter 1808–09 is untouched in print. What I could check (below) shows no reported anomaly in the Sillok or in the 增補文獻備考 象緯考 as indexed by KASI. The richest sources, SJW, Ilseongnok and 內閣日曆, were not reachable from here.

**Result for the core window, 1 Nov 1808 – 28 Feb 1809:** the only entries are a lunar eclipse on 3 Nov 1808 and a solar eclipse on 18 Nov 1808. Both are **computed or forecast** Gwansanggam eclipse reports: the lunar eclipse ran "below the horizon" in daylight, and the solar eclipse gives a computed magnitude and timing. Both fall before the Bogotá onset (11 Dec 1808), and neither records colour or brightness.

**From Sunjo 8/10/1 (18 Nov 1808) to 9/7/1 (11 Aug 1809), the DB has no record at all.** That means no halo, no 白虹貫日, no sun-colour report and no 赤氣/白氣 in the Sillok or the 상위고 (as indexed by KASI).

#### 2.2 The "1808 comet"

The KASI DB has no comet in 1808. The Sillok and the 상위고 record the Great Comet of 1807 (C/1807 R1):
- first seen at dusk on Sunjo 7/9/3 = 3 Oct 1807;
- reported extinguished on 7/11/19 = 17 Dec 1807;
- the 상위고 says it lasted "3개월".

The SJW memorial of 9/10/14 (21 Nov 1809) that blames the drought on a "comet of 1808", as reported by Kim, probably refers to this 丁卯 (1807) comet, which was still in memory. The other possibility is an SJW-only sighting not indexed by KASI.
**Lead for a human:** read SJW 純祖 9年 10月 14日 at sjw.history.go.kr (blocked from here) and check the year cyclical character in the memorial.

#### 2.3 Positive findings for the eruption

**None.** Not one Korean record of an optical anomaly in Sept 1808 – mid 1809 was found in the sources I could reach. This is a negative result from the Sillok and the 增補文獻備考 象緯考 only. It is **not** a negative result for SJW, Ilseongnok or 內閣日曆, where routine Gwansanggam reports (日暈, 白虹, 霧, 雨土) would sit.

### 3. Baseline: how often are such items recorded in the Sillok and 상위고 at all?

Counts come from the KASI DB pages read (1793–1839). The Sunjo-period Sillok is extremely sparse for sky phenomena: in practice it records eclipses (mostly computed), comets and bright meteors, and only rarely halos or white rainbows.

Interpretation:
- From Jeongjo's death (1800) the Sillok and 상위고 almost stop recording "omen" weather optics: there are 0 white rainbows from 1799 to 1820.
- So the absence of a 1808–09 entry has **very low diagnostic power**. It reflects recording practice under Sunjo's regency government, not the sky.
- A real test needs the daily SJW / Ilseongnok / 內閣日曆 Gwansanggam lines for Nov 1808 – Feb 1809. They should be compared with Nov–Feb of 1806/07, 1807/08, 1809/10 and 1810/11, counting 日暈, 白虹, 日色赤/無光, 霧/昏霧, 霾 and 雨土 per month.
- Note that 雨土/黃霧 is a spring (Mar–May) Asian-dust phenomenon. A winter (Dec–Feb) 霾 or 日色 report would be more unusual than a spring one.

# IV. Arabic, Persian, Ottoman, Indian Ocean


Worker report, compiled 2026-10-16. Window: Sept 1808 to mid-1809, core Nov 1808 to Feb 1809. Baseline 1806 to 1811.

### 0. Bottom line

1. **Published work.** I found no study that has looked at Arabic, Persian or Ottoman Turkish records for the 1808/09 unknown eruption. The main eruption papers use no Middle Eastern documentary data. These are Timmreck et al. 2021 (Clim. Past 17:1455), Guevara-Murua et al. 2014 (Clim. Past 10:1707) and the Wikipedia summary of the literature. Timmreck et al. state that "there is no historical source reporting a strong volcanic eruption in 1809". Their documentary inputs are EIC ship logs (Brohan et al. 2012), Chenoweth 2001 (Malaysian temperatures), Dove's station series, and Caldas/Unanue via Guevara-Murua. Two Middle East historical-climatology programmes exist, and neither reaches 1808–09:
   - Vogt, Glaser, Luterbacher et al. 2011, PAGES news 19(1), covers Arabic sources for 800–1500 CE only.
   - White's *Climate of Rebellion* covers 1590–1710.

   I found no al-Jabarti weather-extraction study. Ottoman Istanbul winter studies (e.g. Yavuz et al. 2023, IJGW 29:350) do not list 1808/09 in any reachable abstract. **Searching these languages for this event appears to be new.**
2. **Primary texts.** The best daily source in the region is al-Jabarti's *ʿAjāʾib al-āthār* (Cairo). It is fully digitized, and I read AH 1223–1225 line by line. It records **no dimmed or discoloured sun, no haze, no unusual twilight and no ash** in Cairo in Nov 1808 – Feb 1809. It does record four weather events in the window:
   - destructive hail in Gharbiyya, 16 Nov 1808;
   - a black thunder-cloud over Cairo, with giant hail in the Gharbiyya region, on the night of 16/17 Feb 1809;
   - heavy cold rain with a "fish fall" at Birkat al-Hajj, 18 Mar 1809;
   - grain scarcity in spring 1809, then an **excessive** Nile flood in summer–autumn 1809.
3. **Persian.** Dunbuli's *Maʾāthir-i sulṭāniyya* (read in a modern Arabic translation) gives **severe cold and blizzards in the southern Caucasus**. The Russian siege of Erevan was broken on 28 Nov 1808, and the Russians retreated after 11/12 Dec 1808, when many died of cold "in the forty days of winter" (*chilla*). It mentions no sky phenomena.
4. **Calibration that matters.** For 1815–1817, after Tambora, al-Jabarti is just as silent about the sun and sky. His only dust entry is a routine spring khamsin with "yellow dust" (spring 1817). He was attentive to the sky: he timed eclipses, e.g. 1 Feb 1813, and recorded a red-yellow sunset glow on 20 Oct 1802. So **his silence does not rule out a thin stratospheric veil over Egypt**. It only argues against a conspicuous, alarming effect such as a "silver sun" in Cairo. **Negative evidence is weak.**
5. I found **no positive observation** of the veil, dim sun or twilight glows anywhere in the region from the sources I could reach.

#### F1. Hail at al-Mahalla al-Kubra / Gharbiyya, 27 Ramadan 1223

- **Author / observer:** ʿAbd al-Rahman al-Jabarti (Cairo). He reports this second-hand, from the Delta.
- **Place:** Gharbiyya province and al-Mahalla al-Kubra, Nile Delta.
- **Observed date:** 27 Ramadan 1223, which is **Wed 16 Nov 1808** (±1 day).
- **Document date:** chronicle compiled c. 1805–1821, written from running notes.
- **Location in text:** OpenITI `1250AH/data/1237Jabarti/1237Jabarti.CajaibAthar/1237Jabarti.CajaibAthar.Shamela0011998-ara1`, lines 36854–36856; ed. Dar al-Jil vol. 3 p. 242.
- **Excerpt (digital transcription):** ومن الحوادث السماوية أن في سابع عشرين رمضان غيمت السماء بناحية الغربية والمحلة الكبرى وامطرت بردا في مقدار بيض الدجاج واكبر واصغر فهدمت دورا واصابت انعاما غير أنها قتلت الدودة من الزرع البدري.
- **Translation:** "Among the celestial events: on 27 Ramadan the sky clouded over in the region of Gharbiyya and al-Mahalla al-Kubra and rained hail the size of hens' eggs, larger and smaller. It destroyed houses and struck livestock, but it killed the worm in the early-sown crops."
- **Independence:** a single source. Al-Jabarti reports it as hearsay from the region.
- **Alternative explanation:** an ordinary Mediterranean autumn convective storm. Delta hail in Nov–Feb happens in other years too.
- **What it constrains:** nothing about the eruption directly. It predates the Bogotá veil onset (11 Dec 1808).

#### F2. Black thunder-cloud over Cairo, giant hail in Gharbiyya, night of 1/2 Muharram 1224

- **Observer:** al-Jabarti. The Cairo part is first-hand ("in the evening… then the sky cleared and the stars appeared"). The Gharbiyya hail is reported by people arriving from Bilad al-Samahat.
- **Observed date:** the eve of Friday 2 Muharram 1224, i.e. the night of **Thu 16 / Fri 17 Feb 1809**.
- **Location:** lines 37088–37094; vol. 3 p. 253.
- **Excerpt:** استهل شهر المحرم بيوم الخميس وفي تلك الليلة اعني ليلة الجمعة ثانيه مرت سحابة سوداء مظلمة في وقت العشاء وحصل فيها رعد مزعج وبرق مستنير شديد اللمعان وامطرت في محلات قليلا وفي أخرى كثيرا ثم انجلت السماء سريعا فظهرت النجوم وبعد أيام أخبر الواردون من ناحية بلاد السماحات بالغربية أنها امطرت بتلك الناحية في تلك الليلة بردا كبيرا وصغيرا والكبير في مقدار حجر الطاحون والصغير في مقدار بيض الدجاج وتهدمت منها دور وقتلت مواشي وآدمية وأهلكت زروعا كثيرة.
- **Translation:** "Muharram began on a Thursday. That night, the night of Friday the 2nd, a dark black cloud passed at the time of the evening prayer, with alarming thunder and very brilliant lightning. It rained a little in some places and a lot in others, then the sky quickly cleared and the stars appeared. Some days later, people arriving from the Bilad al-Samahat district of Gharbiyya reported that in that region it had rained hail that night, large and small, the large the size of a millstone (*sic*, hyperbole) and the small the size of hens' eggs. Houses were destroyed, livestock and people killed, and many crops ruined."
- **Alternatives:** a winter cyclone or squall line. The "millstone" size is formulaic exaggeration.
- **Constraint:** the stars were visible right after the storm, so there is no comment on haze. This falls at the end of the Bogotá/Lima veil period.

#### F3. Heavy rain and "fish fall" at Birkat al-Hajj, 1 Safar 1224

- **Observer:** al-Jabarti, who says he saw the fish himself: وشاهدناه.
- **Date:** **Sat 18 Mar 1809**.
- **Location:** lines 37119–37121; vol. 3 p. 254.
- **Excerpt:** وفي ذلك اليوم غيمت السماء بالسحاب وامطرت كثيرا ونزل مطر ببركة الحاج وجدوا فيه سمكا صغيرا من جنس السمك الذي يعرف بالقاروص وصار يتنطط على الأرض وأحضروا منه إلى مصر وشاهدناه وهو في غاية البرودة.
- **Translation:** "That day the sky clouded over and it rained heavily. At Birkat al-Hajj rain fell in which they found small fish of the kind called *qārūṣ* (sea bass), jumping on the ground. Some were brought to Cairo and we saw them; they were extremely cold."
- **Alternatives:** a waterspout or convective storm lifting fish from the lake or Nile, or fish stranded in the flooded pond. Meteorological curiosity only.
- **Constraint:** none.

#### F4. Grain scarcity, then abundance (spring 1809)

- **Location:** lines 37137–37139 (Safar 1224, i.e. March 1809), 37230–37234 and 37245–37246 (Rabiʿ I 1224, i.e. 16 Apr – 15 May 1809); vol. 3 pp. 255–258.
- **Excerpt:** وفي هذا الشهر وما قبله تشحطت الغلال وغلا سعرها حتى بلغ الاردب القمح الفا وستمائة نصف فضة … ولولا لطف الله بوجود الذرة لهلكت الخلائق … وفي أواخره تواجدت الغلال وانحل سعرها وحضر الفلاحون ببدارى الغلة وانحط السعر.
- **Translation:** "This month and the previous one grain became scarce and dear, until the ardabb of wheat reached 1,600 paras… Had God not been kind in providing sorghum, people would have perished… At the end of the month grain became available, prices eased, and the peasants brought the early grain, and prices fell."
- **Alternatives (dominant):** the cause is the **low Nile of 1807–08**, explicitly noted at lines 36741–36744 (Jumada II 1223, Aug 1808: "the shortfall of the Nile last year"), plus Muhammad ʿAli's exactions. Al-Jabarti blames taxation throughout. This predates the eruption and is not a volcanic signal.

#### F5. Nile flood of 1809: early and excessive

- **Location:** wafāʾ (the flood reaching the full-Nile mark) on Thu 28 Jumada II 1224 = 5 Misra = **10 Aug 1809**, lines 37522–37528, vol. 3 p. 270. Excessive flood, Ramadan 1224 (Oct 1809), lines ~37629–37635: فإن النيل في هذه السنة زاد زيادة مفرطة وعلا على الاعالي وتلف بزيادته المفرطة الدراوي والاقصاب بقبلي ("this year the Nile rose excessively, topped even the high lands, and by its excess ruined the sorghum and sugar-cane in Upper Egypt").
- **Baseline:** the 1808 flood was late and weak. Its wafāʾ came only after prayers for rain, around 22–23 Aug 1808 (lines 36741–36770).
- **Relevance:** a large tropical eruption often weakens the East African / Ethiopian monsoon and the following Nile flood (cf. Oman et al. 2006 for Laki/Katmai; Manning et al. 2017). **A very high flood in summer 1809 does not show that response.** At most this weakly suggests that the 1809 aerosol forcing did not suppress the 1809 Ethiopian monsoon. ENSO and other factors could dominate. I have not checked a quantitative Roda nilometer series; Toussoun 1925 and Popper 1951 tables are the lead for that.

#### F6. Southern Caucasus: severe early winter, Russian retreat from Erevan (Persian source)

- **Author:** ʿAbd al-Razzaq Beg Dunbuli (Maftun), *al-Maʾāthir al-sulṭāniyya* (Persian, composed c. 1810s; Tabriz print 1241/1826). He was at the court of crown prince ʿAbbas Mirza in Tabriz, and is not an eyewitness at Erevan.
- **Text used:** the modern Arabic translation by Muhammad Sayyid Abu Zayd (2005), via OpenITI `1250AH/data/1243CabdRazzaqDunbuli/.../1243CabdRazzaqDunbuli.MaathirSultaniyya.ShamAY0033837-ara1`, ch. 112, lines 5275–5396 (print pp. 203–208). **This is a translation, not the Persian original.**
- **Dates:**
  - Russian night assault on Erevan: night of 9 Shawwal 1223, i.e. **28/29 Nov 1808**. This matches the Russian sources' 17 Nov O.S. = 29 Nov 1808 N.S.
  - Russian withdrawal: night of Monday 22 Shawwal 1223, i.e. **11/12 Dec 1808**.
- **Excerpts (Arabic translation):**
  - "ولأن برودة الهواء كانت فى غاية الشدة، وحتى وصول كدويتش إلى الكرجستان، فقد أعجز هجوم برد وبرودة الأربعين الشتوية أيدى الروس…" (l. 5348–5351). Translation: "Because the cold of the air was extremely severe, until Gudovich reached Georgia, the onslaught of the cold of the 'forty days' of winter disabled the Russians…"
  - "اشتدت إلى أقصى درجة ريح الشتاء وبردها، بحيث لم يستطع الرفاق رؤية وجه بعضهما بسبب هجوم الرياح الشديدة" (l. 5384–5385). Translation: "the winter wind and cold reached their utmost, so that companions could not see each other's faces for the violent winds", i.e. a blizzard near Nakhchivan.
- **Alternatives:** an ordinary Armenian-plateau winter. *Chilla* (the "forty days") is a stock seasonal term, and the court historian has an interest in Russian losses. Russian sources independently report heavy frost losses during Gudovich's retreat.
- **Constraint:** cold context only. It begins one or two weeks *before* the Bogotá veil was noticed (11 Dec), so it cannot be caused by a ~4 Dec 1808 eruption. No sky phenomena are reported. The phrase توارت الشمس خلف ستارة الغروب at l. 5361 is only "the sun set".

#### Calibration and negative evidence from al-Jabarti

- Lines 36651–37874 (AH 1223–1224, 28/29 Feb 1808 – 5 Feb 1810) were read in full, together with keyword greps over lines 36400–38200. The terms were: الشمس، غبار، ضباب، قتام، عجاج، حمرة، زلزل، كسوف، ظلام، دخان، كبريت، الأفق، نجم، كوكب، ثلج، صقيع. Apart from F1–F3, there are **no** sky or atmosphere entries. The Shawwal and Dhu l-Qaʿda 1223 entries (20 Nov 1808 – 16 Jan 1809) are brief and purely political or fiscal.
- Al-Jabarti does report sky phenomena when he notices them:
  - l. 28256 ff., AH 1217 (Oct 1802): "احمرت السماء بالسحاب عند غروب الشمس حمرة مشوبة بصفرة", a red sunset glow tinged with yellow, followed by lightning, with an astronomical and Coptic date;
  - eclipses timed to the minute (l. 29650 ff.);
  - 29 Muharram 1228 (**1 Feb 1813**), an eclipse, with a note that people mistook it for winter cloud (l. 40521–40524).
- Tambora test: AH 1230–1232 (Dec 1814 – Oct 1817), lines 42351–44895, have **no** dim-sun, haze or twilight entry. They contain only a spring khamsin that "raised yellow dust and murk in the air" on 19 Jumada II 1232 (Apr/May 1817; weekday and tabular dates disagree by a few days), l. 44433–44437. This is a routine dust storm. The Tambora veil, which was widely seen in Europe, left no trace in al-Jabarti, so **his silence in 1808–09 is not evidence of absence**.
- Earthquake baseline: Muharram 1225 (night of 13 Muharram, ≈ 16/17 Feb 1810), l. 37883–37914, records the felt shock and news of damage on Crete and Malta. This is the 16 Feb 1810 Crete earthquake (non-volcanic, outside the window).

### 4. Leads for a human researcher

1. **Câbî Târihi** (Beyhan ed. 2003, vol. 2): check Ramazan 1223 to Rebiülevvel 1224 (Nov 1808 – May 1809). It is near-daily and Istanbul-based, and is the single best untested Ottoman source. Look for "güneş", "duman", "sis", "kar", "soğuk", "kıtlık".
2. **Ibn Bishr, ʿUnwān al-majd**, years 1223–1224. He routinely notes rain (wasmī), floods (sayl), prices and locusts. Use the archive.org djvu.txt with a local download.
3. **Hasan Agha al-ʿAbd** (Damascus) and **al-Shihabi** (Lebanon) for winter 1808/09: snow, cold, sky.
4. **Persian original of Dunbuli** (Tabriz lith. 1241), to verify F6 wording. Also **Nāsikh al-tawārīkh** for AH 1223–1224.
5. **Roda nilometer series** for 1808–1810 (Toussoun 1925; Popper 1951), to quantify F5.
6. **EIC Mocha / Bushire / Basra factory diaries** Dec 1808 – Feb 1809, which might record sky or weather. They are in the BL IOR (G/17, G/29, R/15). QDL images did not load here.
7. ***Babad Gumi*** and Balinese lontar for Saka ~1730 (1808 CE). GVP lists Agung 1808 as VEI 2 from "historical observations". The primary basis should be checked by the Indonesia strand.

#### 7.3 New findings (beyond §1)

**N1. Southern provinces "all received heavy snow, rare in recent years", winter 1809/10; gale of JQ15/1/17.**
- Observer/author: the official 查淳, reporting orally to the emperor on his journey to the capital. Recorded in the Jiaqing emperor's edict to the Grand Council (軍機大臣).
- Places: "several southern provinces" (南數省); specifically 江南 and 安徽, plus the Hongze lake dikes (盱堰).
- Dates observed: 「去冬今春」, i.e. winter 1809/10 and spring 1810. The gale is 正月十七日 = **20 Feb 1810**.
- Document date: JQ15/2/己丑 = **9 Mar 1810**.
- Excerpt: 「又據奏盱堰兩廳甎石各工。被風掣坍一千餘丈至數百丈不等。正月十七日大風。京中亦然。……據稱去冬今春。南數省普得大雪為近年所稀有。江南安徽一帶。春融雪化流入河渠。均匯歸洪澤湖。」
- Translation: "The brick and stone works of the Xu-Yan sub-prefectures were torn down by wind over lengths of several hundred to a thousand zhang. On the 17th of the 1st month there was a great gale, and the same in the capital. … [查淳] said that last winter and this spring several southern provinces all received heavy snow, which has been rare in recent years. In Jiangnan and Anhui the meltwater of spring flows into the rivers and canals and all collects in Hongze Lake."
- Location and repository: 仁宗實錄 JQ15/2; Daizhige `史藏/别史/清实录嘉庆朝实录.txt` line 29535 (simplified e-text).
- Independence: an independent court-level eyewitness statement. It is not copied from gazetteers. The gale date matches Qingshigao 卷44 「十五年正月十七日，臨邑、章丘、新城風霾晝晦」 (Shandong, wind-blown dust darkening the day). That makes the Qingshigao dust-darkness a synoptic gale and dust event, not volcanic.
- Alternatives: an ordinary cold winter of the winter monsoon. The phrase "rare in recent years" is rhetorical, but it is specific.
- Constrains: corroborates F4 (Huang and Huai frozen, canal ice) as a **widespread cold, snowy winter 1809/10** in east China. That is about one year after the ice-core date, which is consistent with, but does not prove, aerosol cooling.

**N2. Beijing: snowy late winter 1809/10, heavy snow on New Year's Eve, 3 Feb 1810.**
- Source: edict of JQ15/1, early in the month (Shilu line 29363; the 1st month began 丙辰朔 = 4 Feb 1810).
- Excerpt: 「昨冬各省普沾雪澤。京師自臘月以後。瑞雪頻番。……歲除日復大霈祥霙。連宵達旦。……是日酉刻立春。」
- Translation: "Last winter every province received snow. In the capital, from the 12th month on, auspicious snow fell again and again … on New Year's Eve heavy snow fell again through the night until dawn … on that day [New Year's Day] at the you hour came the Beginning of Spring."
- Dates: the 12th month JQ14 = 6 Jan – 3 Feb 1810; 歲除 = 3 Feb 1810. The 立春 at the you hour (17–19 h) on 4 Feb 1810 matches the computed 17:48. This confirms the calendar conversion.
- Independence: a court statement. It is laudatory (an occasion for imperial grace), so it may exaggerate.
- Contrast: the winter of 1808/09 had a snow drought (F3).

**N3. Grand Canal boats frozen in after snow and severe cold, early winter 1809/10.**
- Excerpt: 「吳璥等奏、雪後嚴寒、回空阻凍、現在竭力敲冰……現在已入江境幫船、在邳宿一帶境內者、尚有三十八幫。竟至節節凍阻。」
- Translation: "Wu Jing and others report that after snow came severe cold; the returning empty grain fleets are frozen in, and they are now breaking the ice with all their strength … thirty-eight fleets in the Pi–Suqian reach are frozen in at every stage."
- Location: Shilu JQ14/11/庚辰 = **30 Dec 1809**, line 29128.
- Independence: the provincial river-director's memorial as summarised in the edict.
- Alternatives: the edict itself blames poor water management (low water in the Shandong lock canal), so the freezing was partly managerial.
- Constrains: cold by late Dec 1809 in N Jiangsu. Context.

**N4. Frost damage to autumn crops at Guyuan (Gansu/Ningxia), autumn 1809.**
- Excerpt: 「署固原州知州隆德縣知縣呂榮……聯銜稟報該州東北二鄉所屬五千六百五十七村莊秋禾被水、被霜、被雹、成灾七八分不等。經該督確訪該州東北二鄉被霜情形較輕」
- Translation: the acting magistrate reported that the autumn crops of 5,657 villages were damaged by flood, frost and hail, at 70–80% loss; the governor-general found the frost damage lighter, and the magistrate was dismissed for exaggerating.
- Location: Shilu JQ14/10/壬寅 = **22 Nov 1809**, line 28887.
- Alternatives: early autumn frost is common at Guyuan (about 1,750 m), and the report was judged fraudulent.
- Constrains: little.

**N5. Livestock epizootic in Chahar, early 1810.**
- Excerpt: 「以察哈爾牲畜因疫倒斃。貸五品以下官員一年俸銀。兵丁二年餉銀。」 (Shilu JQ15/2/丙申 = **16 Mar 1810**, line 29593).
- Alternatives: animal disease. A severe-winter mass loss of livestock on the steppe (a dzud) is possible but not stated. Context only.

**N6. Rain prayers in Beijing, Jun 1810.**
- Excerpt: 「甲子。命於黑龍潭、及山高水長、設壇祈雨。上詣山高水長祈雨壇拈香。」 (JQ15/5/甲子 = **12 Jun 1810**; again on 庚午 = 18 Jun 1810; Shilu lines 30058 and 30088.)
- Constrains: an early-summer drought in North China in 1810. Context.

**N7. Crop shortfall in western Guangdong, spring 1810.**
- Excerpt: 「以廣東高州廉州一帶歉收。糧價增昂。命酌量平糶。」 (JQ15/3/丁丑 = **26 Apr 1810**, line 29843.)
- Alternatives: piracy blockade. The 1809–10 coastal-pirate crisis and the closure of the coast (封港) are documented in the same Shilu.

**N8. Famine at Xiuyan (Liaoning), JQ15 (1810).**
- Excerpt: 「嘉慶十五年歲大饑」, from a biography in 《岫巖志略》 (Daizhige `史藏/地理/岫岩志略.txt` line 1225).
- Qingshigao 卷44 also has 「十五年秋，寧津、東光、章丘饑」 (autumn 1810, Hebei and Shandong).

**N9. Storm surge or tsunami (海嘯) at Changle (Fuzhou coast), JQ14/5 = 13 Jun – 11 Jul 1809.**
- Excerpt: 「嘉慶十四年五月，海嘯為災，洋嶼城東門至南門圍牆坍塌。」
- Translation: "In the 5th month of JQ14 the sea surged (海嘯) and caused a disaster; the wall of the Yangyu fort collapsed from the east gate to the south gate."
- Location: 《長樂六里志》 (Daizhige `史藏/地理/长乐六里志.txt` line 138). The text cites 《琴江志》 as its source.
- Independence: derivative of a local Qinjiang record.
- Alternatives: in Qing usage 海嘯 is usually a typhoon storm surge. Jun–Jul is typhoon season, and the Gemalan typhoon of the 6th month of JQ14 (Jul–Aug 1809) was a separate event. No earthquake is mentioned.
- Constrains: nothing volcanic. Logged under clue 5.

**N10. Lunar eclipses recorded without description.**
- Shilu: 「戊寅。月食。」 JQ13/9/15 = **3 Nov 1808** (line 26562); 「壬申。月食。」 JQ14/9/15 = **23 Oct 1809** (line 28714).
- No magnitude or colour is given, so no Danjon-scale information.
- Lead: the 欽天監 observation memorials (題本) for these eclipses, held at the First Historical Archives, may describe the colour.
- Note: the 23 Oct 1809 eclipse falls about 8 months after the ice-core date and is the useful one.

**N11. Control case: Indonesian eruptions do reach Chinese texts.**
- 《海國圖志》 (Daizhige `史藏/地理/海国图志.txt` lines 2488–2498) quotes 《東西洋考每月統記傳》 on a Javanese volcano east of Batavia: 「地震霹靂 如萬炮齊轟……火山發煉石 火灰 滿田盈谷……燒民三千人」.
- It then quotes Sumbawa (三巴哇), 「嘉慶二十年三月 其地火山 忽然發焰沖天……巴城之中白日變暗 火灰下如雨雪……耳聽如雷響」.
- Dates: the Java eruption is undated (probably Galunggung 1822 or Papandayan 1772). The Sumbawa eruption, JQ20/3, is Tambora, April 1815.
- So this channel (Chinese-language missionary periodicals of the 1830s, reprinted in 海國圖志) would carry a Batavia-noticed eruption, but only one remembered in the 1830s. It has nothing for 1808–10.

**Notes on term performance:**
- **Two-character compounds are the productive ones in this period's court record:** 祈雪/謝雪, 雪後嚴寒, 被霜, 歉收, 風霾, 晝晦, 雨土, 海嘯, 月食.
- **Single characters (鳴, 霜, 雪, 饑) are mostly noise.** They hit names, idioms, poems and the ritual rules. 雪 also hits 昭雪, "to redress a wrong". Use them only with a date filter and manual reading.
- **The block's veil phrases never occur in the window** (日無光, 日色赤/白, 日赤如血, 日暗, 黃霧, 昏霧, 赤氣, 天赤 …). In this period the Qing court did not record such phenomena at all: there is no Jiaqing 天文志, and the 欽天監 reports were formulaic until May 1809. A null in the Shilu is therefore weak evidence.
- **Terms that should be added for Qing texts:**
  - 雪後嚴寒, 阻凍 / 凍阻 / 冰凌 (canal ice): good for cold winters.
  - 被霜 (the administrative term for frost damage, as opposed to the gazetteer phrase 隕霜).
  - 風霾 and 大風霾: dust storms; needed to identify and exclude non-volcanic haze.
  - 海水 (+ 漲/溢/退) and 潮 (+ 異常/進退): the phrasing in 球陽 that found the 1808 Naha anomaly; 潮溢 and 海溢 did not hit.
  - 雷響 / 炮聲 phrased as 如萬炮齊轟 / 耳聽如雷響: this is how 海國圖志 phrases eruption booms, not the block's 聲如炮.
- **Place-name terms** (呂宋, 爪哇, 噶喇吧 …) produced no natural-event hits in the window. 呂宋 hits are political (Spain/Napoleon in 海國圖志) or trade; 萬丹 and 婆羅 are false hits on Taiwan place names.

### 6. Rescan to mid-1810 with clue terms

Window: **Sept 1808 – July 1810** (Bunka 5/8 – Bunka 7/6). Search terms come from the "## Japanese" and "## Classical Chinese" blocks of notes/eruption_clue_search_terms.md.

**Method.**
- I used the ERI 地震史料集テキストデータベース detailed search. The undocumented URL parameter `text=` filters on the entry text (本文), and `y1/m1/y2/m2` sets the date range. Example: `https://materials.utkozisin.org/search/detail?y1=1808&m1=09&y2=1810&m2=07&text=霜`.
- The window holds about 250 records in total: 3 result pages unfiltered; `text=地震` gives 2 pages.
- The search folds character variants: 焼 matches 燒, and 汐 also matches 夕 (noise). It matches the full entry text, not only the displayed snippet. Entries with day and month unknown (99/99) are included only by some range queries.
- Every quotation below is the database's OCR text, which it marks [未校訂] (unproofread). None is image-verified.
- Calendar for 1810: Bunka 7 began 4 Feb 1810. Database anchors: 文化七年八月二十五日 = 1810-09-23 and 四月二十五日 = 1810-05-27. Ryukyu used the Qing calendar: 嘉慶15年 began 4 Feb 1810; 2月 = 5 Mar, 3月 = 4 Apr, 8月 = 30 Aug 1810.

#### 6.3 New items in the extended window (fields as in §2)

**C1. Kawamata (Fukushima), cold spells in late spring and summer 1809. Clue 9; weak.**
- Source: 〔累世年鑑〕, a local annal/diary, Kawamata, Date district. Printed in 新収日本地震史料 続補遺, p. 464 ff. OCR.
- Entries:
  - 文化六年四月七–八日 = 20–21 May 1809: 「朝ゟ八日も地風甚冷気両日霧雨」 ("from morning, and on the 8th too, a very cold ground wind; drizzle both days").
  - 四月廿六日 = 8 Jun 1809: 「昼頃ゟ甚冷気」 ("very cold from about noon").
  - 六月四日 = 16 Jul 1809: 「朝少冷し…袷着」 ("somewhat cold in the morning… wore a lined garment").
- Independence: a single source.
- Alternative explanation: *yamase* (cold NE wind) is normal in Tōhoku early summer. There is no yield record.
- Constrains: at most, it is consistent with a cool 1809 summer in southern Tōhoku.

**C2. Kawamata, 文化五年十一月中旬 (≈27 Dec 1808 – 5 Jan 1809). Clue 0/9; very weak.**
- Same source, 続補遺 p. 463, DB ID J2402771, OCR: 「此霜月中旬餘程之地震二度小地震七度地震ニ而小雪降出候而も快晴之様子ニ候 常ハ地震有之候ハゝ雨天ニ相成候所 当冬ハ別段相見候」
- Translation: "In the middle of this 11th month there were two fairly strong and seven small quakes. Even when light snow began to fall, it looked like clear weather. Usually rain follows earthquakes, but this winter looks different."
- This is a folk weather–earthquake remark. It is **not** a veil report. Listed only because it falls in the core window.

**C3. Kawamata, 27 May 1810 (文化七年四月二十五日): 「四月廿五日昼頃地震山鳴る」. Clue 1; local.**
- Source: 続補遺 p. 467, DB ID J2402805.
- Translation: "Around noon, an earthquake; the mountains rumbled."
- Most likely the sound of a local earthquake. It does not constrain a distant eruption.

**C4. Deshima/Edo, 10 May 1810 (文化七年四月八日 entry dated the 10th). Clue 5; local.**
- Source: 〔長崎オランダ商館日記 五〕 (Japanese translation of the Deshima dagregister; the opperhoofd was in Edo on the court journey). 日本の歴史地震史料 拾遺 別巻 p. 985, DB ID J2700693, OCR of a modern translation.
- Text: 「十日 火曜日 終日雨。異常に暖かであった。すでに八日の日かるい地震を感じていたが、今日夕刻七時ごろ長い地震の揺れを感じた。」
- Translation: "10th, Tuesday. Rain all day. Unusually warm. A light quake had already been felt on the 8th; this evening about 7 o'clock a long earthquake was felt."
- The rest of the entry says it was the strongest quake the writer had felt in 11 years in Japan.
- A local quake. Its value is that it shows the vol. 5 translation carries daily weather, which supports reading vols. 3–5 in full.

**C5. Ryukyu, 1810 drought. Clue 9.**
- Source: 伊江親方日々記, as summarised in 山里純一「災害と呪術」 (Univ. Ryukyus 2009, file 17320100-4). Not quoted in full there.
- Entries: 嘉慶15年2月7日 = 11 Mar 1810; 3月1日 = 4 Apr 1810; 8月13日 = 11 Sep 1810. They record a long drought, with phrases like 「世上驚入候処」 and 「世上喜悦」 when rain came.
- No rain prayer (祈雨) is mentioned for 1810.
- Constrains: nothing specific; spring droughts are common in Okinawa.

#### 5.1 Method

- **Corpus.** I checked out 47 OpenITI text files from the 1225/1250/1275/1300/1325/1350AH repos. They cover every history, biographical dictionary, travel text, memoir and diwan of authors who died 1224–1350 AH found in those repos.

  - Malay block: no corpus reachable. The Malay Concordance Project and *Babad Gumi* are both blocked by robots.txt.

#### 5.3 New or extended findings (labels as in §1: modern digital transcription, not image-verified)

**F7. Flood (*sayl*) at Mecca and Jidda, Safar 1225.** Clue 6 / clue 9.
- **Observer:** al-Jabarti, Cairo, reporting news from the Hijaz.
- **Place:** Mecca and Jidda.
- **Dates:** event in Safar 1225, i.e. **7 Mar – 4 Apr 1810** (1 Safar = Wednesday per al-Jabarti, i.e. 7 Mar 1810). The news arrived at the start of Rabiʿ II 1225, i.e. about 5 May 1810.
- **Location:** OpenITI Jabarti Shamela0011998 l. 38076–38079; ed. Dar al-Jil vol. 3 p. 293.
- **Excerpt:** فيه وصلت الأخبار من البلاد الحجازية بنزول سيل عظيم حصل منه ضرر كثير وهدم دورا كثيرة بمكة وجدة واتلف كثيرا من البضائع للتجار حكوا أنه هدم بمكة خاصة ستمائة دار وكان ذلك في شهر صفر.
- **Translation:** "In it news came from the Hijaz of a great flood which did much damage and destroyed many houses in Mecca and Jidda and ruined many of the merchants' goods; they related that in Mecca alone it destroyed six hundred houses. That was in the month of Safar."
- **Independence:** this is a single, second-hand report. The Hijaz chronicles (e.g. Dahlan's *Khulāṣat al-kalām*, or Ibn Bishr for the Wahhabi side) could confirm it, but neither was reachable.
- **Alternatives:** Mecca has frequent historic flash floods from winter–spring convective rain (Wadi Ibrahim). This is a hydro-meteorological event, not a volcanic mud flow.
- **Constraint:** none on the eruption. At most it is evidence of a wet winter–spring 1810 in western Arabia.

**F8. The 16/17 Feb 1810 earthquake, felt in Cairo, with damage in Crete, Malta shipping and Latakia.** Clue 5. Extends the earlier note.
- **Observed:** night of Saturday 13 Muharram 1225, about the 7th hour of the night, i.e. **night of 16/17 Feb 1810** (1 Muharram 1225 = Monday 5 Feb 1810). There was a weaker aftershock on the afternoon of the Saturday, and a rumoured repeat on the night of 22/23 Feb 1810.
- **Location:** l. 37883–37914 and 38002–38008 (vol. 3 pp. 285–289).
- **Excerpts:**
  - "حصلت زلزلة عجيبة وارتجت منها الجهات ثلاث درجات متواليات واستمرت نحو أربع دقائق". Translation: "a remarkable earthquake; the quarters shook in three successive pulses lasting about four minutes".
  - The news report: "وصلت الأخبار من البلاد الرومية والشامية … وحصل في بلاد كريت اتلافات كثيرة … وتكسر على ساحل مالطة عدة مراكب وحصل أيضا باللاذقية خسف … الأرض انشقت". Translation: "news arrived from the Ottoman and Syrian lands … much destruction in Crete … several ships were wrecked on the coast of Malta; there was also subsidence at Latakia … the earth split open".
- **Interpretation:** this is the well-known 16 Feb 1810 M≈7.5 Hellenic-arc earthquake. It is tectonic, not volcanic, and has no bearing on the eruption.
- **Also in the entry:** the Thursday 18 Muharram entry (≈ 22/23 Feb 1810) says that Cairenes who camped outdoors suffered "cold beyond description, because the sun was in Aquarius, the middle of winter" (l. 37909–37911). This is ordinary seasonal cold for a night in the open, not anomaly evidence.

**F9. Unusual early snow near Erevan, autumn 1809 (Persian source, via Arabic translation).** Clue 9.
- **Source:** Dunbuli, *Maʾāthir*, ch. 119 (AH 1224 campaign toward Kökcha and Ganja), l. 5825–5833 (print pp. 226–227, tag PageV01P268).
- **Excerpt (Arabic translation):** "ولما انقشعت سحابة جيش الصيف، واقترب وقت برودة الهواء ووصول الشتاء … وفجأة، تصاحب وصول العريضة مع سقوط الثلوج، فالذى كان قد حدث فى ذلك اليوم، لم يكن قد حدث فى العهد كله، وفى تلك الشدة نفسها من نزول الثلوج وثورة الهواء والجو …"
- **Translation:** "When the cloud of the summer army had cleared and the time of cold air and the arrival of winter drew near … suddenly the arrival of the petition coincided with a snowfall; what happened that day had not happened in the whole era; and in that same severity of falling snow and turmoil of the air and sky [the army set out] for Tehran."
- **Date:** not given in the passage. From context it falls after the Jumada II 1224 (Jul–Aug 1809) operations, at the onset of winter, so about **Oct–Nov 1809**. Uncertain to ±1 month.
- Earlier in the same chapter (l. 5776–5777), the camp near Zakan (Ganja district) suffered "البرد والبرد إلى أقصى درجة". The translator's wording is ambiguous: it is either "cold and hail" or "cold and cold". It refers to the 1809 campaign season.
- **Alternatives:** court-history hyperbole ("never in the whole era") and a normal early-winter storm on the Armenian plateau. It needs the Persian original and the Russian campaign journals.
- **Constraint:** weak. It is compatible with the cold Eurasian winters 1809/10 reported in the literature, but it is not diagnostic.

#### 8.3 Assessment for the Mar–Jun 1808 hypothesis

Chinese-language sources available here show nothing for Mar–Aug 1808 that points to a large eruption:
- no veil;
- no unexplained booms;
- no ash;
- no pumice on the Fujian, Taiwan or Ryukyu coasts;
- no reports of an eruption in Southeast Asia.

The season was dry in spring in North China and wet in late summer, with locusts in Jiangsu and Shandong. The only cold oddity is the 4th-month (late Apr–May 1808) killing frost at Yueqing, Zhejiang.

The seismic and sea items (B6–B8) cluster in Jul–Aug 1808 in the Fujian, Taiwan and Ryukyu region and are most plausibly tectonic or weather-driven.

The caveats of §7 apply:
- The Qing court did not record sky optics in this period.
- Mainland gazetteers are poorly covered here.
- REACHES and Zhang De'er's compendium remain the way to close the gap.

**Term lesson.** Add 地大震 / 大震 and 潮 + 異常/進退 to the Classical Chinese block. Both found real events that the block's own terms missed.

#### 7.2 Items needing comment (fields as in §2)

**7a. Awa (Naka district, Fukui village): a water rise of "五尺" (about 1.5 m). Clue 5; almost certainly the 4 Dec 1808 tsunami, misdated by Musha.**
- **Source:** ＊〔福井村志〕, a 20th-century village gazetteer. Printed in 増訂大日本地震史料 vol. 3, p. 173. DB ID J0300400.
- **Text (OCR):**
  - 綱文 (Musha's headnote): 「文化五年閏六月十七日（西暦一八〇八、八、八、）阿波國那智郡地震ヒ津浪ヲ伴へり、」
  - 本文 (entry text): 「文化五年閏十月○大カ十七日地震、朝五尺高く來る、（武者註）文化五年ハ六月閏ナリ、」
- **Translation:** "Bunka 5, intercalary 10th month [Musha: 大 (?)], 17th: earthquake; in the morning [the sea] came 5 shaku high. (Musha's note: Bunka 5 had its intercalary month in the 6th month.)"
- **Analysis:**
  - Bunka 5 had no intercalary 10th month. Musha moved the entry to 閏6月17日 = 8 Aug 1808.
  - Dropping the spurious 「閏」 gives **十月十七日 = 4 Dec 1808**. That is the exact day of the small tsunami on the facing Kii and Tosa coasts, and "morning" matches the Tosa account (「十七日 朝曇当日手結浦ノ湊へ鈴波入り」).
  - The coast is that of present-day Anan, on the Kii Channel.
- **Independence:** an independent fourth site for the 4 Dec 1808 tsunami.
- **Constrains:** **Nothing for Mar–Aug 1808.** It slightly strengthens the record of the Dec 1808 event, which Tsuji 2025 treats as a local Nankai tsunami earthquake.
- **Check:** look at the p. 173 image and at 福井村志 itself. Tsuji 2025 may already cite this source.

**7b. Kunashiri (Kuril Islands): the Sendai-domain guard diary, 13 and 23 Jul 1808. Negative but important as a lead.**
- **Source:** 〔高屋養庵による仙台藩クナシリ島警護記録〕, ed. 高柳義男 (宝文堂, 1987). Printed in 日本の歴史地震史料 拾遺, p. 208. DB ID J2600833. OCR.
- **Text:** 「同十六日 明方ゟ雨ふり風強く 五時地震致 余程強御座仆（候） 同廿六日 朝 曇り四時ゟ快晴也 同刻地震致仆 此日も暖和ニて一重物着用 夜中迄同断」
- **Dates:** 文化五年六月十六日 = 13 Jul 1808; 六月廿六日 = 23 Jul 1808.
- **Translation:** "16th: rain and strong wind from dawn; at the 5th hour an earthquake, quite strong. 26th: cloudy in the morning, clear from the 4th hour; an earthquake at the same time. This day too was mild, and we wore single-layer clothes; the same until night."
- **Constrains:** nothing on its own: two felt quakes, mild weather, no haze or ash.
- **Why it matters:** it is a **daily diary kept on a Kuril island in summer 1808**, the area relevant to a second North Pacific eruption (Yukon 1809 dacite). The full published diary, presumably covering 1808 and perhaps 1809, should be read for ash, booms, haze, pumice or red skies. Top new lead for the North Pacific question.

#### 7.4 Verdict for the Mar–Jun 1808 hypothesis

- **No Japanese or Ryukyu evidence of an eruption or an aerosol veil between Mar and Aug 1808.**
- In particular there is no red or dim sun, haze, ash, booms, pumice or unusual sea level other than the misdated Awa item.
- The database's sky-colour baseline (§6.2) shows no red-sun entries anywhere in 1807–1808. The first ones are the Edo (Jan 1809) and Tottori (Feb 1809) records.
- **Caveat:** the window holds only 47 records, mostly earthquake-day snippets, so this is a thin negative.
- If an Mar–Jun 1808 eruption produced a veil over Japan in spring or summer 1808, either the earthquake compilers did not copy it or it was too faint to remark on.
- The total lunar eclipse of 3 Nov 1808 (文化五年九月十五日, visible at dusk in Japan) would be a good test of a mid-1808 aerosol. A dark eclipse would support an early-1808 eruption. Shogunate and Hazama-family eclipse logs are the lead for this.

#### 6.3 Finding

**F10. Late, deficient Nile flood; public rain prayers (*istisqāʾ*) for the Nile, Cairo, 20–23 Aug 1808.** Clue 9. Modern digital transcription, not image-verified.

- **Observer:** al-Jabarti, Cairo, eyewitness.
- **Dates:**
  - Sat 27 Jumada II 1223 = 15 Misra = **20 Aug 1808**: the Nile fell about five fingers.
  - **Sun 21 Aug**: rain prayers at the mosque of ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ.
  - **Mon 22 Aug**: the prayers were repeated, with the Copts also present.
  - Night of Mon/Tue (22/23 Aug): the river rose and the *wafāʾ* (flood reaching the full-Nile mark) was proclaimed.
  - **Wed 1 Rajab = 19 Misra = 24 Aug 1808**: the canal dam was cut, and the water flowed weakly.
- **Location:** OpenITI `1237Jabarti.CajaibAthar.Shamela0011998-ara1` l. 36741–36770; Dar al-Jil ed. vol. 3 p. ~239–240.
- **Excerpt:** فلما كان يوم السبت سابع عشرينه وخامس عشر مسرى القبطي نقص النيل نحو خمسة أصابع وانكشف الحجر الراقد الذي عند فم الخليج تحت الحجر القائم فضج الناس ورفعو الغلال من الرقع والعرصات والسواحل وانزعجت الخلائق بسبب شحة النيل في العام الماضي وهيفان الزرع … فقال لهم: اعملوا استسقاء … يصلون به صلاة الاستسقاء ويدعون الله ويستغفرونه ويتضرعون إليه في زيادة النيل.
- **Translation:** "On Saturday the 27th, the 15th of Coptic Misra, the Nile fell about five fingers and the 'lying stone' at the canal mouth below the 'standing stone' was exposed. The people clamoured, grain was withdrawn from the markets, granaries and quays, and everyone was alarmed because of the scarcity of the Nile last year and the withering of the crops … [the Pasha] told them: 'Perform the rain prayer' … they prayed the *istisqāʾ*, invoking God, seeking forgiveness and imploring Him for the rising of the Nile."
- **Independence:** al-Jabarti only, and I found no other source. The Roda nilometer readings (Toussoun 1925; Popper 1951) would quantify it.
- **Alternatives:**
  - The 1807 flood was already deficient ("the scarcity of the Nile last year"). So the 1808 shortfall is the second poor year running, not a new anomaly. Ethiopian-monsoon variability and ENSO are the usual drivers.
  - Short pauses or falls in the rise in mid-August occur in ordinary years too, and *wafāʾ* followed within three days. The flood was late and weak, not a failure.
  - It was followed by an excessive flood in 1809 (F5).
- **What it constrains:**
  - It is *consistent with*, but not diagnostic of, a weakened Jun–Aug 1808 Ethiopian/East African monsoon. A tropical eruption in Mar–Jun 1808 could produce that weakening.
  - The previous year's deficit and the high 1809 flood argue for natural variability.
  - It gives no timing information on any eruption.

#### 6.4 Net result

For Mar–Aug 1808 there is no volcanic-specific clue in al-Jabarti, Dunbuli or the other reachable OpenITI texts. The only natural-event item is the late, weak 1808 Nile flood with its rain prayers (F10). Sensitivity is low: al-Jabarti's coverage of these months is very thin, and Dunbuli's months are undated.
