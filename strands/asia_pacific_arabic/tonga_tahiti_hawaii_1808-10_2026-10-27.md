## 3. Hawaiʻi: Archibald Campbell, Jan 1809 to early 1810 (Gutenberg #46468; OCR-derived e-text)
- Arrival 27 Jan [1809]: "fine weather, with favourable winds"; Mauna Kea "perpetually covered with snow"; Mauna Loa: "We did not see any flame or smoke issuing from the crater" [e-text].
- After early May 1809 (after the death of Keliʻimaikaʻi, "Terremytee"), Honolulu harbour: a large waterspout on a "fine" day "with a clear atmosphere, and nearly calm"; when it broke "the sea in the harbour was raised at least three feet upon the beach"; "Hundreds of dead fish were picked up upon the reef" [e-text]. This is a waterspout, a weather event, **not** a tsunami or volcanic sign (inference); recorded so nobody re-flags it.
- Nov 1809: land grant; taro and yams growing normally. No haze, odd sun, cold, famine or sickness noted. Weak negative (Campbell had no interest in weather).
