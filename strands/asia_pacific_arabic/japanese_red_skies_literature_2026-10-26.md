# Japanese-language literature on the winter 1808/09 red skies (26 Oct 2026)

Thread: "Japanese writing on the 1809 red skies". Question: has anyone writing in Japanese linked the red twilights of Bunka 5–6 (Edo, daily, 9 Jan to c. 16–25 Jan 1809, image-verified; Tottori 5 Feb 1809, OCR only) to a volcanic eruption or to the 1809 ice-core sulfate, or catalogued them at all? Prior Japanese work (not repeated here): notes/asian_arabic_round4.md parts II, V.japan, VI.japan; notes/pacific_china_pass3.md §3.

## Answer

**No one found.** In Japanese-language scholarship reachable from here (J-STAGE full search, metsoc.jp *Tenki* PDFs, KAKEN project records, JpGU/EMS abstracts, NIPR repository, Japanese web), nobody connects the Edo or Tottori red skies to a volcano or to the 1809 sulfate, and nobody catalogues them apart from Musha 1943 (as earthquake omens). Verification: search-result and abstract level; full texts of two key papers could not be opened (see "Needs William").

Three things the search did turn up:

1. **Japanese ice-core work does name the 1809 event, without any historical link.** Kawano (河野美香), Fujii, Kusakabe and Fukuoka 1999, 南極H15雪氷コアに記録された過去300年の火山シグナル, *Seppyo* 61(1):13–24, doi:10.5331/seppyo.61.13. A 120 m core from H15, Mizuho Plateau, East Antarctica (69°05'S, 40°47'E): 19 non-sea-salt sulfate peaks; the English abstract lists "an unknown volcano in 1809" among the key signals. Verification: abstract only (J-STAGE page); PDF robots-blocked here and from William's Mac shell. It adds an independent East Antarctic (Dronning Maud/Mizuho sector) core with the 1809 peak; it does not discuss Japanese records. Kawano 2000, *Seppyo* 62(3):197 (review, 氷床コアに保存された火山起源物質) mentions no 1809 source in its abstract. Igarashi et al. (RIKEN/NIPR), JpGU 2008 W155-006 (Dome Fuji dating, 1260–2001) names Tambora at 12.5 m but does not mention 1809 in the abstract.

2. **A diary-based summer temperature series for western Japan covers 1809, but nobody has discussed that year.** Hirano (平野淳平), Mikami (三上岳彦) and Zaiki (財城真寿美) 2018, 広島の古日記天候記録による1779年以降の夏季気温の復元, *Geographical Review of Japan Ser. A* 91(4):311–327, doi:10.4157/grj.91.311 (free PDF on J-STAGE). July–August mean daily maximum temperature, Hiroshima, from 1779, reconstructed from counts of rainy days. The abstract names cool 1780s and 1830s–40s and warm 1820s and 1850s; no single year, no volcano. Also EMS2017-272 (same authors, 1779–1871). KAKEN 16K16350 says results were shared via the Japan-Asia Climate Data Program (JCDP). **The 1809 and 1810 values are in the paper's figure/table, not read yet.** Caveat: rainy-day counts measure wetness as much as heat, so a single year means little.

3. **Survey of Japanese diary-climate literature: 1808–1810 never singled out.** Kondo 1985 (*Tenki* 32:241, Tenpō cold summers), Kondo 1985 (*Tenki* 32:363, rice yield 150 yr), Kondo 1989 (*Tenki* 36:515), Kondo 1994 (*Tenki* 41:465) and Kondo's web essays K226 and M107 on eruptions and cool summers go from Asama/Laki 1783 to Tambora 1815 and Tenpō; none mentions 1809. Kondo 1988 (*J. Climate* 1:775) is the standard English paper on eruptions and Tōhoku famines (not opened; table starts with large known eruptions). Mikami 2018 NIPR symposium slides, Mikami JpGU 2016, Zaiki 2025 (Teikoku Shoin *ChiReKo*), Sho/Kamatani/Hirano presentations, Hirano KAKEN 19K01163 (typhoon tracks), two high-school poster studies (Ikeda village diary 1758–1912; Nijō household diary): none mentions 1808–1810. Matsuda & Homma 2024, *Seppyo* 86(3):167 (snow records from 日本氣象資料 1939 and 日本の雪害史) has a red-snow category, Hokuriku/Tōhoku only; years not checked.
   - Japanese aurora/赤気 scholarship (Iwahashi 2022/2025 on 1770; Kokugakuin and Hitotsubashi pieces; Yamaguchi archives astronomical records, ASJ 2026a Z105a, 80 entries 1680–1862, half comets) has nothing in 1806–1811.
   - Japanese popular writing (ja.wikipedia 夏のない年, 1808年; Tambora and "1831 mystery eruption" news pieces) never mentions the 1809 event. National Geographic Japan 2014 news 「19世紀の地球低温期、原因は謎の噴火」 (on Guevara-Murua et al. 2014) exists but is robots-blocked; by its title it reports the Bogotá/Lima study, not Japan.

So the statement for the blog stays as it is: the Edo red skies are "not found in the literature we reviewed" in a volcanic context; Musha 1943 printed them as earthquake omens.

- The 1809 peak is the second-largest nssSO4 peak in 300 years at H15. Its peak concentration is about 0.63 times Tambora's (226 vs 356 ng/g). That is a peak concentration, not a deposition flux, so the ratio is only rough. Next largest: Cosigüina 1835 at 181 ng/g.
- **Dating:** the annual-layer (ECM) count puts the 1809 layer at **1810**. The text (p. 18) says the ECM age of the Tambora layer agrees with acid deposition in 1816, one year after the eruption (citing Dai et al. 1991). The table, though, prints 1815 for that layer, so the ECM ages carry about ±1 year. A deposition year of 1810 for the 1809 event fits Cole-Dai et al. 2009, who put Antarctic fallout in mid-1810.
- Pages 22–23: "the older prominent signal q corresponds to unknown 1809 eruption which has been found in cores from many sites in Greenland and Antarctica (e.g. Clausen and Hammer, 1988; Dai et al., 1991). Kohno et al. (1996) have discussed these prominent signals of p and q in detail." There is no discussion of a source, a hemisphere, or historical records.

### Hirano, Mikami & Zaiki 2018, Geogr. Rev. Japan A 91:311–327. Verification: text layer read; Fig. 9 digitised by Claude from the embedded raster image (inferred values, ±0.2 °C reading error on top of the paper's standard error)

- **Source diary:** 『村上家乗日記』, kept by the Murakami family, retainers of the Tōjō Asano house (Hiroshima domain). It runs from 1778 to 1881 in 219 volumes, with weather recorded almost daily from 1779 to 1871.
- **Months excluded because the writer was away from Hiroshima** (p. 313):
  - July: 1783, **1808**, 1828, 1842, 1862.
  - August: 1799, **1807, 1808**, 1828, 1842.
  - So Hiroshima has **no July or August value for 1808**.
- **Method:** July and August mean daily maximum temperature is estimated from the number of rainy days. The regression standard errors are ±0.95 °C for July and ±0.82 °C for August. The 1981–2010 normals are about 30.8 °C for July and 32.5 °C for August.

- **Reading:**
  - Midsummer 1809 at Hiroshima was not cool. If anything it was warm and dry, with few rainy days.
  - The very cool, wet July is 1807, more than a year before the inferred eruption, so it is unrelated.
  - This fits the pattern of the other proxies: the 1809 eruption's cooling falls mainly in 1810 and later.
  - Caveat: the method responds to rainfall frequency and the rainy season (baiu). It is one site in western Japan, and the paper itself discusses only decades.
- No earlier finding needs changing. The "hint of a cool early summer in southern Tōhoku in 1809" (round 4) is neither supported nor contradicted, because Hiroshima is in a different climate region.

## Update 2, 26 Oct ~21:30 UTC: Kohno et al. 1996 read (William's download KJ00000767984.pdf)

**Citation.** Kohno, M., Fukuoka, T., Fujii, Y. & Kusakabe, M. 1996. "Volcanic records and dating of the upper half of the H15 ice core from Mizuho Plateau, East Antarctica." *Proc. NIPR Symp. Polar Meteorol. Glaciol.* 10:36–54. The paper is in English.

**Verification.** I read the text layer of all 19 pages, and checked pp. 49 and 51 (Fig. 6 and the key paragraphs) against the page images.

- **Core.** H15 is at 69°04'46"S, 40°46'54"E, 1050 m above sea level. It was drilled to 120.2 m by the 32nd Japanese Antarctic Research Expedition in Sep–Oct 1991.
- **Two acid peaks, at water-equivalent depths of 46.7 m and 48.3 m.** Peak excess sulfate is 350 and 200 ng/g, equal to 7 and 4 µequiv H+/kg.
- **Deposition, integrated over the H+ peaks (p. 49):**
  - Tambora: 46.46–46.90 m, **68 kg/km² H2SO4**.
  - 1809: 48.12–48.27 m, **44 kg/km² H2SO4**.
  - The authors give the ratio as 1.5. As 1809 ÷ Tambora that is **0.65**.
  - My caveat: the 1809 integration window is only 0.15 m w.e. against 0.44 m for Tambora. That is about 0.6 years against 1.7 years at their 26 g/cm²/yr accumulation rate. In Fig. 6 the 1809 H+ and sulfate rise looks broader than that window, so the 1809 total may be underestimated. This is my reading of the figure.
- **The dating is not independent (pp. 49–50).**
  - The two layers were first dated to 1828 and 1822 by the firn-densification model, and to 1826 and 1821 by counting ECM peaks.
  - The authors attribute this offset of about 10 years to a hiatus marked by three melt layers at 15–26 m w.e.
  - They assign the layers to 1815 and 1809 because the core shows the same pair of peaks, with the same Tambora-larger ratio (1.5–2), as other cores. They also cite Machida et al. 1995.
  - So H15 adds a sulfate ratio, but it gives no independent date for the 1809 event.
  - Kawano 1999's later "ECM age 1810" comes from a recalibrated count and should be read the same way.
  - Kawano 1999 also prints different depths, 44.29 and 45.78 m w.e., so the depth scale was revised between the two papers.
- **Ash (p. 50).** The authors note the andesitic fine ash in the 1809–10 and 1816 layers at the South Pole and Dome C (De Angelis et al. 1985; Palais et al. 1990). They also report "fine ash-like materials (~10 µm) have been found in the corresponding layers of the H15 core", but these were never chemically analysed. **This is a possible new lead:** a fourth Antarctic 1809 tephra sample, if the NIPR archive still holds the H15 core. I found no later paper analysing it (searched J-STAGE 26 Oct; not exhaustive).
- **δ18O.** It decreases after Tambora but not after 1809. The authors call this not interpretable.
- **History.** They quote the standard line that "a volcanic event in 1809 has not been described in the historical literature", citing Dai 1991 and Langway 1995. There is no Japanese or other historical material.

**Comparison with the Antarctic sulfate map** (data/antarctic_sulfate_1809_vs_tambora.csv; H15 row added 26 Oct):
- The H15 ratio of 0.65 is above the Antarctic median of about 0.47. It is close to Dyer Plateau (0.60), DML03 (0.76) and DML07/17 (0.49–0.50).
- H15 is 3,590 km from Mt Melbourne (Claude's calculation), so it says nothing about the Talos Dome excess.
- It fits Cole-Dai 2009 in giving 1809 about half to two-thirds of Tambora in Antarctica.
- Units differ: H15 is H2SO4, the other rows are sulfate. The ratio is unaffected.
