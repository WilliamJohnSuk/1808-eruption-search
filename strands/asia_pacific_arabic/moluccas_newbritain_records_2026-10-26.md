## Short answer

- **Not found.** No record of ash fall, darkness, booms, roof or crop damage, dead livestock or fish, or a sea wave from the Moluccas, northern Sulawesi, Mindanao, New Britain or the Solomons, Nov 1808 to mid-1810, in any source reached. This is a weak negative: the colonial record for exactly these places and years is thin and partly lost (below).
- **Why the record is thin (source statements, OCR):** Van der Crab (1862) p. 146 says the Amboina clove harvests of 1809, 1810 and 1811 are unknown "uithoofde de archieven van het toenmalig Engelsche bestuur niet meer voorhanden zijn" (because the archives of the British administration no longer exist). Amboina fell to the British on 18-19 Feb 1810, Banda and Ternate in Aug 1810. The *Bataviasche Koloniale Courant* began only on 5 Jan 1810, and Manila had no periodical before Aug 1811 (insular_asia_pass3 §2.2). There were no Protestant missionaries in the Moluccas, Minahasa, Sangihe or Melanesia in 1808-10 (Kam reached Ambon in 1815; the LMS and NZG missions came later), so the "mission record" route does not exist for these years (inference from mission chronology).
- **Two small positives against heavy ash on Banda (OCR, weak):** the BKC of 22 Jun 1810 reports spice ships arriving at Surabaya with a "rijke lading van Banda" (rich cargo from Banda); and on 30 Mar 1810 the government auctioned "beschadigde noten muscaat" (damaged nutmeg), which the context ties to storage spoilage in the case against the ex-governor of Amboina (OCR "Cramsen"), which speaks of spoilage ("bederf") of stored nuts, not of the weather.
- **Bleeker 1856 tables, checked as page images (26 Oct, William's screenshots): no help.** The archive.org "page" numbers I gave were scan leaves; the printed pages are 76-83, 118-125, 180-187 of the Amboina-Uliasers part. (a) **Clove yield of Amboina and the Uliasers, 1675-1855** (printed p. 120, page image), Amsterdam pounds: 1804 142,895; 1805 174,123; 1806 385,968; 1807 255,530; **1808 163,872** (third digit hard to read, 163,872 or 168,872); **1809, 1810, 1811 "onbekend"**; 1812 288,411; 1813 659,370; 1814 793,368; 1815 811,000. So the post-eruption years are exactly the missing ones, and 1808 (harvested before or around the eruption) is low but within the run of low years since 1804; year-to-year swings of 3-10x are normal in the series (e.g. 1817 26,442; 1820 82,077). Nothing diagnostic. (b) **Amboina population minimum in 1810** (printed p. 78, page image): Bleeker gives 18,299 in 1803 and blames the monopoly, forced deliveries, "Hevige epidemiën" from time to time, and under Daendels "een nieuw middel van ontvolking", mass recruitment of Ambonese into the army, then: "Men mag aannemen, dat de bevolking van Amboina in het jaar 1810 haar minimum van getalsterkte bereikte." By 1820 it was again 24-25,000 (p. 79). No natural disaster is mentioned, and there is no 1808-1810 census figure. (c) The "minimum at the start of this century" (printed p. 182) is **Saparua, not Banda** (my earlier OCR attribution was wrong); its table (p. 183) jumps from 7,217 in 1803 to 10,084 in 1833, no 1808-10 figure. Verdict: negative; the tables cannot show 1809 damage because 1809-1811 are missing.
- **Off-strand find (catalogue):** BL IOR/F/4/346/8092 contains meteorological observations taken at Penang (Prince of Wales Island) Nov 1807-Dec 1808 and Sep 1809-Feb 1810, with hospital statistics for Banda, Amboina and Malacca 1802-08. This may be one of the unidentified "Malaysian" temperature series behind Chenoweth 2001 (inference; see insular_asia_pass3 §4).

## 1. Ships' sailing directions: ships in the eastern islands Dec 1808 to 1810

Source: Horsburgh, *India Directory* vol. 2 (1841 ed.), archive.org `indiadirectoryor02hors`, in-book search (OCR/summary). Horsburgh compiled ships' remarks with dates, and he does record eruptions when ships saw them: p. 710, the *Scaleby Castle*, sailing 27 Nov 1814: "During the night, one of the most violent explosions of the volcano occurred ever known at Ternate." So the method can catch an eruption, and the absence below means something, though not much.

Ships passing through or beside the unwatched zone in the window (all OCR):
- **Castlereagh, Capt. Gardner**, "left Bombay in November, 1808, went through Allass Strait", north of Flores, then **Pitt Passage and Dampier Strait** (NW New Guinea/Waigeo) to China, "seventy days" (p. 621); "in December, 1808, passed between Rusa Raji and the coast of Flores" (p. 688); "saw Velthoens Island at day-break January 1st, 1809" (p. 617). This ship was in the Banda Sea and off Halmahera/New Guinea in late Dec 1808-Jan 1809, i.e. inside the inferred eruption window. No remark about ash, haze, smoke or sounds. This is a Bombay country ship, not the EIC *Lord Castlereagh* (Capt. Kymer, Trincomalee 11 Dec 1808, Bengal) whose journal is IOR/L/MAR/B/188C; no log for Gardner's ship was found.
- **Minerva and Ardassier**: "left Ceylon, December 11th, 1808"; east point of Java 5 Jan 1809; Sumbawa banks "at daylight, January 9th, 1809" (pp. 148, 660). No remark.
- **Cumbrian, Capt. Tate**: "close to [the Catherine Islands, Gilolo Passage] in the night of August 26th, 1809" (p. 625). The BL journal IOR/L/MAR/B/83B starts only 23 Nov 1809.
- **Fox frigate**, Sep 1809, an island group near 2°35'S (p. 554). **HMS Psyche**, 1809, observations at Surigao, Mindanao (p. 536; log ADM 52/3787 already on the Kew list). **Sibbald, Capt. Forbes**, bound for Amboina, Flores Sea 16-20 Apr 1810 (pp. 685, 688). **HMS Baracouta**, Flores Sea, 19 Sep 1810 (p. 660).
