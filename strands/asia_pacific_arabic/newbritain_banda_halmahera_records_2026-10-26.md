## Short answer

- **No eruption, ash fall, darkness, unexplained booms or sea wave** is recorded for New Britain, the Banda arc or Halmahera between Nov 1808 and mid-1810 in any source reached. Weak negative, for the reasons given in B48 (thin and partly lost record).
- **Banda Api specifically: a better negative.** Verbeek's Banda volcano chronology (Jaarboek van het mijnwezen 1901, scan p. 473, OCR) goes from "1778. Uitbarsting? (volgens Junghuhn)" straight to "1816. Eruptie en hevige aardbevingen, 11-14 ...", and says a "1798" date elsewhere is a misprint for 1778. The same page lists the Batavia papers 1810-1828 (Bataviasche Koloniale Courant, Java Government Gazette 1812-1816, Bataviasche Courant) among its sources. Verbeek's 1908 Moluccas report (Jaarboek 1908, `jaarboekvanhetm05gebigoog`) has no hit for 1808 or 1810, and its 1809 hits are only citation dates. So Banda Api, the one volcano in this zone with a resident European population in 1808-10, was quiet then.
- **New lead, Java, not the target zone: a drought-driven rice failure in 1809** (Van der Chijs, *Nederlandsch-Indisch Plakaatboek* vol. 15, OCR):
  - 3 Mar 1809 (scan p. 546): the Hooge Regering discussed "de algemeene, herhaalde klagten over de schaarsheid van het rijstgewas in de Bataviasche ommelanden".
  - About 14-16 Jul 1809 (scan pp. 824-825, between edicts dated 14 and 16 July): officials told to plant other food crops "bij de schaarsheid van rijst, vooral te Batavia".
  - About 24-26 Aug 1809 (scan p. 858, edict "Verbod tegen den uitvoer van rijst", day illegible): "Ongunstige berichten nopens den aanstaanden ryst-oogst in den Oosthoek gaven aanleiding tot dit verbod" (bad reports on the coming rice harvest in East Java).
  - **5 Oct 1809** (scan p. 920; index "Droogte ... 912", printed page): edict "Voorziening in rijstgebrek in de Bataviasche ommelanden", opening "Zijne excellentie, in aanmerking genomen hebbende, dat door de ongewoone droogte van het saisoen ..." (because of the unusual drought of the season). **Page image verified 26 Oct (William's screenshot, printed pp. 912-913, file '1808-9 volcano research/plakaatboek_vol15/plakaatboek15_p912-913.png'):** "5 October. *Voorziening in rijstgebrek in de Bataviasche ommelanden.* Zijne excellentie, in aanmerking genomen hebbende, dat door de ongewoone droogte van het saisoen, niet alleen in verre de meeste streken van de ommelanden van Batavia een volstrekt misgewasch van rijst plaats heeft, maar ook de aanplanting van aardvruchten, welke was aanbevolen om aan dat gemis te subintreeren, om die zelfde reden niet is geslaagd, waardoor de bewoners van sommige districten zich geheel van voedsel ontbloot vinden" (because of the unusual drought of the season, the rice crop has failed completely in by far most parts of the Batavia environs, and the root crops recommended as a substitute failed for the same reason, so that people in some districts are wholly without food). The government, having tried other remedies "te vergeefs", orders weekly issues of twelve koyang of rice from its own warehouses to the drossaard, to be shared among districts "pro rato der grootte van het misgewas", to avert "hongersnood" and despair, with weekly reports to Daendels. The edict names only the Batavia environs; no other region, no month of onset, no sky or weather beyond the drought. The neighbouring pages (printed 908-911, 914-917, also screenshotted) are cemeteries, the Cheribon Preanger rules (3 Oct), canal dredging (5 Oct) and firewood leases in the Krawang forests (12 and 19 Oct): nothing on weather. The other rice edicts (Mar, Jul, Aug 1809; 1810) are still OCR only.
  - Vol. 16 (1810-11): 30 Jan 1810 government rice sales at Batavia (scan p. 62); 19 Jul 1810 "Voorziening van Batavia van rijst", "Aangezien de toenemende schaarsheid van rijst" (scan p. 270).
  - **Why it matters (inference, a suggestion, not a finding):** large tropical eruptions tend to weaken the Asian-Australian monsoon and bring drought to the Indonesian region in the following year or two, so an unusually dry 1809 season on Java would fit an eruption in late 1808. It is **not diagnostic**: an El Niño does the same, and Daendels's forced labour on the Great Post Road (1808-09) and the British blockade also cut rice supply. To make anything of it we would need (a) the page images of vol. 15 printed pp. 912 ff., (b) whether 1809 was an El Niño year in the ENSO reconstructions, and (c) Java rainfall or harvest records of 1809. Not yet checked: none of the three.
- **Banda rice duty (vol. 15 scan p. 425, late 1808, date not fixed, "zie bij 10/17 November 1807"):** inbound duty on rice for Banda abolished to prevent "eene schaarsheid en prijsvermeerdering". Banda always imported its rice, so this is routine (inference).

## 2. Banda arc travellers

- **Kolff, *Voyages of the Dutch brig of war Dourga* (1840, Earl transl.), `voyagesofdutchbr00kolf`** (Banda arc, Aru, Tanimbar, Damar, 1825-26): "volcano" only Banda Api (p. 44), an outbreak on 18 April, "memorable for having formed a new crater" during Kolff's own visit (1825, inference from the voyage dates), and a peak "from the crater of which smoke issues almost without interruption" (p. 124). Islanders on p. 126-127 "had not seen a single Dutchman" for thirty years. "eruption" only Tambora "in 1814" (sic). No remembered eruption c. 1808. This shows that a passing ship did record eruptions here.

- Whalers: not searched item by item. American whalers did not reach the Banda or Bismarck seas before about 1820, and British South Sea whalers worked the Pacific coast of South America and New Zealand in 1809-11 (inference from the standard whaling histories; not checked this session). Makassarese trepang fleets sailed to northern Australia each Dec-Apr, passing the Banda arc. We found no written account from these voyages for 1808-10 in the sources searched; Makassar and Bugis courts kept written chronicles and day-diaries in lontara script, so one may survive (the earlier wording here, "left no written record (inference)", was an assumption, corrected 12 Nov 2026).
