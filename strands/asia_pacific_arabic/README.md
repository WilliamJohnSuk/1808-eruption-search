**Question.** Do Asian, Pacific, Dutch East Indies or Arabic and Persian records show the eruption, its veil, or its aftermath?

**What was read.** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Persian and Ottoman sources; the REACHES coding of Chinese gazetteers; the Vietnamese court chronicle *Đại Nam thực lục* for 1808-10 (page images of the 2002 translation); Japanese-language scholarship on the 1809 red skies; Dutch colonial registers and the Batavia newspaper (page images); Moluccan, Sulawesi, New Britain, Banda and Halmahera records; Wichmann's earthquake catalogue; Raffles on Bali; Tonga, Tahiti and Hawaiʻi accounts.

**Main results**
- **F04** [page image]: very red sunrises and sunsets at Edo every day from 9 Jan to about 16-25 Jan 1809. **F05** [OCR]: a vermilion sun at Tottori, 5 Feb 1809. Japanese scholarship has never linked them to a volcano.
- **F13** [OCR]: Raffles (1815) reports that Bali residents remembered lesser effects from an Agung eruption "about seven years" earlier. Scale unknown.
- **F17, F24** [page image]: drought and rice failure around Batavia in 1809; a rice shortage was expected by July 1809. **F25** [coded]: drought-famine in Guangdong and Guangxi in 1809 and frost on the Guangxi coast in Nov-Dec 1808. **F26** [page image of the translation]: drought, typhoon and famine in the Red River delta from late 1809 to July 1810. Together these form a 1809 drought belt from about 6°S to 23°N; none is diagnostic.
- **F21** [inferred]: no summer cooling at Hiroshima in 1809.
- Negatives, limited to the records named: we found no eruption, ash, darkness or booms in the Moluccan, Sulawesi, New Britain, Banda and Halmahera records, in the Batavia registers, in Wichmann's catalogue (no 1809 entry), in the Vietnamese chronicle (F26), or in the Tonga, Tahiti and Hawaiʻi accounts (F36). The records for eastern Indonesia are themselves very thin for 1806-1844.

**Open.** The ANRI registers of Banda and Ternate (T20), the Bugis court diary (U34), the Deshima register for Apr-Nov 1809 (T25), Davies's Tahiti journal at SOAS.
