## Summary (inference)

1. **No published deposit at any of these volcanoes is dated to c. 1808.** The one exception that could be is **Cabalían (S Leyte, Philippines)**: an uncalibrated radiocarbon date of 135 ± 30 BP on a pyroclastic-flow deposit, which GVP renders as "1820 ± 30". A date that young sits on the radiocarbon plateau of the last three centuries, so it cannot tell 1700 from 1808 from 1900 (inference). No composition for Cabalían has been found.
2. **Dakataua:** the glass match (Makalia 114G, 0.92) is to an undated Holocene lava. The only dated tephras are the caldera-forming Dk (c. 1350-1270 cal BP) and a possible c. 800 CE unit. GVP says Dk was followed by "at least five subplinian or vulcanian eruptions", but no paper reached gives them dates or names. The isthmus tephra studies 20-50 km south find nothing from Dakataua younger than Dk. The latest eruption (Makalia lava and cinder cones) rests on 1963 village testimony, "about 1880-1890".
3. **Southern Banda:** Teon and Nila have no record between 1693 and 1903-04, and no young deposit has been dated at either. The Snellius-II expedition (1984) sampled a Teon ash-flow sequence it attributed to 1660 and collected charcoal for dating, but no date was found published. Gunungapi Wetar has no confirmed eruption. The GEOROC rows labelled "Wetar" are almost certainly from Wetar island itself (Neogene), not from Gunungapi Wetar (inference), so that volcano's 0.83-0.85 score in B50 sec. 9 should be dropped.

Ibu's 0.91 is the Lake Todoke sample NH44, and Ibu's own rocks score ≤0.78. Gunungapi Wetar has no analysed sample. ## 1. Dakataua (New Britain, PNG; GVP 252040; tier B)

**Eruption history (GVP, text fetch).**

| Date | VEI | Evidence | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1895 ± 5 | 2 | anthropology | Makalia |
| 800 ± 60 CE | not given | radiocarbon (uncorrected) | "D-Wn1 ?" |
| 653 ± 18 CE | 6 | radiocarbon (uncorrected) | Dk |

GVP description (text fetch): the caldera is 10.5 × 13.5 km, with a lake about 50 m above sea level. The latest caldera-forming episode was about 1,150 years ago and was "followed by at least five subplinian or vulcanian eruptions". A north-south line of post-caldera cones and explosion craters crosses the lake. Makalia, a c. 350 m andesitic stratovolcano, "last erupted in the late 19th century". GVP sources: Branch 1965/67 (BMR Record), Lowder & Carmichael 1970, Machida et al. 1996, Cooke & Johnson 1978, Fisher 1957, Newhall & Dzurisin 1988, Silver et al. 2009.

**The latest eruption and how it is dated.**
- Johnson 2013, *Fire Mountains of the Islands*, ch. 4, Table 2 (text fetch): Makalia, an eruption in 1884-1899, dated from "Information collected in 1963 from villagers at the northern end of Willaumez Peninsula". The eruption "must have taken place in about 1880-1890". It produced a lava flow and three small cinder cones in the summit crater (volcanohotspot blog 2023, after GVP and Blong's 1988 photographs; secondary source).
- Johnson 2013 ch. 16 (text fetch) lists Makalaia-Dakataua among four PNG "sleeper" volcanoes, "capable of producing eruptions larger than when each was last active in the nineteenth century".
- Specht & Torrence 2007 (Tech. Rep. Aust. Mus. 20, text fetch): Dakataua "last active in the late 19th century (Branch, 1967)". "For most of these volcanoes there are no recent records of eruptions."

**Dated tephras younger than c. 1,500 years.**
- **Dk (caldera-forming).** Neall et al. 2021 (Tech. Rep. Aust. Mus. Online 34:5-24, text fetch) date it to 1300 cal BP (modal, after Petrie & Torrence 2008). McKee et al. 2011 (Bull. Volc., abstract only) give charcoal 1,383 ± 28 BP = AD 635-670. Torrence, Neall & Boyd 2009 (Pacific Science 63:507, text fetch) give 1,350-1,270 cal BP, VEI 5, phreatomagmatic and ignimbrite-forming, with "up to 1 m of Dk" on Garua Island and "only a trace" on the isthmus.
- **Dk glass** (Neall 2021, text fetch): SiO2 66.5, FeO 5.1, CaO 4.0, Al2O3 14.7. K2O is only in Table S1 on figshare (not read). It scores 0.78 against Dome C (B50).
- **"D-Wn1?" c. 800 CE** (GVP only): its source paper is not identified here. It is probably Machida 1996 (inference).
- **Post-Dk Dakataua tephras:** none named or dated in the papers reached (Neall 2021, Torrence 2009, Specht & Torrence 2007). Neall 2021's Lake Umboli core and isthmus sections (20-50 km south of the caldera) have Witori units above Dk but no later Dakataua unit. The "at least five" post-caldera eruptions are known only through the GVP summary. They presumably come from Machida 1996 (paywalled, not read).
- **Witori units in the same sections** (for reference; Witori itself is ruled out on glass, B50): W-H3 to W-H6 all fall c. 1288 to post-1426 cal AD, and W-H7 is 1914 (Neall 2021 Table 1, text fetch). GVP's Witori "1800 ± 50 tephrochronology VEI 4" (candidate CSV) is presumably one of the W-H units. Its glass (W-H6 0.66) does not fit.

**Compositions** (file: data/georoc_scored_2026-10-26.csv).
- Makalia 114G groundmass glass: SiO2 61.6, FeOt 9.0, K2O 1.78. Scores **0.92 Dome C** and 0.89 South Pole. It is lava groundmass, "Holocene", undated.
- The same sample's whole rock scores 0.89.
- Four other Makalia glasses are rhyolite (75-78 SiO2) and score ≤0.50.
- Eight Holocene (1,300-6,000 BP) Dakataua whole rocks have K2O 0.96-1.87 (median 1.13). That is lower than the Dome C glass (2.05), but a glass would be richer in K2O than its whole rock (inference).

**Size.** Dk is VEI 5-6 with about 10 km³ bulk tephra (secondary source, after McKee 2011). The post-caldera eruptions are called subplinian or vulcanian (GVP), which is smaller. The 1880s Makalia eruption was a lava flow and cinder cones, VEI 2. **An eruption with a stratospheric sulfur load like 1809's would need to be one of the larger post-caldera events** (inference; the 1809 load is about half of Tambora's).

**Could a dated deposit be c. 1808?** No dated deposit is known. The "1880-1890" date for the latest eruption rests on 1963 testimony about events 70-80 years earlier. An older eruption could have been merged into it, but nothing supports that (inference).

**What would settle it (suggestions).**
- A short sediment core from Lake Dakataua (about 120 m deep), with ²¹⁰Pb/¹³⁷Cs and ¹⁴C dating, would show every ash layer of the last few centuries next to the vents. Its glass, with trace elements, could then be compared with the ice shards.
- Failing that, ¹⁴C on charcoal or soil just below the youngest Makalia ash and the cinder-cone deposits on the central peninsula.

## 2. Southern Banda arc

### 2a. Teon (GVP 265050; tier B)
- GVP eruptions (text fetch; all "historical observations"): 1659 Nov 11 (VEI 3), 1660 Feb (VEI 4), 1663 Jan 18 (VEI 3), 1693 (VEI 3), 1904 Jun 3 (VEI 2). **None between 1693 and 1904.**
- Snellius-II report (van Bergen, Poorter & Varekamp 1985, VLIZ, text fetch): coastal cliffs expose "an ash-flow sequence" attributed to the 1660 eruptions. Charcoal was collected for dating, and Teon's plinian falls were "slated for study". **No published date from this charcoal was found** (search 26 Oct; not exhaustive).
- Neumann van Padang 1983, *Scripta Geol.* 71 (text fetch): Verbeek visited in 1899 and called Teon a simple volcano.
- Riedel 1886 (B51, OCR): Teon described as a volcano, no remembered eruption.
- Compositions (file): 10 whole rocks, SiO2 57.5-61.1, K2O 0.91-2.24. Best 0.87 Dome C (TE1C). No glass and no ages.
- **What would settle it (suggestion):** ¹⁴C on the Snellius charcoal or new charcoal from the cliff sequence, to test whether every unit really is 1660. Also glass from the uppermost fall deposit. The island was evacuated to Seram in 1978 (the TNS resettlement; general knowledge, not checked this session), which also cuts off oral history on the island itself.

### 2b. Nila (GVP 265060; tier B)
- GVP eruptions (text fetch): 1903 Dec 8 (VEI 2), 1932 Mar 13 (VEI 2, an SE-flank fissure whose ash forced the abandonment of Rumadai village), 1964 (VEI 1), 1968 (VEI 1). **Nothing before 1903.** The young cone stands inside a low-rimmed caldera that is open to the sea. The caldera is undated.
- Snellius-II (text fetch): the 1968 vent is a small phreatic pit. The young cone differs in composition from the older caldera sequence.
- Compositions (file): 17 whole rocks, K2O median 1.92, best 0.84. No glass and no ages.
- **What would settle it (suggestion):** date the youngest pre-1903 deposits on the young cone. Sea-cliff sections of the caldera rim would show whether a sizeable explosive unit lies just below the 20th-century deposits.

### 2c. Gunungapi Wetar (GVP 265030; tier B)
- GVP (text fetch): **no confirmed eruption.**
  - 1512, Portuguese fire streams, is discredited (Sollewijn Gelpke 1995: actually Sangeang Api).
  - The listed "1699" uncertain entry quotes Neumann van Padang: "On April 8th 1899 steam and SO2 gases rose from the central crater". **The year 1699 is very likely a typo for 1899** (inference).
- It is a 282 m island cone, small for a source of an eruption of the 1809 size (inference).
- **Correction to B50 sec. 9:** the 17 GEOROC "WETAR" rows have no Gunungapi label, and one is tagged NEOGENE. Wetar island itself is an extinct Neogene arc segment, and Snellius-II sampled its granites and rhyolites. So these rows almost certainly do not represent Gunungapi Wetar (inference). **Gunungapi Wetar has no composition on file.**
- **What would settle it (suggestion):** any sampling at all. On size it is a low priority.

## 3. Brief profiles

### 3a. Cabalían (S Leyte, Philippines; GVP 272050; tier A, "central Philippines")
- GVP (text fetch): one eruptive period, "1820 CE (± 30 years)", explosion and pyroclastic flow, dated by radiocarbon (uncalibrated) on a pyroclastic-flow deposit, "135 ± 30 years BP". The UP Resilience Institute profile (text fetch) repeats "a very young age of around 135 ± 30 years before present". Sources: COMVOL 1981, PHIVOLCS hazard map 2007, and Del Mundo & Arpa and Newhall pers. comm. to GVP. The dated sample's publication was not found.
- **Inference:** "1820" is simply 1950 - 135 (the uncalibrated date). A radiocarbon age of 135 ± 30 BP falls on the plateau of the calibration curve after c. 1650. Calibrated, it spans roughly 1680-1940 cal AD in several ranges (Claude's approximate reading of the curve, not a computed calibration). **1808 lies inside that range, and so do most of the 18th and 19th centuries.** The eruption size is not given. "Pyroclastic flow" with no VEI suggests a small or moderate event (inference).
- Composition: none on file (GEOROC Luzon arc file has no Cabalían rows; notes/glass_candidates_compositions.md "none found"). GVP says andesite.
- Records: Cabalían was a Spanish mission town on Sogod Bay, with parish and Jesuit/Augustinian (later Franciscan) records (general knowledge, not checked). The Spanish Philippine chronicles read in B48 are silent (weak negative).
- **What would settle it (suggestions):**
  1. Find the sample behind the 135 BP date (COMVOL 1981 or PHIVOLCS hazard-map report) and re-date it, with a calibrated range and ideally several dates.
  2. Glass from that pyroclastic-flow deposit, with trace elements, compared with the ice shards.
  3. A search of the Cabalían and Sogod parish books and the Leyte mission correspondence for 1808-09 (archival; would need someone in the Philippines or in the Franciscan and Augustinian archives).

### 3b. Gaua (Banks Islands, Vanuatu; GVP 257020; tier B)
- GVP (text fetch): no dated eruption before 1962 except solfataric activity from 1868 (Mallick & Ash 1975). The caldera (6 × 9 km, Lake Letas) formed with the mafic Santa Maria Pyroclastic Series. It is undated (Wikipedia, citing Robin, Eissen & Monzier 1995, Terra Nova 7:436).
- European contact was Quirós in 1606, then nothing regular until the Melanesian Mission in the later 19th century (inference from the absence of any earlier record; dates not checked).
- Compositions (file): 68 whole rocks, K2O median 2.35. Best SM30A, 0.94 South Pole and 0.86 Dome C, from the "Santa Maria pyroclastic series". Some rows are tagged Pleistocene (1.7 Ma). No glass.
- **What would settle it (suggestions):** date the youngest tephra on the caldera rim and the Lake Letas sediments. Vanuatu's 1809 oral record is part of the eHRAF search that is William's.

### 3c. Awu (Sangihe; GVP 267040; tier C)
- GVP (text fetch): 1640-41?, 1646, 1699?, **1711 Dec (VEI 3, about 2,030 dead, Valentijn)**, **1812 Aug 6-8 (VEI 4?)**, 1856, 1875, 1883, 1885, 1892, 1893, and later.
- **No entry for 1800-1811.** The 1812 eruption shows that Sangihe events did reach the record in this period (inference: a weak negative for 1808).
- Compositions (file): GEOROC has 3 Awu whole rocks (K2O 1.2-1.4, best 0.82) and the Gill sheet has 5 (K2O ≤1.4, best 0.81).
- **Correction to B50 sec. 9:** the "Awu 0.92" there is a name-matching false hit on **MahAWU** (Mahawu, N Sulawesi, 0.92). Awu itself is a poor fit (≤0.82), on the low-K side.
- **What would settle it (suggestion):** the GVP sources (Matahelumual 1985; Neumann van Padang 1951) for any 1808-09 Sangihe report.

### 3d. Ibu (Halmahera; GVP 268030; tier B)
- GVP (text fetch): the first observed eruption was 1911. No prehistoric dates are on the page.
- **Correction to B50 sec. 9:** the GEOROC row giving "Ibu 0.91" is sample NH44, which Gill's sheet places at Lake Todoke (Todoko-Ranu; B50 sec. 7). Ibu's own analysed rocks are dacite (SiO2 66.6-67.8, ≤0.76) or basaltic andesite (≤0.78). **Ibu proper is a poor fit** (file; inference).

### 3e. Dukono (Halmahera; GVP 268010; tier C)
- GVP (text fetch): a major eruption c. 1550 (lava filled the strait to Mamuya; the town of Tolo destroyed, per Neumann van Padang 1983), then nothing until near-continuous activity from 1933. The Malupang Warirang crater's eruptions are undated.
- Compositions: B50 (0.89-0.93 whole rock). The Ternate chronicle is silent 1763-1840 (B48).
- **What would settle it (suggestion):** tephra stratigraphy of the Dukono and Tarakani area with dates. None was found published.

### 3f. Negros tier-B centres (Mandalagan, Silay, Cuernos de Negros)
- They have no historical or dated Holocene eruptions in GVP (candidate CSV). GEOROC whole rocks score ≤0.88 (file). Nothing further was found.
