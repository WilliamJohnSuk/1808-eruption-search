## Short answer

- **No eruption of Long Island, or of any of its neighbours, is recorded between 1700 and 1827.** Johnson 2013 lists no Papua New Guinea volcano sighting of any kind dated 1797-1826 (OCR/summary). The European record for those years is almost empty, so that absence proves little.
- **A large (sulfur-rich) Long Island eruption in 1808 is ruled out on physical grounds.** It would have laid a second Tibito-type ash over the highlands. Firth et al. 2024 found none at 17 sites, and Blong's survey found only one thin, local ash above Tibito. Gabriel et al. 2025 also put Long Island's own VEI 6 eruption at only about 4 ± 1 Tg S, several times less than the 1809 sulfate. A rerun of Tibito would not have been enough (inference from those sources).
- **A small Long Island eruption between c. 1780 and 1845 is not ruled out, and there are hints of one.** D'Urville (Aug 1827) found the shore "more arid than all the other islands", with no coconuts or people. The first resettlers, who landed in the 1840s-50s, found the island "devoid of trees" with steam "still coming from the ground". Genealogies give "no earlier than 1780" for the eruption they remember. That fits poorly with an eruption in the 1660s, after which Dampier saw the island green and flourishing in 1700. The thin "minor airfall tephras" at the top of the Matapun beds have never been dated separately. This is a lead, not a finding, and Blong reads the same facts as slow regrowth on porous ignimbrite.
- **Neighbours:** Ritter was active in 1700 and 1793, then quiet when D'Urville passed in 1827. Manam has a gap between 1700 and a doubtful 1830 report. Karkar's next entry after 1643 is an uncertain 1830. Bam is first recorded in about 1872. Umboi and Sakar have no dated Holocene eruption. Crown and Tolokiwa are not on the GVP Holocene list. All but Long Island and Bam are basaltic, which fits the 1809 glass (andesite, about 60% silica) worse than Long Island does (inference).
- **Verdict (inference):** the Bismarck arc stays in the glass "family", but no volcano there can supply the 1809 sulfur on present evidence. The one testable item is the undated thin ash at the top of Long Island's Matapun beds, together with the post-Tibito ash at Blong's Upper Wage site 400. A date and glass analysis on either would settle whether a smaller c. 1800 eruption happened. Neither could be the main 1809 source.

## 1. Long Island: what the record shows, 1660s-1953

### Date and size of the Tibito eruption
- GVP (Long Island, 251050; OCR/summary): eruptions listed are 2040 BCE (VEI 6), 1660 ± 20 (VEI 6, "Time of Darkness"), then **1933 and 1938 (Lake Wisdom, no detail)**, 1943, 1953-54 (Motmot appears), 1955, 1961 (uncertain), 1968, 1973-74, 1976, 1993. **There is nothing between 1660 and 1933.** No discredited entries. https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=251050
- Blong & Kurbatov 2020, JVGR 395:106828 (abstract, OCR/summary) review more than 100 date estimates, "from the late 16th to the early 20th century", and settle on 1665-1668 from ice-core sulfate. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvolgeores.2020.106828
- **Gabriel, Innes, Abbott, ... Hutchison, McConnell, ... Sigl 2025**, JVGR 464:108346 (CC BY, St Andrews repository PDF; OCR/summary): Long Island is narrowed to 1654 ± 1 or 1662 ± 1 CE, with 1662 tentatively preferred. **VSSI 4.2 [0.8] Tg S** (PMIP4 had 0.8). No Long Island glass has yet been found in any ice core; the 1667 shards are Tarumae. The paper says nothing about 1808/09 or about any Long Island activity after 1700. The reference glass for Long Island is Coulter et al. 2009. https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/32287 , doi:10.1016/j.jvolgeores.2025.108346
  - Inference: the Bismarck-arc magma that gave the closest glass match produced only about 4 Tg S in a VEI 6 eruption. The 1809 event is usually put at several times that. This argues against Long Island as the 1809 source even before the stratigraphy is considered.

### Stratigraphy on the island
- Blong, Pain & McKee 1982, Rec. Aust. Mus. 34(7):419-426 (OCR/summary): the last catastrophic eruption was "during the first half of the 17th century" (later revised to 1630-1670). OCR: "At the top of the Matapun beds minor airfall tephras occur, often exhibiting a platy structure", and the sequence "concluded with the deposition of a thin, fine-grained airfall tephra". There is also a lake terrace 10-15 m above the present level. **No deposit is described as younger than the Matapun beds, and the minor tephras at the top are not dated separately.** https://journals.australian.museum/media/Uploads/Journals/17578/289_complete.pdf
- Egloff & Specht 1982, Rec. Aust. Mus. 34(8):427-446 (OCR/summary): charcoal in the Matapun beds is dated ANU-1125 380 ± 70 bp and ANU-1126 230 ± 75 bp. They describe no younger deposits. Mennis (1978) puts the loss of Yomba before the last Long Island eruption. https://journals.australian.museum/media/Uploads/Journals/17577/290_complete.pdf
- Firth, Blong, Haberle et al. 2024/25, Holocene 35:234-248 (abstract and Table 3 via earlier thread; OCR/summary): no tephra younger than Tibito at their 17 highland sites (notes/glass_candidates_compositions.md line 135).
- Blong 1982 pp. 28, 88-95 (page image, earlier thread): the only post-Tibito ash known on the mainland is a 2.5 cm bed at Upper Wage site 400. It is undated, its source is unknown (Blong suggests the Schouten Islands or Manam), and it has XRF trace elements only (Zr 274, Y 40 ppm, unlike Tibito).

- **Blong's reasoning (p. 187, page image):** regrowth to the point where "the island's appearance would not have provoked comment from a careful observer would have taken a minimum of about 20-30 years and a maximum of perhaps 40-50 years". So the eruption "could not have occurred in the periods 1680-1700 (Dampier, 1700) or 1807-1827 (D'Urville)", and "it seems impossible ... that the eruption could have occurred later than about 1807". Blong explains D'Urville's arid shore by the porous ignimbrite, which he says makes the island look "more 'arid' than its neighbours Crown Island and Tolokiwa" even today (p. 187, page image).
  - Inference: Blong's "1807-1827" exclusion applies to the *great* Tibito-type eruption, which mantled the whole island in ignimbrite. It does not exclude a small eruption, of VEI 3-4 for example from the caldera lake, around 1808. Such an eruption could have killed the shore vegetation and left an "arid" shore in 1827 without leaving a highland ash.
- **Ball 1982** (Rec. Aust. Mus. 34(9):447-461; OCR/summary): Dampier's green island means the last major eruption was not in 1670-1700; "the eruption must have occurred before 1670 or in the interval 1700-1800". He does not mention D'Entrecasteaux or Miklouho-Maclay, and gives no observation of smoke, steam or ash after 1700. https://journals.australian.museum/media/Uploads/Journals/17576/291_complete.pdf

### Oral history of the resettlement (Ball & Hughes 1982, Rec. Aust. Mus. 34(10):463-525; OCR/summary)
https://journals.australian.museum/media/Uploads/Journals/17575/292_complete.pdf
- The first resettler, Korimi, came from Sakar via Tolokiwa: "sailed to Long Island with his young son, being the first to land since the eruption". He "was very probably more than 30 years of age by the time he landed", and "must have been born by 1815" (OCR). That puts the first landing at about 1845 or later (inference).
- Condition on landing (OCR): "When Korimi and his party arrived the island was devoid of trees." "Steam was still coming from the ground and there were stunted trees" (Matapun account). "There was still smoke coming from all over the island and there was no grass" (Poin Kiau account).
- Resettlement "between about 1850 and 1875". "Even on the most generous age estimates, that would have the eruption no earlier than 1780". Mennis's mainland genealogies say "8-9 generations ago" (OCR).
- Ball & Hughes themselves doubt the young date: trees present "some 250-350 years after the eruption casts further doubt on the age estimates". Evans and Womersley judged the trees to be about 30-50 years old (OCR/summary).
- The lake reached its present level (190 m) and the Monono River became permanent only "shortly before the war" (OCR).
- **Inference:** the Long Island evidence pulls two ways. Dampier's green island in 1700 and the ice-core date of 1662 mean the great eruption was in the 1660s. Yet the resettlers of the 1840s-50s found a treeless, steaming island, and D'Urville in 1827 found the shore arid. That is hard to square with 180 years of tropical regrowth. Either the oral account is telescoped and the "steam" is the Lake Wisdom hydrothermal system, or a later, smaller eruption stripped the island again some time between about 1780 and 1840. Nothing found here decides between the two. The second reading would put a candidate eruption in the right decades, but a small one, unable to make the 1809 sulfur.

## 3. Fit to the 1809 evidence (inference)
- **Sulfur:** Long Island's VEI 6 gave about 4 Tg S (Gabriel 2025), and its neighbours are smaller, mostly basaltic, open-vent volcanoes. To make the 1809 sulfur, a Bismarck-arc eruption would have to be larger than Tibito. That would leave a highland ash at least as widespread as Tibito, and none exists (Firth 2024; Blong's survey).
- **Glass:** Dome C 1809 glass (SiO2 59.4, FeO 9.0, K2O 2.05) is more evolved than Tibito (SiO2 55-57, FeO 11-12; data/glass_candidates.csv). The basaltic neighbours (Manam, Ritter, Umboi, Sakar) are further from it still. Bam and Kadovar are andesites but tiny.
- **Timing:** a small Long Island or Ritter eruption in 1808 is possible and would be invisible in the European record. It would, though, be a side event, like the Antarctic local ash at Siple Dome, not the 1809 source.
- Net: this strand **weakens** the Bismarck Sea as the home of the 1809 eruption. The glass "family" resemblance points more to a larger, more evolved Melanesian or Indonesian arc volcano (New Britain calderas, Halmahera, Sangihe, Banda arc) than to Long Island or its neighbours.
