## 0. Bottom line
1. **No Portuguese-language description of a strange sky, haze, dimmed or red sun, or unusual twilight between Nov 1808 and mid-1809 was found.** The key periodicals for that window could not be searched:
   - *Gazeta do Rio de Janeiro* 1809 (Hemeroteca BN blocked; Internet Archive holds only Sept–Dec 1808).
   - *Gazeta de Lisboa* 1808–09 (no digitized copy reached).
   - *Diario Lisbonense* from 1 May 1809 (purl.pt/14341 blocked).

   This is an access gap, not a negative result.
2. **The only volcanic eruption in the Lusophone world in 1808–09 is São Jorge, Azores.** It ran from 1 May to about 10 June 1808 and was a basaltic fissure eruption with phreatomagmatic surges.
   - Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program (GVP): VEI 1.
   - Contemporary relations are printed in *Archivo dos Açores* vol. V (1883–84), pp. 437–465. They were reachable as Internet Archive OCR.
   - Ash fell on Terceira "por muitos dias", and reached the tip of Pico, Angra (Terceira) and São Miguel.
   - It falls inside Chenoweth's (2001) proposed **March–June 1808** window. But it is far too small (VEI 1; mostly effusive; low sulfur yield; mid-latitude 38.6°N) to produce a bipolar sulfate spike or global cooling.
   - It **cannot** explain haze in Bogotá or Lima in Dec 1808. It should be listed as a coincidental event, not a candidate.
3. **No other Lusophone-area eruption is recorded for 1808–09.** Frederico Machado's 1964 eruption sequence goes Tenerife 1798, then Fogo 1799, then São Jorge 1808.3, then the São Miguel submarine eruption of 1811.
   - Nothing in the Canaries or Cape Verde for 1808–09.
   - The next Azores event is 1811: submarine eruptions off São Miguel, including Sabrina in June 1811. Archivo dos Açores V also records "fumo sahindo do mar" in Feb 1812.
4. **Instrumental meteorology:**
   - **Portugal:** no published series runs across 1808–09. One possible exception is the Almada physicians' registers, 1788–1813, with a "state of sky" field; 1808–1812 may be missing (see §3).
   - **Brazil:** no known series for 1808–09. Sanches Dorta's Rio series ends in 1788. In 1808 the observatory collection was transferred to the Academia Real Militar (founded 1810).
5. **Traveller accounts in Brazil, 1808–1810:** IA-OCR searches of Mawe, Luccock, Koster and Santos ("Padre Perereca") found nothing relevant.

**Chronology from secondary works, not independently verified**
- 1 May: onset.
- 2–4 May: new vents.
- 11 May: lava at Santo Amaro.
- 15–17 May: renewed explosions and a "nuvem ardente" surge that scalded some 30–60 people.
- 5–10 June: ash on Pico, Terceira and São Miguel.
- GVP end date: 10 June 1808.

**What it constrains**
- It explains nothing observed in Dec 1808 – 1809.
- It is a coincidental match for Chenoweth's March–June 1808 date. It is worth stating explicitly that it is not a plausible source of a global signal.
- Any Azores or N Atlantic "smoke" reports from May–June 1808 should be attributed to it.
