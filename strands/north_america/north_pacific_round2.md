## Bottom line so far
- No catalogue from 1834 to 1917 that I could search names any North Pacific or Icelandic eruption with a specific date in 1808–1810. The catalogues checked are Keferstein via Perrey, v. Hoff 1841, Grewingk 1850, Scrope 1862/72 and Sapper 1917. The only North Pacific items in the window are:
  1. **Sheveluch (Kamchatka) "certainly active between 1790 and 1810".** Source: Erman 1848, a retrospective footnote with no specific event. Erman's basis is not stated; it is probably local Kamchadal or Cossack testimony gathered in 1829.
  2. **Klyuchevskoy "1810" eruption.** Sapper 1917 (in brackets) says Perrey (p. 293) has it "nach Keferstein" (after Keferstein). Sapper's warning about Perrey-after-Keferstein items is that they are third-hand and therefore uncertain. The original source is untraced. **Caution:** v. Hoff (1841, vol. 5, scan p. 18) says Keferstein dated a Klyuchevskaya eruption to a year around 1762, and that v. Hoff "find[s] no other report" of it. Perrey's "1810" may therefore be a Keferstein year that got shifted or garbled, or a different item. Unresolved.
  3. **Zao (Japan) 1809 (文化6年, Bunka 6).** JMA Souran 4th ed. (2013): "水蒸気噴火？ … 6月から活動を始め、12月29日に爆発。硫黄流入し、川魚被害。" ("Phreatic eruption? … activity began in the 6th month and it exploded on the 29th day of the 12th month; sulphur flowed in and river fish were harmed"). The citations are only to modern compilations (Murayama 1978; Imada et al. 1985 Yamagata Prefecture report; JMA 2005). **The entry is not in the 1938 JMA review of Zao history (Ueno 1938, which used Omori's 震災予防調査会報告 no. 86), nor in Sapper 1917.** The primary source is still untraced. The dates are probably lunisolar: 文化6年12月29日 ≈ 2 Feb 1810. [Correction 16 Oct: the primary source 栗藩史稿 (ERI J0300419) gives 文化六年十一月二十三日 = 29 Dec 1809 Gregorian, so JMA's 29 Dec 1809 is already Gregorian; see asian_arabic_round4.md. The 2 Feb 1810 conversion is wrong.] GVP's "12 Jun 1809" looks like the lunar "6th month" read as June. Zao is small, andesitic and phreatic: not a candidate for a dacitic tephra reaching the Yukon.
  4. **Bogoslof 1806–1823.** Slow dome growth seen by RAC crews (Langsdorff, Dall, Grewingk, Veniaminov). Nothing specific to 1808–1810.
  4b. **Tarumae (Hokkaido), "Bunka era 1804–1817", VEI 3** (GVP and JMA). This is the only NH-extratropical VEI-3 eruption whose span covers the window. The documentary basis is hearsay recorded in 1856 about a disaster "40 years before" (≈1816), and Hokkaido historian Kōno Tsunekichi doubted it. There is a real 2–3 cm fall deposit NE of the volcano, but its date is unconstrained (see F8).
  5. **Colima 1806–1809 (or "1808–18" per Mercalli/Sapper).** Mainly effusive; already ruled out in the lead's notes.
- **Iceland:** no eruption in 1808–1810 in any list checked (Thorarinsson 1965 on submarine eruptions; the eldey.de compilation; Jökull 1990). The record jumps from Bárðarbunga/Holuhraun 1797 to Grímsvötn 1816.
- **Eclipse Icefield (Yukon) sulfate (NOAA Paleo file):** annual SO₄ loading is 1808 1.01, **1809 1.44, 1810 2.48 Tg**, versus 1815 at 3.21. Dating error is ±1 yr. A North Pacific peak in **1810** fits a separate, regional NH eruption close in time. No tephra IDs are given in that file.

### F2. Erman 1848: Sheveluch active 1790–1810
- Adolph Erman, *Reise um die Erde durch Nord-Asien und die beiden Oceane … 1828, 1829 und 1830*, Abt. 1, Bd. 3 (Berlin 1848). IA scan of a Google copy: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_XAgPAAAAQAAJ (also 11448840bsb and bub_gb_HCpRAAAAcAAJ). The passage is a footnote on scan p. 291; printed page not determined.
- OCR fragment, verbatim: "Bald uach der Mitte dieser Periode und namentlich zwischen 1790 und 1810 ist aber der Schiwelutsch jedenfalls thätig geweseu."
- Full-sentence rendering, from WebFetch's reconstruction of the snippet. The German was normalised by the tool, so treat it as approximate:
  - "Wahrscheinlich äussert sich einige Thätigkeit am Schiwelutsch nur während der Ruhe des benachbarten Kliutschewsker Vulkanes. So war der erstere unthätig, sowohl von 1735 bis 1740, während der Kliutschewsker Berg starke Gipfelauswürfe zeigte, als auch jetzt (1829) während eines der grossartigsten Lavenausbrüche desselben Vulkanes. Bald nach der Mitte dieser Periode und namentlich zwischen 1790 und 1810 ist aber der Schiwelutsch jedenfalls thätig gewesen."
  - English: "Probably Sheveluch shows some activity only while the neighbouring Klyuchevskoy volcano is quiet. Thus it was inactive both in 1735–40, when Klyuchevskoy had strong summit eruptions, and now (1829), during one of that volcano's grandest lava eruptions. Soon after the middle of this period, and especially between 1790 and 1810, however, Sheveluch was certainly active."
- Scrope 1862/1872 repeats it (IA volcanoscharact00scrogoog, scan p. 480): "Great eruptions took place from it in 1739 and between 1790 and 1810". Scrope upgrades Erman's "thätig" (active) to "great eruptions". **This is a secondary amplification, not independent evidence.**
- Verification: OCR text only. The observer is Erman, on the spot in 1829; his informants were not named. It is a retrospective generalisation with no year inside 1790–1810.
- Relevance: Sheveluch is a major andesitic explosive centre at 56.6°N. It is a plausible kind of source for NH extratropical tephra, but this text does not place an eruption in 1808–1810.

### F8. Tarumae (Shikotsu), Hokkaido: "Bunka-era (1804–1817)" eruption
- GVP v4.6.7 (local copy of the pyvolcans table) lists **Shikotsu [Tarumae] 1804–1817, VEI 3, confirmed**. It is the only NH-extratropical VEI-3 eruption whose span covers 1808–1810. Others: Bogoslof 1806–23 (VEI 2, dome); Sheveluch "1800, uncertain, VEI 3".
- JMA Tarumae history (https://ds.data.jma.go.jp/svd/vois/data/sapporo/109_Tarumae/109_history.html), verbatim: "▲1804～1817（文化年間）年 中規模：マグマ噴火 … 山頂から噴火(文化年間のある時期に噴火があった)。降下火砕堆積物は北東側に分布(植苗で層厚2-3cm)。死傷者多数とする文献もあり。総噴出物量0.05km3 … (VEI3)". English: "1804–1817 (Bunka era), medium-scale magmatic eruption … eruption from the summit (an eruption occurred at some time in the Bunka era). Fall deposits lie to the NE (2–3 cm thick at Uenae). Some documents say there were many casualties. Total ejecta 0.05 km³ … (VEI 3)."
- **Primary-source chain** (Tomakomai Weather Station 1965, 「樽前山噴火史」 "History of Tarumae eruptions", *Kenshin Jihō* 30, p. 83ff, at p. 85: https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/kishou/books/kenshin/vol30p083.pdf; OCR via WebFetch):
  - The casualty story comes from the Himeji Confucian scholar **菅野白華 (潔), Sugano Hakka (Kiyoshi)**. He passed Tarumae on 安政3年8月17日 (Ansei 3, 8th month, 17th day ≈ Sept 1856) and wrote in his travel diary (title OCR'd as 「北静乗」, probably 「北游乗」) about **"40年前の…災害"** ("the disaster of 40 years before"), i.e. **about 1816, hearsay**.
  - The Hokkaido historian **河野常吉 (Kōno Tsunekichi)** called the event doubtful: "当時この地方は幕府の直轄地であって，他のいろいろな記録があるのにこの噴火を書いた記録がないから…事実は疑問である" ("at the time this area was directly governed by the shogunate, and although various other records exist, none records this eruption … so the fact is doubtful").
  - The deposit (2–3 cm at Uenae) is geological evidence that some Bunka-era eruption happened. The only dating clue (~1816) points **away from 1808–09**.
- Assessment: this is the only Hokkaido/Japan magmatic eruption that could even loosely fall in the window. Its date is unconstrained within 1804–1817. Tarumae magmas of 1667/1739 are andesitic to dacitic, but Bunka-era glass chemistry was not found. **A candidate worth checking against the Yukon (Eclipse) dacitic shards** (Yalcin 2006), but the documentary dating favours ~1816 and the eruption is small (0.05 km³).

### F10. Eclipse Icefield sulfate and a 2024 analogue
- NOAA Paleo file https://paleo.ltrr.arizona.edu/icecore/trop/eclipse/eclipse-volcanic.txt: annual SO₄ loading 1808 1.01, 1809 1.44, **1810 2.48** Tg (1815 3.21). Dating ±1 yr.
- Hutchison et al. 2024/25 (PNAS 122(1), "The 1831 CE mystery eruption identified as Zavaritskii caldera, Simushir Island (Kurils)"; https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10566108) shows that a sizeable Central Kuril eruption (1831) went **completely unrecorded historically**.
  - The Central Kurils had no permanent observers in 1808–1810: the Russian Urup colony was abandoned by 1805, and the Japanese were only on Iturup/Kunashir. The absence of documentary evidence for a Kuril source in 1809/10 is therefore weak evidence.
  - The paper refers to the "1808/9 CE mystery eruption" (~19 Tg S) as still unidentified.
