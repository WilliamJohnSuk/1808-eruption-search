## Direct answer on 4 November 1808
**Moose Factory records no darkness on Friday 4 Nov 1808.** The entry is about work, the weakened river ice, and a boy hearing "Crys of People" from the packet party crossing the ice. It says "The Warm weather we have had lately has very much weakend the Ice". The nearest darkness remark is **Tuesday 1 Nov 1808: "this Day has been remarkably dark & towards evening we have heavy rain"**. On 5 Nov the latter part of the day was "dark & rainy". Both come with rain, during an unusually warm spell in which the river reopened (5 Nov) and rain thawed the ground (12–14 Nov). The simplest reading is overcast rain-bearing weather. It does not independently confirm an aerosol veil, but it shows that the first week of November 1808 was gloomy in James Bay as well as at York. It also shows that **"remarkably dark" was noteworthy to this writer**. No similar remark appears anywhere else in the year read. Caveat: this is the London fair copy, and B.135/a/96, the rough journal with daily wind and weather, stops about 6 Oct 1808, so finer weather detail for 1–12 Nov is not available in these files.
