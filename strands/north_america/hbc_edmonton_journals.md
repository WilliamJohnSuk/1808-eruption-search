## Headline
**Edmonton has no usable record for early November 1808, or for any optical or atmospheric phenomenon, in 1807–11.** The volumes are not daily post journals. Each is James Bird's *"Sketch of the principal Transactions & Occurrences"*: an irregular summary (entries usually 2–10 days apart) written for the London Committee about trade, Indians, provisions, transport and the NWC. Weather is mentioned only when it affected the trade or travel (snow depth, freeze-up, breakup, storms on the passage). There is **no remark of any kind** on darkness, haze, smoke, "thick" weather, the colour of the sun or moon, twilights, halos, aurora, ash or dust, or reports. **No thermometer readings** appear in any volume.

**Early Nov 1808:** entries exist only for **1 Nov** (trade) and **6–7 Nov** (trade and tobacco). **No entries for 2, 3, 4 or 5 Nov, or 8–10 Nov.** So York Factory's "darkest day" (Fri 4 Nov) and Moose's "remarkably dark" (Tue 1 Nov) **can be neither confirmed nor ruled out** from Edmonton. Silence in this kind of document means nothing.

**Winter 1808–09 signal (snow only):** Bird twice blames an **"extraordinary depth of Snow"** for the shortage of provisions (20 Jan 1809, 12 Mar 1809). Winter 1807–08 at the same post was the opposite: **"want of Snow"** made the ground "too slippery" for hunting (Jan–Feb 1808). The river froze **14 Nov 1808**, against **22 Nov 1807** and **8 Nov 1810**. The ice broke **15 Apr 1809**. In 1811 the river "did not open till the 5th of May".

**No Edmonton journal for 1809–10 exists in these files.** B.60/a/8 ends Aug 1809 and B.60/a/9 begins Sept 1810. The 1810–11 volume does record an exceptionally severe, deep-snow winter (see below), but it gives no optical remarks.

## Direct answer: early November 1808
Edmonton gives **no evidence either way**. Bird wrote about the trade on 1 Nov ("about 70 Beavers... of a very inferior Quality") and on 6–7 Nov (tobacco complaints, 76 Beavers). **He made no entries for 2–5 Nov.** Even on the days he wrote, he never once remarks on the sky, light or ordinary weather in 1807–11, unless it stopped hunting or travel. So the absence of a "dark day" here is uninformative. That fits the existing reading of York Factory (4 Nov) and Moose (1 Nov) as **weather events**. Edmonton neither supports an early-November aerosol arrival from the west nor rules one out.

## Month summary (Oct 1808 – Apr 1809, plus context)
- **Oct 1808:** Blood, Sarcee, Fall and Muddy River Indians trading. Hunters sent towards Red Deer River. No weather.
- **Nov 1808:** trade (1, 6–7, 11–12, 16 Nov). **River froze 14 Nov.** Fall canoe stopped by ice below the South Branch (reported 22 Nov). No sky remarks. 2–5 Nov missing.
- **Dec 1808:** 4 entries (10, 11, 19, 22). Trade, a hunting party, the last fish (2000 in all). No weather.
- **Jan 1809:** **"extraordinary depth of Snow"** (20th). Meat scarce; 9 days to haul 6 bulls 60 miles.
- **Feb 1809:** trade (wolves very numerous: 520 on 5 Feb, 538 on 15 Feb). Provisions short. No weather words.
- **Mar 1809:** **"great depth of Snow"** stops the buffalo pounds (12th). Meat "bad".
- **Apr 1809:** **River ice broke 15 Apr.** Trade; birch rind gathered.
- **Contrast with 1807–08:** "want of Snow"; freeze-up 22 Nov 1807.
- **1810–11:** freeze-up 8 Nov 1810. "Extreme cold" in Dec. "Extreme severity of the winter & Extraordinary Depth of snow". Sudden thaw 18 Jan 1811. Breakup 5 May 1811. Prairie fire (autumn 1810) and forest fire (spring 1810) are both mentioned.
