## Part (a): volcanologists' compilations (done 21 Oct 2026, ~12:10-13:00 UTC)

### Summary
- No compilation we reviewed records a darkness, ashfall, sea-wave or "island destroyed" tradition in Melanesia, eastern Indonesia or Mindanao that its authors date anywhere near 1808. Not found in the literature we reviewed.
- **Best lead (OCR/summary):** Blong's own genealogical dating. Terence E. Hays's review of *The Time of Darkness* (Pacific Studies, pp. 82-87) reports that "Genealogical studies of Enga people ... yield estimates for the time period referred to in their legends as ranging from 1803-1899", and that Blong conceded "most" estimates point to a late-18th or early-19th-century eruption; Blong nevertheless chose the radiocarbon date for Long Island (mid-17th century), calling it a matter of weighting. Blong et al. 2018 (*Holocene* 28: 529-544) now put the Tibito Tephra eruption at 1651-1671 AD (95.4%).
  - Inference: genealogies usually telescope (they shorten time), so a 1803-1899 genealogical date for a c. 1660 event is the expected error and is not by itself evidence of a second, later ashfall. But Blong assumed every legend refers to the most recent tephra in the area, and Hays notes the versions show "few meaningful spatial patterns". If any group's legend sits on a younger ash than Tibito, or its genealogy is unusually deep, it could be an 1808 memory. This is testable only in Blong chapter 14 (dating) and his informant table, which are on William's Mac (blong_pages). **Top task when William is back.**

### What part (b) means (inference)
- Nineteenth-century writers who lived among Melanesians did record remembered eruptions when they existed: Savo's activity "fifty years before" and "within the memory of living men" got into Codrington and Guppy. Nothing comparable surfaced for a great darkness around 1808 in the Solomons, Vanuatu, Tonga, Sangihe, the Moluccas or the Celebes texts searched. That is mild evidence against a very large eruption in those well-visited island groups, and no evidence either way for the interior of New Guinea, the Banda Sea outer arc, Mindanao's interior or the Southern Ocean, where the searched books say little.
- The New Guinea highlands remain the one place with a widely collected darkness tradition whose genealogical dates fall in the 19th century (part a).

## Blong ch. 14, genealogical dates (page image), 22 Oct 2026
Read by the "Eruption markers knowledge base" thread from William's screenshots (blong_pages p089-p097 = Blong 1982 pp. 176-193). **Page image verified** unless marked inference.
- **Method (p. 177):** a generation is counted as 30 years (Blong says this overestimates), or the informant's own calendar estimate is used; 15 years are subtracted to put the event mid-generation. Error at least ±20 years.
- **Table 29 (p. 178), Enga:** 22 estimates from Paul Mai's genealogies, each 1-4 generations back. Earliest: no. 23 (4 generations) 1803; no. 7 (3-4) 1807; no. 18 1812; no. 13 1815; no. 8 1829. Most fall 1840-1880; latest 1905. Mai's own analysis: 1820-1860.
- **Table 30 (p. 181), other highland and lowland accounts:** 009 1727 (>8 generations); 016 1757 (>7); 023 1700 (7); 056 1803 (5 generations before 1953); 059 1775-1825 or earlier; 050 1820-1850 or earlier; 094 Wanuma 1822; 097 1837; most 1840s-1880s. Overall 33 of 51 estimates fall 1840-1890, and all but seven fall 1800-1890.
- **Different ash?** Blong flags only Wanuma (094) and Washkuk (098) as possibly relating to a different ash fall than Tibito Tephra. No group is tied to a named younger eruption.
- **Long Island genealogies (pp. 180-182):** Koromi people came from Tolokiwa while the ground was still steaming, giving ~1810-1840; Poin Kiau 1835-1860; Ball & Johnson earlier than 1815-1835; Naval Intelligence 1945 ~1795 or ~1645; Ball & Hughes 1980 no earlier than 1780, resettlement 1830-1880. Range ~1630-1860, mostly late 18th to early 19th century.
- **Historical observers (pp. 182-187):** Dampier (1700) saw Long Island green. D'Urville (Aug 1827) saw "deserted beaches", the island "more arid than all the other islands", "neither coconut trees nor any trace of inhabitants". With 20-50 years for revegetation, Blong excludes 1680-1700 and 1807-1827 and calls an eruption "later than about 1807" impossible; he prefers the 18th century or before 1680.
- **Radiocarbon (pp. 191-193):** "almost certainly in the mid-seventeenth century (say 1630-1670)"; post-1700 not wholly excluded; the withdrawn lead-210 date of 1680-1690 conflicts with Dampier.
- **What this means (inference):** the genealogies cluster around 1800-1860, but Blong and later radiocarbon (1651-1671, Blong et al. 2018) place Tibito in the 17th century, so the near-1808 genealogical dates (1803, 1807, 1812) are most likely telescoped and are not evidence for an 1808 eruption. D'Urville's 1827 description of a barren island with "deserted beaches" is the one detail that fits a younger disturbance, but Blong rules out 1807-1827 on revegetation time. Wanuma and Washkuk are the only accounts worth a second look for a different, younger ash.

### Wohlt 1978 thesis read (22 Oct 2026, 20:05 UTC)
Source: P. B. Wohlt, *Ecology, Agriculture and Social Organization: the Dynamics of Group Composition in the Highlands of Papua New Guinea*, PhD, University of Minnesota, 1978 (461 PDF pages; William's download ~/Downloads/1808-9 Volcano Project/"dissertation .pdf"). Searched the OCR text in full; key pages checked as **page images** (printed pp. 27, 199). Fieldwork at Yumbisa (upper Wage, Kandep area) 1972-75.
- **No account of the darkness, ashfall, a volcano or a tephra layer is in the thesis.** Table 1 (OCR, p. 25) shows that Wohlt ran a minor survey on the "Time of darkness": "Find out who knows the story and ask following questions...". The answers stayed in his field notebooks. They became Blong's legend 059 and the ANU tape, and are not printed here.
- **Settlement date (page image, pp. 27, 199):** Yumbisa was uninhabited high pandanus bush until Kapii came from Bipi "in the beginning of the nineteenth century". Wohlt estimates settlement "170 years ago" (OCR p. 17), i.e. c. 1805, from living people's ages projected back through the genealogies. He lengthened them himself because they are compressed: "Yurimi represented as two generations; Kepa and Pao as two families each" (OCR p. 198). On p. 199 he says: "The situation before 1840 is thus uncertain and the analysis presented here relies on subsequent periods." The founding story is told "plus a great deal of reconstruction and 'literary license'" (p. 27 footnote).
- **Other remembered disasters (OCR pp. 30-31, 144):** frosts are the remembered calamities: an "especially bad frost" about 1900 that sent everyone to relatives at lower altitude, another around 1940 with famine, and the 1971-72 frost. Hail fell 12 times in three years of his weather records.
- **What this means (inference):** the post-Tibito ash at site 400 lies in a basin that people say they occupied only from about 1805, give or take a generation, and Wohlt calls the pre-1840 part uncertain. If legend 059 (stones "like marbles", "only three survivors") is a local memory of that ash, the fall would date to after settlement, roughly 1805-1850. But the darkness story may equally have come with the settlers from Bipi, where their ancestors lived at the time of the 1660s Long Island ash. A 2.5 cm ash also fits a moderate north-coast eruption better than the 1808/09 one. Nothing here dates the layer. The deciding evidence would be Wohlt's own "Time of darkness" notebook answers, or a date or analysis of Blong's sample 536 from site 400.
