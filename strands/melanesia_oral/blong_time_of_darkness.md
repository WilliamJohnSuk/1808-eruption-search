## Chapter 1, Introduction (pp. 1-7; read 18 Oct 2026)
- Aim: document a cataclysmic eruption in PNG "some 300 years ago" (p. 3) through stratigraphy plus local legends. PNG historical record "far from complete for the period before about 1870 even for coastal areas"; interior highlands not entered by Europeans until the 1930s (p. 3).
- Origin: Kuk swamp archaeological site (Western Highlands, from 1970) showed thin volcanic ash layers; the uppermost, named Tibito Tephra, traced by geochemistry to Long Island in the Bismarck Sea (Chs 2-5). Isopach volumes put Long Island equal in magnitude to Krakatau 1883 (p. 4).
- Three founding legend versions (pp. 4-5): Melpa (Mount Hagen, told to Vicedom in the 1930s; Vicedom and Tischner 1948, vol. 3 p. 50: ash-storm, 4-5 nights indoors, starvation); Agarabi of Kainantu (Watson, heard 1954, published 1963 p. 152: three dark days, sand over everything, torches, a white pig killed); Huli of Tari, *bingi* (Glasse 1963 p. 271: thunder, darkness, three days of white pumiceous silt, ritual precautions, a recurring future event).
- Method (p. 6): literature plus a questionnaire circulated to anthropologists, missionaries, linguists and others gave about 56 versions; informant codes (e.g. 082, 023) used throughout; maps of duration, quality of darkness, particle size (Chs 9-10).
- Testing legend against reality three ways (p. 7): physical character of the fallen material vs Tibito Tephra (Ch. 12); effects vs worldwide tephra-fall impacts (Ch. 13); dating from genealogies vs radiocarbon (Ch. 14, "not entirely conclusive").
- Useful precedents cited (p. 2): Nicaraguan Indians fixed their ages to "La Oscuridad Grande", the ashfall of Cosegüina 1835 (Spinden 1919); Tambora 1815 tephra seen on Bangka >1500 km west; Tambora tidal oscillations 1-3 m in East Java 780 km away (Raffles 1830).

## Relevance to 1808/09 (inference)
- Shows a Krakatau-size eruption in a region without writing left a strong, widespread oral record ~300 years later, collectable by questionnaire to field workers. An 1808 eruption of similar or larger size in Melanesia or eastern Indonesia could have left similar legends, recorded 1880-1960.
- Method to copy: survey ethnographies and missionary records for darkness/ashfall legends, map them, then match to a dated tephra; genealogical dating is weak (Ch. 14 to read).

Blong prefers 17th c.; genealogical dates "seriously in error, many of them by 200 years or more".
- Ch 15 conclusions: Tibito >80,000 km2 >1.5 cm; >10 km3; ~10^25 ergs; one of greatest eruptions of last 1000 yr. 54 versions definitely Tibito. Legends accurate except passage of time ("genealogical amnesia"; earlier published versions put it in the 1880s).
- Open questions (p. 196): Krause (1965) reports a grey-green sand in sediment samples over a wide area of the Bismarck Sea; no offshore coring yet; Tibito may reach Telefomin and the Irian Jaya border as a thin layer in high swamps (Oksapmin and further west unsurveyed).
- **p. 199: Mary Mennis (1978), detailed work around Madang, "seems to indicate clearly that there were two times of darkness, the earlier one related to the disappearance of Yomba Island, presumed to be a Bismarck Sea volcano which vanished (total caldera collapse?) during the eruption."** Kuk: perhaps eight thin tephra falls in the last 6000 years, i.e. about one per thousand years. Olgaboli c. 1200 yr ago from an undetermined Bismarck Sea volcano; "Could Olgaboli Tephra have been erupted from Yomba?"
- Melpa: few people now know the legend though Vicedom recorded it in the 1930s; Chimbu: Bergmann and Brown had not heard it; Dom and Salt-yui near Gumine remember it. Some groups in the fall area have no memory (migration, fading, shallow inquiry).

There were continuous earthquakes and a big rain ... then a bush fire burnt the houses"; people "dead but their hearts were still beating"; "Bodies were buried, houses were buried, coconuts were buried by the ash"; animals and birds "killed by the fire"; "Ash fell on Tolokiwa ... Lake: no water after eruption; "A tidal wave only occurred once (?)"; **Motmot Island "wasn't there when the lapun was a boy" (inference from background knowledge, not in Blong: Motmot emerged in Lake Wisdom in 1953, so the old man was a boy before 1953)**; "After Koromi there were at least two generations before the lapun's father was born." Poin Kiau: "Rain, wind and earthquakes are coming"; "a high water came.
