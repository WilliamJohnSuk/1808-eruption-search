**Question.** What did Hipólito Unanue record in Lima, and do Andean or Southern Hemisphere sources show anything else in 1808-09?

**What was read.** Unanue, *Observaciones sobre el clima de Lima*, 2nd ed. (Madrid 1815), Sección I pp. 13-14 n. 3 (Wellcome Collection copy, OCR); seven Andean and Southern Hemisphere eruption catalogues; travel books of 1809-1830.

**Main results**
- **F02** [OCR]: an evening glow rising as a cone to the zenith towards Chorrillos, every night to about 20:00, from mid-December 1808 to mid-February 1809. Independent of Caldas, but coarsely dated. The project corrects the published reading: Chorrillos is the direction of the glow seen from Lima, the month comes only from the narrative order, and the same footnote records a separate reddish dust storm on 30 Apr 1808. Zodiacal light may explain part of it.
- We found no eruption, ash fall or volcanic haze for 1806-1810 in Peru, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina or southern Ecuador in the seven catalogues read (OCR).
- A Bushire weather journal of Nov-Dec 1808 (Morier 1812) was found in passing; it is not volcanic evidence.

**Open.** The *Minerva Peruana* for Dec 1808-Feb 1809, and the page image of Unanue's footnote.
