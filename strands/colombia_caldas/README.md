**Question.** What exactly did Francisco José de Caldas see in Bogotá in December 1808, and did anyone else in New Granada or Quito see it?

**What was read.** Caldas's "Noticia meteorológica" in the *Semanario del Nuevo Reyno de Granada* (año 2, no. 5, 5 Feb 1809) and his 1808 baseline issue (no. 7, 14 Feb 1808), both on page images; Roulin's 1831 Académie des sciences account; Restrepo's histories (1827, 1858); the Popayán and Quito correspondents of Caldas; Cotopaxi catalogues.

**Main results**
- **F01** [page image]: a whitish sky and silvery sun, mistaken for the moon, from 11 Dec 1808 to late January or early February 1809. This is the core dated witness (already published by Guevara-Murua et al. 2014).
- **F15** [page image]: Caldas's own 1808 definition of his "velo", which shows the coronaless December veil was abnormal by his standard.
- **F03** [OCR]: Pérez Valencia in Popayán in 1815 recalled a dimmed sun in December 1808 and January 1809 (not found in the literature reviewed).
- Roulin 1831 follows Caldas and is not independent.
- Restrepo's "sol opaco" note most likely belongs to Oct-Nov 1807 (the 1807 comet), not 1808. His "Cotopaxi 2 April 1808" is unconfirmed and may be a misdated 1803.
- We found no other dated sky observation from New Granada or Quito for Dec 1808-mid 1809 in the sources reachable online.

**Open.** Caldas's letters to Santiago Arroyo of 21 Jan, 6 Feb and 6 Mar 1809 (printed in *Cartas de Caldas*, 1917 and later editions) are the top unread source here. See the unavailable-sources list.
