**English (mine).** From 11 Dec 1808 to the end of January 1809 the sun's disc looked pale and lacked its dazzling rays, so that several times people at first glance took it for the moon. High in the sky it largely recovered its usual look. Near the horizon, morning and evening, it was sometimes tinged light rose, light green (also seen at Bologna) or a steel-like bluish grey. ### 1.3 Analysis of the Roulin text

**Who added what.** The statement is in the Revue's reporting voice, with no quotation marks. I cannot tell whether "jusqu'à la fin de janvier 1809" and "de mémoire d'homme" are Roulin's words or the reporter's. The *Procès-verbaux* (Gallica) were not reached.

**Source.** No source is cited in the snippet: no mention of Caldas, the Semanario or informants. Even so, it plainly paraphrases the Semanario Noticia, point for point and in the same order:
- silver sun mistaken for the moon;
- brighter at mid-sky;
- rose, light green and steel-grey near the horizon;
- cold and frost that burnt plants;
- uniform veil, day and night;
- no coronae;
- 4th–5th magnitude stars lost;
- dry; south winds and calms;
- the same five places.

**Spelling clue.** Roulin writes "Neyba", which is the Semanario's own 1809 spelling (see §2). This points to a printed Semanario copy rather than oral report.

**Pairing clue.** The place list is printed with hyphens: "Pasto-Popayan, Neyba-Tunja". This looks like a misreading of the comma list.

**Differences from Caldas:**
- **(a) End date.** "jusqu'à la fin de janvier 1809" is not in Caldas, who wrote in early Feb 1809 that the phenomenon was ongoing ("ha dos meses"). Possible sources:
  - an inference from the Semanario issue date (No. 5, 5 Feb 1809);
  - Roulin's own Bogotá informants (he was in Colombia 1822–28);
  - a later Semanario note. None was found in the 1809 volume (see §2.3), but the search was not complete.

  **Status: unverified; most economically explained as an inference from the publication date.**
- **(b) "De mémoire d'homme sur le plateau".** This is an embellishment or an informant's detail. Caldas says only that the cold was greater than altitude and latitude warrant, and that the frost burnt the sensitive plants.
- **(c) "Toujours du sud".** Caldas has "por intervalos" south winds. Roulin turns "por intervalos" into "par courts intervalles" and adds "toujours". This is a minor intensification.
- **(d) Star dimming simplified.** Caldas has 1st–3rd magnitudes dimmed and 4th–5th lost. Roulin keeps only the 4th–5th loss.
- **(e) "Du premier au douzième degré de latitude sud".** This error **is already in the 1831 French**, so it is not introduced by the English magazine. It is wrong: the five places lie about 1°N–11°N. It is probably a slip for latitude *nord*, with "douzième" a rounding of Santa Marta's ~11°15′N. It is not an independent extent statement.
- **(f) Arago's reply** is about the **1783** dry fog ("1784" in both texts). Its geography: "Napolie" in the French, rendered "Napoli di Romania" (Nauplia) in the English. "Napolie" may be an OCR or press garble. Arago's reply says nothing new about 1808.

**Context.** Roulin raised the 1808 case because of the August 1831 blue/pale sun, which was also seen at Saint-Sever and Bologna. In the same sitting the Académie heard of the new submarine volcano off Sicily (Ferdinandea, July 1831).

- **Text (OCR, joined):**

  > …usual condition of the atmosphere observed in New Grenada. From the 11th of December, 1808, to the end of January 1809, the disk of the sun at his rising appeared pale, and totally divested of its splendour, so that it was often mistaken for [the] moon. However, after gaining a slight elevation, it reassumed its ordinary aspect. Both in the morning and evening, it often appeared tinged with a slight [hue] of rose-colour or light green, and sometimes of a bluish grey, nearly resembling the hue of steel. The cold, during the whole of this time, was more sensibly felt than general; and frequently in the morning the plains in the neighbourhood of Bogota were covered with a hoar frost, which nipped the tender shoots of plants, a circumstance before unknown in this district within the memory of man. The sky was constantly clothed with a transparent haze, uniformly extended, and continuing during the day as well as the [night]. This haze produced none of those coloured halos, which are generally observed on such occasions surrounding the sun and moon. It concealed all stars below the fourth magnitude. The air was constantly free from moisture, and generally calm; and the winds which blew at short intervals, came always from the South. This phenomenon was observed at Pasto Popayan, Neyba Tunja, and Santa Martha; that is, from the first to the twelfth degree of south latitude. M. Arago remarked that the mist of 1784 was not less extensive, since it was observed at the same time at Napoli di Romania and in Africa, and that its duration was still longer. This mist was remarkable for the absence of moisture; and the observations of Sennebier show that the hygrometer, when exposed to its influence, advanced towards the point of dryness. Some persons considered it as the tail of a comet, while others attributed its appearance to the eruption of a volcano, which took place about that time.

- **Comparison: a close translation of the Revue encyclopédique report (or of a common source), with small drifts:**
  - "at his rising appeared pale" narrows the French "se montra pâle" to sunrise.
  - "after gaining a slight elevation" against the French "très-élevé dans son cours". The English weakens the elevation.
  - "concealed all stars below the fourth magnitude" against the French "4th and 5th".
  - "hoar frost … within the memory of man" against "gelée … de mémoire d'homme sur le plateau".
  - The latitude error and "1784" are inherited.
- **Comet/volcano hypotheses:** these refer to the **1783** fog (in Arago's reply), **not** to 1808. Readers of Guevara-Murua et al. App. C should not take "eruption of a volcano which took place about that time" as an 1808–09 attribution.
- **Independence:** none beyond what Roulin added. It is derived from Caldas via Roulin.

## Bottom line
1. **Roulin's 1831 report.**
   - It was given at the **29 Aug 1831** séance.
   - It is a close French paraphrase of Caldas's Semanario Noticia, including the Semanario spelling "Neyba".
   - It cites no informant.
   - It adds three things: "jusqu'à la fin de janvier 1809", "de mémoire d'homme sur le plateau" and "toujours du sud". All three are unsourced and probably editorial or inferred.
   - The "12° south latitude" error is already in the French.
   - The comet/volcano remarks are Arago's, about **1783**.
   - It has no independent evidential weight for the end date.
2. **The 1809 Semanario original.** The JCB scan contains the Noticia at leaves 63–65. It confirms "once de Diciembre" and the place list, and it supplies original readings ("yelo" read as "velo", "este planeta", "considerables"). The rain article (June 1809) gives Aug–Dec 1808 rainfall only, with no sky remarks.
3. **Golovnin.** No primary text was reached. The 1819 scan OCR is unusable, and az.lib.ru is blocked.
