## 1. Result in brief

- The volume prints six letters from Caldas to Arroyo in the window: 6 Nov 1808 and 21 Jan, 6 Feb, 6 Mar, 6 Jun and 21 Aug 1809 (nos. 106-111, pp. 265-268). Posada says letters 102-111 were first printed in *Repertorio Colombiano* (p. 261 note) [OCR], so the *Repertorio* lead for these letters is answered by this text.
- **We found no report of the veil, the silver sun or any sky phenomenon in these letters.** We also found no request from Caldas to Arroyo, Arboleda or Pérez Valencia for observations of it. Search terms: sol, nube, velo, fenómeno, niebla, plateado, eclipse, Pasto, Popayán, Neiva, Tunja, Santa Marta, Pérez Valencia [OCR, whole volume].
- The letters are about Caldas's post after Mutis's death (Sept 1808), money, the *Semanario*'s subscriptions, and the bishopric boundaries of Popayán.
- What Arroyo did send from Popayán in these weeks was data: a table of births and marriages (received by 21 Jan 1809) and a note with rainfall amounts and locust news (received by 6 Feb 1809, the day after the Noticia was printed) [page image for 6 Feb]. The letter does not mention the sun. Whether that note carried the Popayán sighting is not known [inference].
- **p. 259 is dated 6 Feb 1808, not 1809.** It is the second page of letter no. 100, headed "Santafé y febrero 6 de 1808" on p. 258 [OCR; index p. 509 gives "6 febrero 1808 ... 258" [OCR]]. Page 259 itself says "Mi cabeza padeció con el cometa y ya estoy restableciéndome" [page image], which fits the Great Comet of 1807 (visible into early 1808) [inference]. So Pacheco's date was correct; the separate 6 Feb 1809 letter (no. 108, p. 266) also exists.
- **Second, partial answer from the same *Semanario* issue.** No. 5 of 5 Feb 1809, which carries the Noticia, also prints the "Lista de los Señores Subscriptores ... en el año de 1808" by town [page image, banrep 1606, leaves 9-12]. It shows who in Caldas's five towns was on his mailing list (section 3). This does not show who reported the sun, but it shows which of the five towns had readers he could hear from by post.

## 2. The letters (Spanish, OCR or page image, with English gloss)

**No. 106, Santafé, 6 Nov 1808, to Arroyo in Popayán (p. 265)** [OCR]
> "Yo me alegro de haber publicado ese sistema, porque me ha proporcionado las noticias que usted me comunica. Yo sabré aprovecharme de ellas y publicarlas con su nombre en el año entrante. ... No admito la excusa que usted alega para no escribir. Espero buenos papeles, buenas miras y buenos proyectos."

Gloss: Arroyo's letter on the Cauca waters is welcome; Caldas will publish his news under his name next year, and expects more papers from him. News of Caldas himself will come by word of mouth through Antonio Arboleda. Before the event; it shows the channel (Arroyo and Arboleda in Popayán, writing by each post).

**No. 107, Santafé, 21 Jan 1809 (pp. 265-266)** [OCR]
> "Mi Santiago: recibí la de usted acompañada del estado de nacidos y casados para un quinquenio, que lo publicaré cuanto antes. Temo mucho que El Semanario pare por falta de suscripciones, pues no llegan todavía a cincuenta."

Gloss: received your letter with the five-year table of births and marriages, which I will publish soon. I fear the *Semanario* will stop for lack of subscriptions; there are not yet fifty. The rest is about the Expedition's future and his health. Six weeks into the veil; no sky content.

**No. 108, Santafé, 6 Feb 1809 (p. 266)** [page image]
> "Mi querido Santiago: recibí su esquelita con la cantidad de lluvia y noticias de langosta, que estimo infinito y que publicaré cuanto antes."

Gloss: I received your short note with the amount of rain and news of locusts, which I value greatly and will publish soon. The rest: the Viceroy closed the Expedition's house five months ago; Caldas lost lodging, table, servants, light and laundry; he has lived on loans from friends; congratulations on Arroyo's post as alcalde. One day after the Noticia appeared; no sky content.

**No. 109, Santafé, 6 Mar 1809 (p. 267)** [OCR]
> "A mí me deja Jefe independiente en el Observatorio, y me asocia a la continuación de la Flora de Bogotá, con mil pesos. ... ¡Con qué lentitud y con qué miseria se pagan los conocimientos!"

Gloss: the Viceroy's new plan makes him independent head of the Observatory and joins him to the *Flora*, at 1,000 pesos, plus the mathematics chair. This is the "treinta y nueve años" letter quoted in *Soy Caldas* (round 2 note), now dated: 6 Mar 1809. It also mentions the recent death of his father. No sky content, and so no end date for the veil.

**No. 110, Santafé, 6 Jun 1809 (p. 267)** [OCR]
Asks Arroyo, now in the Consulado, and Antonio Arboleda to obtain good reports from the Popayán cabildos for the confirmation of his posts at court. "Con don Ignacio Torres irá su barómetro" (your barometer will go with don Ignacio Torres). No sky content.

**No. 111, Santafé, 21 Aug 1809, "Al señor doctor don Santiago Pérez de Arroyo y Valencia" (p. 268)** [OCR]
On the division of the bishoprics of Popayán and Antioquia: Pasto should go to Popayán, but only as far as the Guáitara; the valley of Neiva is cut off from Popayán by the Guanacas range. "A Arboleda le comunico algunas noticias de Humboldt y de Cabal." No sky content. The address form confirms that Arroyo is the "Santiago Pérez Valencia" of the subscriber list below [inference].

**Other material in the volume in the window** [OCR]: the index (pp. 509-511) lists no other Caldas letter between 6 Nov 1808 and 6 Feb 1810. Letters to Caldas: only Fernández Madrid, 10 Nov 1809. Letters about Caldas: Amar to the commander at Cartagena, 18 Jul 1809. Neither mentions the sky. Caldas's later statement against Eloy Valenzuela (pp. 367-369) [page image p. 367] says that at the end of 1808 he sent "por la primera vez una carta circular impresa" to men of letters across the kingdom, asking them to write for the *Semanario* and subscribe; Valenzuela answered from Bucaramanga on 5 Nov 1808 and 15 Jan 1809. This shows a printed call for contributions went out just before the event [page image], but the text of the circular is not in Posada.

## 4. Answer to William's question

We found no report of the silver sun in Caldas's letters to Arroyo of Nov 1808 to Aug 1809, and no request for observations of it, in Posada 1917. These letters do not tell us how Caldas learned of the sightings. The best evidence on the channel is indirect: Arroyo was sending Caldas Popayán data (vital statistics, rain, locusts) by each post in Jan-Feb 1809, and the 1808 subscriber list shows readers in Popayán, Pasto and Tunja, but none named in Neiva or Santa Marta [page image; inference].
