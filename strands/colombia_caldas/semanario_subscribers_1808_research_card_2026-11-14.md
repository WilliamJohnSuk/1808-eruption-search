Gloss of the Nota's last part: the publishers warn that unless subscriptions rise to cover printing costs the paper will be suspended. Caldas's letter to Arroyo of 21 Jan 1809 says the same ("no llegan todavía a cincuenta") [OCR, Posada p. 265].

**How subscriptions worked.** The notice that closes year 1 ("Santafé 8 de Agosto de 1808") says that subscriptions for 1809 were taken "en todas las Administraciones de Correos": 6 pesos in the capital, 8 elsewhere in the viceroyalty, 10 outside it. All subscribers received the numbers postage-free at the post office where they subscribed [OCR, JCB copy on archive.org, year 2 front matter]. The *Semanario* was printed "en la casa de la Imprenta Real, calle de San Felipe" [OCR]; Caldas names the "empresario" (business manager) as Bruno Espinosa de los Monteros (Posada p. 367) [page image] and the Santafé postmaster as Diego Tanco (Posada p. 368) [OCR].

**Price and accounts.** For 1809: 6 pesos a year in Santafé, 8 elsewhere in the viceroyalty, 10 outside [OCR]; Valenzuela calls it "ocho pesos anuales" (Posada p. 367) [page image]. Price for 1808 not checked (1807 prospectus). For scale, Caldas's salary in Feb 1809 was 400 pesos a year (Posada p. 266) [page image], so a Santafé subscription was about 1.5% of it [computed]. Caldas's account of 1809 (Posada pp. 367-368) [page image; OCR]: subscriptions came to 1,034 pesos; 1,000 were spent on "papel, tinta, oficial de caja, tirador y portes de correo" (paper, ink, typesetter, pressman, postage); 34 pesos profit, split with the business manager Espinosa de los Monteros; Caldas gave his 17 to Espinosa. As 45 subscribers in Feb 1809 cannot make 1,034 pesos, subscriptions must have recovered later in 1809 [inference].

**Why it matters for the 1809 search [inference].** The list maps the postal readership through which Caldas could hear of the sun. Popayán (12), Pasto (1) and Tunja (2) appear; Neiva and Santa Marta do not appear as headings. The southern places that do appear, from Quito to Riobamba and Cuenca, and the three post-office heads, show the list reached far down the Quito road. Note that this is the **1808** list: by Feb 1809 only about 45 subscribed, so the network that reported the veil was mostly not the paying readership of 1809.

### 4.1 Subscribers in the five towns of the Noticia

**Popayán (12): D. Torivio Rodríguez; D. Gerónimo Tovar; D. José Pérez Valencia; D. Manuel María Arboleda; D. José de Caldas; D. Antonio Mendizaval; D. Santiago Pérez Valencia; D. Antonio Arboleda; D. Antonio Texada; D. Andrés Pérez Valencia; D. Pedro Borrero; D. Domingo Pérez Valencia.**

- **Santiago Pérez (de Arroyo y) Valencia.** Caldas's closest Popayán friend and correspondent, addressed in 1809 as "doctor don Santiago Pérez de Arroyo y Valencia" (Posada p. 268) [OCR]. A lawyer of Popayán's leading families. In 1809 he held a post in the Consulado and was alcalde (letters of 21 Jan and 6 Feb 1809) [OCR; page image]. He sent Caldas Popayán rainfall, locust news and five years of births and marriages for the *Semanario* [page image]. Later active in the independence-era politics of Popayán [background]. Most likely channel for the Popayán sighting [inference].
- **Antonio Arboleda.** Caldas's "íntimo y fiel amigo" in Popayán, who stood proxy at Caldas's marriage in 1810 (Posada p. 269) [OCR]. With Arroyo he was one of Caldas's Popayán rain observers (pass 3 note, Semanario p. 233) [OCR via earlier note]. From the Arboleda family of Popayán, large landowners and mine owners [background].
- **Manuel María Arboleda.** Of the same Popayán family; possibly Antonio's relative [background, unchecked].
- **José de Caldas.** Francisco José's brother in Popayán [background]. Posada prints no letter to him in the window.
- **José, Andrés and Domingo Pérez Valencia.** Probably relatives of Santiago Arroyo (the Pérez de Arroyo y Valencia family) [inference from the shared surname].
- **Torivio Rodríguez, Gerónimo Tovar, Antonio Mendizaval, Antonio Texada, Pedro Borrero.** Not identified [no source checked].

**Pasto (1): D. Gabriel Santa-Cruz.** Not identified. A web search on 14 Nov ("Gabriel Santacruz" Pasto 1809) returned only general studies of Pasto in the independence wars; none was opened. He is the only named reader in Pasto, so he is the first candidate for the Pasto report [inference].

**Tunja (2): D. José Jovér; D. Francisco Torres y Roxas.** Not identified; a web search for "José Jover" Tunja found nothing specific. Joaquín Camacho, earlier suggested as the Tunja informant (`caldas.md` s.2), is not on the 1808 list [page image].

**Neiva (no heading).** The Neiva valley has: **La Plata**, D. Andrés Ordóñez; **Garzón**, D. Bernardo Méndez, D. José Méndez; **Gigante**, D. Agustín Santos Mendivil; **Villavieja**, D. José Joaquín Cardoso [page image]. None identified.

**Santa Marta (no heading).** Nearest listed places: Cartagena (25) and the river towns of Mompox (10), Honda (7) and Nare (3) [page image].

### 4.2 Other subscribers Claude can identify (all [background] unless marked)

- **El Exmo. Sr. Virey del Reyno (7 copies).** Antonio José Amar y Borbón (1742-1826), viceroy of New Granada 1803-1810. He decided the future of the Botanical Expedition after Mutis's death and gave Caldas the Observatory post (Caldas to Arroyo, 6 Mar 1809 [OCR]). Deposed by the Santafé junta of 20 July 1810.
- **José Celestino Mutis (1732-1808).** Spanish physician, priest and naturalist, director of the Royal Botanical Expedition of New Granada from 1783. He died in Santafé on 11 Sept 1808, so he subscribed before his death. Caldas's patron (Posada prologue [OCR]).
- **Francisco José de Caldas (1768-1816).** Born in Popayán; self-taught astronomer and geographer; editor of the *Semanario*; head of the Observatory from 1805. Author of the 5 Feb 1809 Noticia (F01). Later an engineer for the independent governments; executed by Spanish forces in Santafé in Oct 1816.
- **Camilo Torres (1766-1816).** Lawyer from Popayán living in Santafé; author of the 1809 "Memorial de agravios" protesting American representation in the Junta Central; later president of the United Provinces of New Granada; executed 1816. His letters to Arroyo of 1809 (on the Junta Central election) survive (round 2 note).
- **Miguel Pombo (1779-1816).** Lawyer from Popayán in Santafé, a friend of Caldas from student days (Pombo is named among Caldas's few close friends, Posada prologue [OCR]); later a federalist writer; executed 1816.
- **Frutos Joaquín Gutiérrez (1770-1816).** Lawyer from Cúcuta, Santafé; leader in the 1810 junta; executed 1816.
- **José María Salazar.** Probably the lawyer and writer José María Salazar (1785-1828), author of a "Memoria descriptiva del país de Santafé de Bogotá" printed in the *Semanario* (1809), later a diplomat for Colombia. Identification probable.
- **Manuel del Socorro Rodríguez (1754-1819).** Cuban-born librarian of the Royal Library in Santafé and editor of the first Santafé periodical, *Papel Periódico* (1791-97), and of *El Redactor Americano* (1806-09). Caldas criticised the *Redactor* (Posada pp. 365-366 print Valenzuela's complaints about both papers [OCR]).
- **Diego Martín Tanco.** Administrator of the Santafé post office ("Administrador de Correos, don Diego Tanco", Posada p. 368 [OCR]). He is a key figure for our question: post offices took the subscriptions and carried the reports [inference]. A Tanco-Caldas correspondence is noted in BHA vol. 10 (round 2 note).
- **Francisco Ulloa.** Probably Francisco Antonio Ulloa (c. 1783-1816), Popayán-born lawyer and *Semanario* contributor who lent Arroyo a geography book (Caldas to Arroyo, 6 Jan 1808: "la Geografía que ha cedido a usted Ulloa" [OCR]); executed 1816. Identification probable.
- **Fr. Diego Padilla.** Probably the Augustinian Diego Francisco Padilla (1754-1829), preacher and editor of the patriot paper *Aviso al Público* (1810). Identification probable.
- **Nicolás Mauricio de Omaña.** Priest in Santafé; member of the 1810 junta [background, probable].
- **Pedro de la Lastra, Manuel Pardo, Pablo Plata, José María Lozano.** Names that also appear among the Santafé notables of 1810 [background; not checked]. José María Lozano was possibly of the Lozano de Peralta family (Marqués de San Jorge).
- **Crisanto Valenzuela.** Relative of Eloy Valenzuela (he carried Eloy's donation, Posada p. 369 [OCR]); later a member of the Santafé junta [background].
- **Juan Manuel Sordo.** Santafé resident who witnessed Caldas's payment to Valenzuela's agent in April 1810 (Posada p. 368 [OCR]).
- **José Manuel Restrepo (1781-1863), Medellín.** Lawyer and geographer; drew the map of Antioquia sent to Caldas (Caldas to Restrepo, 19 Feb 1808 [OCR]) and wrote on Antioquia for the *Semanario*; later Colombia's interior minister and author of the *Historia de la revolución de la República de Colombia*.
- **Juan del Corral (1778-1814), Antioquia.** Merchant born in Mompox; later dictator of the independent state of Antioquia (1813) who decreed a law of free birth for enslaved children (1814).
- **José Ignacio Pombo (3 copies), Cartagena.** Merchant and leading member of the Real Consulado of Cartagena; patron of the sciences; financed Caldas's work and wrote economic reports; brother-in-law or relative of the Popayán Pombos [relationship unchecked].
- **El Real Consulado (6 copies), Cartagena.** The merchant guild and commercial court of Cartagena, founded 1795. Its six copies suggest institutional support [inference].
- **Manuel Torizes, Cartagena.** Probably Manuel Rodríguez Torices (1788-1816), lawyer, later president of the independent State of Cartagena; executed 1816. One of Caldas's named observers (pass 3 note) [OCR via earlier note]. Identification probable.
- **José María del Real, Cartagena.** Lawyer; later Cartagena's deputy and a leader of the independent state [background, probable].
- **Pantaleón Ribón, Mompox.** Probably Pantaleón Germán Ribón, leader of Mompox's declaration of independence of 6 Aug 1810. **Marqués de Torre Hoyos, Mompox:** holder of a Mompox noble title [background].
- **Colegio de S. Pedro, Mompox.** The Colegio-Universidad de San Pedro Apóstol, founded on a 1790s bequest [background, unchecked].
- **Excmo. Sr. D. Juan María Villavicencio, Havana.** Probably the Spanish naval officer Juan María Villavicencio y de la Serna, then commanding at Havana; later Regent of Spain (1810) [background, possibly].
- **Joaquín Caycedo (y Cuero), Cali.** Probably Joaquín de Caicedo y Cuero (1773-1813), leader of Cali's 1810 junta and president of the Ciudades Confederadas del Valle; executed in Pasto 1813. **José María Mallarino, Cali:** father of the later president Manuel María Mallarino [background, probable]. **José Cuero y Caycedo** is of the same family.
- **Mariano del Campo Valencia, Quilichao.** Probably Mariano del Campo Larraondo y Valencia (1772-1860), priest at Alegría near Caloto, one of Caldas's named observers (pass 3 note) and the grower of breadfruit near Caloto noted in *Semanario* year 2 no. 1 [OCR]. Identification probable.
- **Miguel Cabal, Buga.** Subject of Caldas's last *Memoria* (no. 11), "Elogio histórico de don Miguel Cabal" [text via WebFetch]; Caldas writes in 1809 that he passes news "de Humboldt y de Cabal" to Arboleda [OCR]. Probably the same man, a Buga naturalist; identity of the 1808 subscriber with him is probable [inference].
- **Joaquín Fernández de Soto, Buga.** Leader in Buga in 1810-11 [background, possibly].
- **Sr. Marqués de Selva-Alegre, Quito.** Juan Pío Montúfar (1758-1819), president of the Quito junta formed on 10 Aug 1809. **Manuel Rodríguez de Quiroga, Quito:** lawyer and minister of that junta, killed in the Quito prison massacre of 2 Aug 1810. **Xavier Montúfar, Riobamba:** probably a member of the Montúfar family [background].
- **Pedro Antonio Fernández de Córdova, Cuenca.** Not identified with confidence [background].
- **Eloy Valenzuela (1756-1834), Bucaramanga.** Priest of Bucaramanga, botanist and first sub-director of Mutis's Expedition; wrote for the *Semanario* and sent 50 pesos to support it (letters of 5 Nov 1808 and 15 Jan 1809; Posada pp. 367-369) [page image p. 367; OCR]. Later quarrelled with Caldas over the money. Killed in 1834.
- **Juan Nepomuceno Azuero, Manare.** Possibly Juan Nepomuceno Azuero Plata (1780-1857), priest from Socorro, later a liberal politician and member of Colombia's constituent congresses [background, possibly].
- **José Mutis Consuegra, Pie de la Cuesta.** Probably a relative of the Mutis family (José Celestino's nephew Sinforoso was Mutis Consuegra) [background, possibly].
