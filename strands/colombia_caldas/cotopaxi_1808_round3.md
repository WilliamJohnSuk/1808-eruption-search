## 4. Further checks (v2)

### 4.1 Restrepo also has a Quito earthquake in 1808
- **1827, t. 2, pp. 77–78** (inside.php q=terremoto, OCR): the earthquake list runs "El 25 de abril de 1755 hubo uno terrible que continuó hasta el 28" … "En 1756 por otro terremoto cayó la iglesia de Latacunga" … "Hemos ya hablado del terremoto de 1797, otro se repitió en Latacunga en 1800" … "**Ultimamente en 1808, se recuerda en Quito otro largo terremoto**" (OCR ",808"). It continues on p. 78 with Honda, 16 Jun 1805: "el mas fuerte fué el que arruinó la villa de Honda el diez y seis de junio de 1805".
- **1858, p. 618:** "Últimamente, en 1808 hubo en Quito un fuerte terremoto."
- **Check:** the Instituto Geofísico (EPN) "Catálogo de Terremotos del Ecuador – Intensidades", reproduced in a PUCE thesis, pp. 114–126, jumps from 4 Feb 1797 to 20 Jan 1834 with **no 1808 entry**. Beauval et al. 2013 (BSSA) has no event between 1786 and 1834. If the 1808 Quito shock happened, it was below damaging intensity, or it is unrecorded. Either way, Restrepo pairs a remembered ("se recuerda") 1808 Cotopaxi eruption with a remembered 1808 Quito earthquake. Both look like Quito oral or informant tradition and are not documented events.

### 4.2 Was anyone on the spot in April 1808?
- **Stevenson**, *Narrative* vol. 2 (IA b29350347_0002, inside.php q=1808): "in June, 1808, we embarked at Callao for Guayaquil" (p. 215). He then went up to Quito with President Ruiz de Castilla, and he was "ordered by the President, in December, 1808, to visit the river Napo" (p. 367). So **he reached Quito c. Jul–Aug 1808, about four months after 2 Apr 1808.** His statement that Cotopaxi "during the greater part of the three years that I was a resident … was continually ejecting either smoke or flames … particularly visible in the morning and evening" (p. 342) is **consistent with a volcano in a mild post-eruptive or open-vent phase in 1808–10**. His history of Cotopaxi (pp. 348–351) lists 1698, 1738, 1743, 1768, 1774 and Jan 1803, and **no 1808**. "Cotopaxi" gives 12 hits and none describes a recent eruption.
- **Caldas, *Semanario* 1808** (IA semanariodelnuev01unse; OCR partial): "Cotopaxi" 0 hits. "volcan" gives pp. 27, 271–272 and 430, all geography with no eruption. "Latacunga" gives p. 26, an altitude table. No eruption news was found, but the OCR is partial, so this is a weak negative.
- **Humboldt**, Cotopaxi text (Vues des Cordillères, 1810; English extract reprinted 1819, humboldt.unibe.ch): 1738, 1744, "4th of April, 1768", and "The explosion which took place in the month of January, 1803, was preceded by a most dreadful phenomenon, the sudden melting of the snows that covered the mountain … in a single night the subterraneous fire became so active" that at sunrise the cone appeared bare. Nothing after 1803.

### 4.3 Is "1808" a slip for 1803?
Evidence that **Restrepo's "1808" episode may be the January 1803 eruption, mis-dated**:
1. Restrepo **omits 1803 entirely**, although the Jan 1803 eruption was the one best known to his contemporaries. Caldas wrote about it on 21 Jan 1803: "Cotopaxi nos asusta: ha bramado sin interrupción…" (see caldas_correspondents_pass3.md), and Humboldt publicised it.
2. Restrepo's diagnostic detail is "habiendo quedado el cerro sin nieve alguna" and torrents of meltwater. This is exactly the hallmark of Humboldt's 1803 account: the snow cover melted "in a single night" and the cone was left bare.
3. A handwritten "3" and "8" are easily confused, and this would happen at the manuscript or compositor stage, not in the OCR. Both the 1827 Paris and 1858 Besanzón typesettings read 1808, so the error, if any, predates 1827.
4. Restrepo's day-dates are loose elsewhere. He gives "5 de abril" for 1768, where Humboldt and Stevenson give 4 April. An "abril" could have drifted from the 1768 entry.

Against this:
- The day and month (2 April vs 4 January) do not match.
- Restrepo gives a specific local geography: more ash on the Vallevicioso side than the Latacunga side, and testimony from Mulaló residents. That suggests a separate informant report.
- Stevenson saw continuous activity from mid-1808, which shows the volcano was restless that year.
- Wolf (1878, p. 24) states that documents for 1803–1853 are "casi completamente" lacking. Every modern list (GVP, IG-EPN, Pistolesi, Hall & Mothes) descends from Wolf, so its silence on 1808 carries little weight.

## 5. Verdict
- **The status of 2 Apr 1808 is unresolved, and it is plausible as a minor event.** It rests on **one secondary source**, Restrepo 1827, which the 1858 edition repeats without new information. No contemporary document has been found. Restrepo was not in Quito, cites no source, and phrases the parallel earthquake entry as tradition ("se recuerda"). Confusion with the Jan 1803 eruption is a credible alternative, and I rate it about as likely as a genuine separate event. Confusion with 4 Apr 1768 is unlikely because Restrepo lists 1768 separately.
- **Size, if real:** small. The effects were local: ash, "tierra" and hot water ("agua ardiente/caliente", i.e. probably muddy or phreatic ejecta or meltwater lahars) fell around the volcano, mainly toward Vallevicioso. The snow cap melted and meltwater floods were heard by Mulaló residents. Unlike his 1768 entry, Restrepo reports **no darkness or ashfall in Quito**, so the event was probably VEI ≤2. It may belong to the same open-vent episode that Stevenson saw in 1808–10.
- **Relevance to the 1808/09 sulfate event:** none as a source. The date is about eight months before Caldas's veil (from 11 Dec 1808) and roughly 10 months before the ice-core central estimate. An event of this size cannot produce a bipolar stratospheric sulfate signal. At most, a degassing Cotopaxi in 1808–10 could add to the regional tropospheric haze background in the northern Andes, which matters only as a confounder for local sky observations near Quito. It is unlikely to have mattered for Bogotá, about 1,000 km away. The GVP has no 1808 Cotopaxi entry. A note to the GVP or the lead could flag "Restrepo 1827: Cotopaxi 2 Apr 1808, local ashfall (unconfirmed)" as a possible uncertain event.
- **Madrid 1886 snippet:** irrelevant. It concerns the Pinerolo (Piedmont) earthquake swarm of 2 Apr – 17 May 1808.
