## 0. Bottom line
1. [Update 16 Oct: dating examined in restrepo_date_round3.md. The observation most likely belongs to Oct–Nov 1807 (about 75–90%, with the 1807 comet), though Restrepo's own text says 1808; treat as unresolved but probably 1807.] **New and important: Restrepo's "sol opaco" note, with two conflicting dates.** J. M. Restrepo, *Historia de la revolución de la República de Colombia*, reports that the sun was seen "opaco y visible el disco á la simple vista" morning and evening near the horizon, every day, in Santafé, Antioquia and Quito, in **October–November**. A comet seen from 25 September in Libra accompanies it.
   - **1827 1st ed.:** the note is a footnote in the section headed "Año de 1807". The comet is the Great Comet C/1807 R1, which was near Spica on 9 Sep 1807 and moving away from the Sun, so it would be in Libra by late September. This places the dim sun in **Oct–Nov 1807**.
   - **1858 2nd ed.:** the same note, now in Restrepo's biographical note on Caldas, reads "**En los meses de octubre y noviembre de 1808 observó Cáldas, lo mismo que los demas ciudadanos, que el sol se vió opaco…**". It adds "y por algunos meses, sin que se pudiese dar una explicación satis[factoria]" and keeps the 25 September comet.
   - The comet anchors the 1827 reading (1807); I know of no naked-eye comet in Libra in Sep 1808, though I have not checked this in a comet catalogue. The 1858 relabel to 1808 and the attribution to Caldas look like a conflation with Caldas's Dec 1808 – Jan 1809 veil.
   - **Either reading matters.** (a) If 1807 is right, it is independent support for Portilla's hypothesis (Rev. Acad. Colomb. Cienc., c. 2017, "Caldas y el gran cometa de 1807") of a hazy atmosphere about 15 months before Dec 1808. Portilla based that on Caldas's poor view of the comet's tail and does not cite Restrepo. (b) If 1808 is right, the dimming would start in Oct–Nov 1808, before Caldas's 11 Dec onset, which would bear directly on the eruption date.
   - Restrepo's 1858 wording "por algunos meses" is consistent with Caldas's two-month veil.
2. **No other writer was found who compares the 1815–17 skies with 1808/1809.** The Pérez Valencia remark still stands alone. Searches covered Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian and English phrase queries, Höhenrauch / brouillard sec / nebbia secca with year filters, Chladni's 1818 catalogue of sun-darkenings, and Humboldt's *Cosmos* list. None of them mention 1808–09.
3. **Bogotá negative for 1815.** J. M. Caballero's Bogotá diary (Posada ed. 1902) has no sun remark in Apr–Jun 1815. That page range shows no 1815 reaction to Tambora in Bogotá.
4. **Control case.** A verse chronicle in the same volume records a blood-red sun and moon at sunset over Honda on 29–30 January, probably 1815 (pre-Tambora, dry season). It shows that red low suns in the Magdalena valley in January can occur without any known eruption.
5. **Dependent items (Roulin/Caldas reprints):** the Württemberg *Correspondenzblatt* (IA bub_gb_po81AAAAMAAJ, 1831 volume) prints a German version of Roulin's 1831 report ("vom 11. December 1808 bis 31. Januar 1809 zu Neugranada"). It is dependent and adds nothing.
