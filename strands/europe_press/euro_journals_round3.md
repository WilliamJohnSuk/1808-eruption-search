- The only 1809 "Heerrauch" found is **Berlin, ~11 July 1809** (F1). It is a single day in midsummer, after a thunderstorm. That is typical of the ordinary North-German summer Höhenrauch (Haarrauch, i.e. peat-burning/moor smoke). It does not give evidence for a stratospheric veil.
- The Etna eruption of **24–27 March 1809** produced a local "densa caligine" over eastern Sicily and ashfall up to ~6 Zoll [inches] (F2, F3). Any Italian sky turbidity in late March to April 1809 has to be attributed to Etna first.

They do not rule out a subtle one: the Paris "état du ciel" entries use routine wording ("Vapeurs à l'horizon", "Ciel chargé de vapeurs") that could hide mild turbidity. ### F1 — Berlin: "Die Witterung des Jahres 1809" (Heerrauch, July 1809)

- Excerpts (OCR; ſ normalised):
  - Jan 1809, p. 168: "Hätte dieser Monat nicht so viele trübe, sondern mehr helle und heitere Tage gehabt, so würde die Kälte noch ungleich stärker gewesen seyn." Then Feb ("gelinde Witterung bis zum 7ten … Vom 18ten bis 21ten weheten heftige Stürme") and Mar ("blieb noch immer kalt und unangenehm … Hagel und Sturm"). No haze remark.
  - May, p. 169: "Selbst bis zum 7ten May blieb es kalt und unfreundlich … vom 8ten bis 28sten war anhaltend schöne warme Witterung, nur sehr trocken."
  - p. 169: "Am 26ten sahe man Ringe um die Sonne und den Mond und am 11t. und 23ten Feuerkugeln." (month probably June; not certain from the snippet)
  - July, p. 169: "Bis zum 5ten Julius waren angenehme Tage mit fruchtbaren Regen; am 3ten ein starkes Gewitter …"; fts snippet: "… Gewitter am 11ten war Heerrauch. Vom 18ten bis 23ten gab es kühle Tage mit Regen".
  - p. 173: the same review lists 1809 volcanic activity: Vesuvius (Feb, Apr, 13–14 Aug, 14 Sep, with ash to Naples) and Etna, "am 24ten März stieß er eine ungeheure Menge schwarzer Asche aus, die eine dicke Wolke bildete, 12 bis 15 Meilen weit flog, und an einigen Orten 6 Zoll hoch lag", plus a second outbreak on the 25th. No unknown or distant eruption is mentioned. The volume index also has "Erdbeben im Jahre 1809", "Feuerkugeln im Jahr 1809", "Hitze im Jahre 1809" and "Vulcane, Verhalten derselben im Jahre 1809".
- Event date: ~11 July 1809 (one day). Writing date: early 1810. Publication date: 1810.
- Independence: independent of Caldas and Unanue.
- Alternative explanations: ordinary summer Haarrauch (moor/peat burning in NW Germany; dry, warm spell); thunderstorm-related haze.
- What it constrains: it is a negative for a conspicuous Berlin veil Jan–Jun 1809. The observer recorded weather in detail and noted the Heerrauch in July, yet did not note one earlier. Halos (if June) are not diagnostic.
- Verification level: OCR via WebFetch only.

### F3 — Etna eyewitness: Mario Gemmellaro, *Memoria dell'eruzione dell'Etna avvenuta nell'anno 1809* (Catania, Tipografia del Senato; IA date 1820, probably printed 1809)
- IA `bub_gb_EDNoKIlarh0C`: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_EDNoKIlarh0C . Summarised in *Transactions of the Geological Society of London* vol. 1 (1811), IA `transactionsgeo04londgoog` ("Memoria dell' ultima Eruzione dell' Etna, accaduta il dì 27 di Marzo 1809").
- p. 2 (OCR): "Nel giorno dunque 27 ora scorso Marzo 1809, continuando il Cielo coperto di densa caligine come nel giorno innanzi, e proseguendo impetuoso l'ovest-sud si fe sentire al far dell'alba un tremuoto assai più forte degli antecedenti, e si vide una quantità immensa di arene che alzandosi a nuvoloni dal piede nord-est dell'ultimo Bicorne dell'Etna per una novella bocca ivi aper[ta] …"
- Translation: "On the said day, 27 March 1809, the sky continuing covered with dense haze as on the day before, and the west-south[west] wind still blowing violently, at dawn an earthquake much stronger than the previous ones was felt, and an immense quantity of sand was seen rising in great clouds from the NE foot of the last twin peak of Etna through a new vent opened there …"
- What it constrains: a local volcanic haze over Catania on 26–27 March 1809. Any Sicilian or southern Italian reports of a dim sun in late March to April 1809 should be assigned to Etna, not to the 1808/09 unknown eruption. The WSW wind would carry the plume ENE, over the Ionian Sea and towards Calabria and Greece.
